# கலித்தொகை · பரிபாடல் · பதிற்றுப்பத்து — எட்டில் வேறுபட்ட மூன்று

**The three odd ones out of the Eight Anthologies**

---

## 0. ஏன் இம்மூன்றும் ஒன்றாக | Why these three belong in one document

`../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` reads the corpus through அகம்/புறம் and the ஐந்திணை — the machinery
of குறுந்தொகை, நற்றிணை, அகநானூறு, ஐங்குறுநூறு, புறநானூறு. Those five are, formally, one
kind of book: short poems in **ஆசிரியப்பா (அகவல்)**, arranged by திணை or by length, spoken by
unnamed conventional voices.

The remaining three are not that. Each breaks the pattern on a different axis:

| நூல் | உடைக்கும் இடம் | The break |
|---|---|---|
| **கலித்தொகை** | யாப்பு | Not akaval. **கலிப்பா** — a metre built for two or more voices in the same poem |
| **பரிபாடல்** | பொருள் + வடிவம் | Not அகம்/புறம் but **devotion**; and it is a **songbook**, carrying music attributions |
| **பதிற்றுப்பத்து** | அமைப்பு | Not an anthology of loose poems but **eight surviving decads**, each with a prose dossier attached |

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The differences are not accidents of survival. Two of them are
prescribed by the grammar. The third is the consequence of a different purpose for the book.

---

## 1. இலக்கணம் முதலில் சொல்கிறது | The grammar names the pair first

Before reading any of the three, one Tolkāppiyam sutra reframes the whole question. In
பொருளதிகாரம், அகத்திணையியல், நூற்பா 56:

> நாடக வழக்கினும் உலகியல் வழக்கினும்
> பாடல் சான்ற புலனெறி வழக்கம்
> கலியே பரிபாட்டு ஆயிரு பாவினும்
> உரியது ஆகும் என்மனார் புலவர்.

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `_src/txt/pmuni0100-tholkappiyam-moolam.txt`, lines 2307–2310 (sutra
numbered 56 in the file).

**சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word**

| சொல் | பிரிப்பு | பொருள் | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| நாடக வழக்கினும் | நாடகம் + வழக்கு + இன் + உம் | நாடகத்தின் மரபிலும் | in dramatic usage also |
| உலகியல் வழக்கினும் | உலகு + இயல் + வழக்கு | உலக இயல்பான வழக்கிலும் | in ordinary worldly usage also |
| பாடல் சான்ற | — | பாடலுக்கு உரிய, சிறந்த | such as befits poetry |
| புலனெறி வழக்கம் | புலன் + நெறி + வழக்கம் | அறிவுநெறி சார்ந்த மரபு | the convention of the learned way |
| ஆயிரு பாவினும் | ஆ + இரு + பா + இன் + உம் | அந்த இரண்டு பாக்களிலும் | in those two metres |
| உரியது ஆகும் | — | உரிமையுடையது ஆகும் | is proper / belongs |

**பொருள் (தமிழ்):** நாடக வழக்கிலும் உலகியல் வழக்கிலும் அமைந்த, பாடலுக்குப் பொருந்திய
புலனெறி மரபு — **கலி, பரிபாட்டு** என்னும் அவ்விரு பாக்களுக்கே உரியது என்பர் புலவர்.

**Meaning (English):** The learned poetic convention, in *dramatic* usage as much as in ordinary
usage, belongs — say the poets — to those two metres: **kali and paripāṭṭu**.

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Two of the three works in this document are named together, as a pair, by the
grammar itself, and the word attached to them is **நாடக வழக்கு** — dramatic usage.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** So "kalittokai is theatrical" is not a modern impression imported
onto the text. It is the classification the tradition already made, and it made the same
classification for paripāṭal. What follows is an attempt to see the mechanism.

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** What did **நாடக வழக்கு** mean to whoever wrote this sutra —
staged drama with actors, or "the manner of speech proper to represented characters"? The sutra
does not define it, and this document does not decide it. The two readings have very different
consequences for what கலித்தொகை was *for*.

Three further sutras from the same file set the metrical frame:

> ஆசிரியம் வஞ்சி வெண்பா கலி என
> நால் இயற்று என்ப பா வகை விரியே. (101)

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** lines 3558–3559. Four pā: ஆசிரியம், வஞ்சி, வெண்பா, **கலி**.

> ஆசிரிய நடைத்தே வஞ்சி ஏனை
> வெண்பா நடைத்தே கலி என மொழிப. (104)

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** lines 3565–3566. வஞ்சி walks in the ஆசிரியம் gait; **கலி walks in the
வெண்பா gait**. That is a precise claim: kali is rhythmically closer to the tight, turning வெண்பா
than to the long even flow of akaval. **[காதுக்காக]** — whether that is audible is Ilam's call, not
mine.

> ஒத்தாழிசைக்கலி கலிவெண்பாட்டே
> கொச்சகம் உறழொடு கலி நால் வகைத்தே. (126)

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** lines 3615–3616. **கலி is itself four kinds**: ஒத்தாழிசைக்கலி,
கலிவெண்பாட்டு, கொச்சகம், உறழ். This matters below — it predicts that the 150 poems of
கலித்தொகை will *not* all share one architecture.

## 2.2 ஐந்து ஆசிரியர்கள் — ஆனால் யார் சொன்னது? | Five poets, on whose authority?

The file states the attributions plainly, one per section head:

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0221`, the ஆசிரியர் line under each section header:

| பகுதி | ஆசிரியர் (as written in file) | வரி |
|---|---|---|
| பாலைக்கலி | **பெருங்கொடுங்கோன்** | 60 |
| குறிஞ்சி | **கபிலர்** | 871 |
| மருதக்கலி | **மருதநிலங்கன்** | 1679 |
| முல்லைக் கலி | **சோழன் நல்லுத்திரன்** | 2730 |
| நெய்தல் கலி | **நல்லாந்துவனார்** | 3420 |

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The same file's English header note, lines 36–38, quoting Zvelebil's *Lexicon
of Tamil Literature* p. 309, says the attribution rests on a single verse and immediately doubts it:

> One of the verses in peruntokai (#2012) ascribes pAlai to perungkaTungkOn, kuRinji to kapilar,
> marutam to marutanilanAkan, mullai to cOlan nalluttiran and neital to nallantuvanAr, but the
> verocity of this venpA may be doubted.

("verocity" is the file's typo for *veracity*.)

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This is a rare case where the primary e-text hands us its own
source-criticism. The five names are **not** colophons attached to individual poems, the way
புறநானூறு and குறுந்தொகை carry them. They come from one late anthology-verse (பெருந்தொகை
#2012) that assigns whole sections wholesale. That is a much weaker kind of evidence than a
per-poem colophon.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS — testable]** If the five sections really are five single-author books,
each section should be internally more uniform than the anthology as a whole on measurable
features (line-length distribution, hinge-word frequency, vocative inventory). §2.4 below gives one
such measurement, and the result does **not** cleanly support single authorship — the variation
between sections is large, but that is equally explained by திணை and by metre-subtype. A proper
test would need per-section stylometry against a null model, which this session did not run.

**One name to hold on to.** நல்லாந்துவனார், credited here with நெய்தல் கலி, appears in the
பரிபாடல் file as **நல்லந்துவனார்**, the poet of four surviving paripāṭals (6, 8, 11, 20) —
`_src/txt/pmuni0087-ettuthogai-paripadal.txt`, lines 509, 738, 1132, 1925. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]**
Same man or not? The spellings differ by one letter (நல்லா- / நல்ல-) and this document cannot
settle it — but if it *is* one poet, then the single most-attested author across the two "dramatic"
metres is the same person, which would be a fact of some weight.

## 2.3 கலிப்பா என்ன செய்ய விடுகிறது | What kalippā lets a poem do

### The hinge word, prescribed and then found

Tolkāppiyam, செய்யுளியல், நூற்பா 131:

> அடை நிலைக் கிளவி தாழிசைப் பின்னர்
> நடை நவின்று ஒழுகும் ஆங்கு என் கிளவி.

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0100`, lines 3625–3626.

**சொல்லுக்குச் சொல்**

| சொல் | பொருள் | English |
|---|---|---|
| அடை நிலைக் கிளவி | இறுதி நிலைப் பகுதி | the closing-position element |
| தாழிசைப் பின்னர் | தாழிசைக்குப் பின் | after the tāḻisai (the repeating stanzas) |
| நடை நவின்று ஒழுகும் | நடையில் பயின்று செல்லும் | proceeds in a practised gait |
| ஆங்கு என் கிளவி | 'ஆங்கு' என்னும் சொல் | the word *āṅku* |

**பொருள் (தமிழ்):** தாழிசைகளுக்குப் பின் வரும் அடை-நிலைப் பகுதி, **'ஆங்கு'** என்னும்
சொல்லோடு நடையில் தொடர்ந்து செல்லும்.

**Meaning (English):** The closing member, coming after the repeating stanzas, runs on in its own
gait — with the word **āṅku**.

**The grammar names a specific word.** That is testable. And the anthology obeys:

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Machine count over `pmuni0221`:

- **`என ஆங்கு` occurs 66 times** in the file.
- **24 lines begin with `ஆங்கு`** at line-start.
- **70 of the 150 poems (47%) contain at least one such hinge**, 71 occurrences in all when counted
  per poem.
- **55 poems place it on a line entirely of its own** — a bare `என ஆங்கு,` occupying one whole
  line, as at file lines 55, 109, 135, 156, 212, 239, 263, 286 and 48 more.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** A single word, prescribed by the grammar as the joint of a
metrical form, is physically visible in the e-text as a one-word line seventy times over. The
transition is *typographically* a hinge because it is *structurally* a hinge: the several parallel
stanzas stop, and one voice — usually a summarising or resolving voice — takes over. In a dialogic
poem, that is the moment the argument is handed to whoever gets the last word.

And the distribution across sections is not flat:

| பகுதி | poems with a hinge | share |
|---|---|---|
| பாலை (2–36) | 23 / 35 | 66% |
| குறிஞ்சி (37–65) | 15 / 29 | 52% |
| மருதம் (66–100) | 10 / 35 | **29%** |
| முல்லை (101–117) | 2 / 17 | **12%** |
| நெய்தல் (118–150) | 19 / 33 | 58% |

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** The hinge belongs to **ஒத்தாழிசைக்கலி** — the sub-type with தரவு /
தாழிசை / அடை architecture that sutra 128–132 describes. Its scarcity in மருதம் and முல்லை is
then a *positive* finding, not a gap: those sections lean on the other three kali sub-types the
grammar names in sutra 126 (கலிவெண்பாட்டு, கொச்சகம், உறழ்), which do not require an ஆங்கு
close. மருதம் is the quarrel-landscape, and quarrels do not resolve into a summarising voice —
they end in the middle of the fight. **This prediction can be put at risk:** if a scholarly
edition's per-poem sub-type labels were checked against this table, the low-hinge sections should
be dominated by கொச்சகம்/உறழ். This session had no such labelled edition and did not test it.

### The measurable difference from akaval

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Syllable-count statistics computed over the verse lines of all three files
(independent vowels plus consonants not carrying புள்ளி; headers, colophons and numerals excluded):

| நூல் | யாப்பு | lines | mean | median | **sd** | ≤8 syll | ≥18 syll | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **கலித்தொகை** | கலிப்பா | 4,304 | 12.93 | 13 | **3.05** | **10.7%** | 2.6% | 25 |
| **பதிற்றுப்பத்து** | ஆசிரியப்பா | 1,813 | 12.26 | 12 | **2.16** | **3.4%** | 0.9% | 27 |
| **பரிபாடல்** | பரிபாடல் | 2,031 | 12.38 | 12 | **3.12** | **9.7%** | 3.6% | **32** |

**The mechanism, stated concretely.** All three average about the same line length. What separates
them is **variance**. Kali's standard deviation is **41% larger** than akaval's, and kali runs a
short line (≤8 syllables) **three times as often**. Akaval is an even walking metre — nine lines in
ten sit between 10 and 15 syllables. Kali keeps interrupting itself.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** That is exactly what dialogue costs. "வாரல் நீ; வந்தாங்கே மாறு;"
— *Don't come. Go back the way you came* — is three and a half feet, because a person shutting a
door does not speak in four-foot lines. Akaval can narrate a quarrel; kali can *be* one. The extra
variance is the room the second speaker takes up.

**[ellai — a limit of this method]** Line division in a Project Madurai e-text follows its printed
edition, and editions generally follow the traditional அடி — but not invariably, and a few very
long lines in பரிபாடல் (max 32) may be edition artefacts rather than single அடி. The *relative*
comparison across three files prepared by three different volunteer teams is the weaker part of the
claim; the kali/akaval gap is large enough (3.05 vs 2.16) that it is unlikely to be an artefact,
but it has not been checked against a critical edition.

### The vocative as stage direction

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0221` contains **386 question marks** and **566 exclamation marks**.
`pmuni0038-ettuthogai-pathitrupathu-moolam.txt` contains **zero** question marks.

**Important caveat, stated up front:** classical Tamil manuscripts carry no such punctuation. Every
`?` and `!` in these files was supplied by a modern editor. So the count is **evidence about the
editor's reading**, not directly about the text. It is still informative — a modern editor
punctuated 386 sentences in kali as questions and *not one* in pathitrupathu — but it is
second-hand.

The language-internal marker is stronger. கலித்தொகை has no speech tags. Nobody in it is introduced
with "she said" or "he replied". The speaker changes are carried by **விளி — the vocative** — and
the vocative is gendered and status-marked, so it tells you who is being addressed and therefore who
is speaking:

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** counts in `pmuni0221`: **எல்லா!** 37 · **நெஞ்சே** 12 · **ஏடா** 2 ·
**ஏடி** 0.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** `எல்லா` and `ஏடா` address a man; forms like `தெரி இழாய்`
(you of the chosen jewels), `மான் நோக்கி` (doe-glancing one), `மட மொழியோய்` address a woman;
`நெஞ்சே` addresses the speaker's own heart, and marks the aside — the line spoken past the
antagonist, to oneself, which the other character is not supposed to hear. **That is a stage
convention.** A poem with asides has an audience.

**[காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear]** Whether `எல்லா!` lands as neutral address, as familiar, or as
openly contemptuous — 37 occurrences and the tone is doing real work in every one of them. I can
count it; I cannot hear it.

### பொருள் (தமிழ்)

சூதுதான். என் வீட்டுக்கு நீ வராதே; வந்த வழியே திரும்பு.

**அவன்:** இவை என்ன சொற்கள்? ஒரே உயிருள்ள பறவையின் இரு தலைகளுள் ஒன்று மற்றொன்றோடு
போருக்கு நின்றாற்போல் — ஊடாதே. நீ இப்படிச் சொன்னால் என் அரிய உயிர் நிற்கும் வழி யாது?

**அவள்:** ஓஓ! தெளிந்துவிட்டோம் நாங்கள். கோபியாதே — எல்லாம் வல்லவனே! பெருங்காட்டுக்
கொற்றியிடம் பேய் கதை சொன்னாற் போல, உன் வஞ்சத்தைச் சொல்லி வருந்தாதே.

**அவன்:** மருந்தே இல்லை — மன்னவன் சினந்தால் அதில் தவறு உண்டோ? நீ விரும்பிய இனிய
நகையுடையாய்! தீமை ஒன்றும் என்னிடம் இல்லை.

**அவள் (தன் நெஞ்சுக்கு):** எல்லாவற்றையும் மறந்துவிட்ட இந்த நாணமில்லாதவனுக்கு இந்தப்
போர் — நெஞ்சே, முதுகைத் திருப்பிக்கொண்டு பார்ப்பாயாக! எதிர்த்து இவனைப் பொய் நிலைக்க
விடமாட்டோம் என்று நெருக்கினால், "தவறு செய்துவிட்டேன்" என்று காலில் விழுவதும் உண்டு.

### Meaning (English)

**She:** Who is this — the man grabbing my hair? Here is another piece of trickery well suited to
your "manliness." Don't come to my house. Go back the way you came.

**He:** What talk is this? As if one of the two heads of a single-lived bird turned on the other in
war — don't sulk. If you talk like that, by what road is my dear life to keep standing?

**She:** Ohhh — we understand perfectly. Don't get angry, O all-capable one! Don't wear yourself out
reciting your deceit — like a ghoul carrying tales to Koṟṟi of the great forest.

**He:** There's no cure for it. If the king rages, is any fault found in him? O sweet-laughing one
you desired — there is no wrong in me.

**She (to her own heart):** For this shameless man who has forgotten everything — this fight!
Heart: turn your back and just watch. If we press him, push back, refuse to let the lie stand, there
is such a thing as his coming to your feet saying *I was wrong.*

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**Four things this poem does that an akaval poem structurally cannot.**

**1. It quarrels in real time, with no narrator.** No line says who is speaking. The speakers are
identified only by the vocatives they use on each other — `எல்லா!` at him, `இன் நகை!` at her — and
by grammatical person. When she calls him *இவன்* ("this fellow"), he is standing right there. The
poem's opening move is a woman refusing to address her husband in the second person while he has
hold of her hair. **[விளக்கம்]** That is contempt encoded in grammar rather than stated in
vocabulary — and it is a device that needs at least two live voices to work at all.

**2. The joke is structural, not verbal.** `ஊராண்மைக்கு ஒத்த படிறு` — "trickery befitting your
*ūrāṇmai*." ஊராண்மை is a praise-word: public spirit, the manly virtue of the generous man. She
attaches it to *padiṟu*, deceit. The sarcasm is in the collocation. Similarly `எல்லாம் வல் எல்லா` —
"O all-capable one" — hurled at a husband who has just been caught out. **[காதுக்காக]** Whether a
Tamil ear reads `எல்லாம் வல் — எல்லா!` as heavy sarcasm or as something drier is exactly the kind
of thing I cannot judge.

**3. His counter-argument is a real argument, and it is beautiful.** `ஓர் உயிர்ப் புள்ளின் இரு
தலையுள் ஒன்று / போர் எதிர்ந்தற்று` — the two-headed bird with one life. If one head fights the
other, the bird dies, and so does the winner. He is not pleading; he is proving that the quarrel is
self-destructive. **[விளக்கம்]** In a marutam poem the husband is normally in the wrong and knows
it. Giving him a genuinely good image to argue with is what makes this a scene rather than a
complaint.

**4. The last word goes to a strategist.** The closing is spoken by the wife to her own heart, and
it is neither grief nor forgiveness. It is *tactics*: turn your back, hold the line, press him, and
he will fold. `காண்டைப்பாய்` — *just watch.* **[விளக்கம்]** The comedy is in the register-switch:
the poem ends not on emotion but on confident prediction of the opponent's collapse. The audience is
being invited to enjoy watching a fight that the woman is going to win.

**Why the ending needed கலி.** Notice the line lengths in the closing: a long line, a long line,
then `என்று அடி சேர்தலும் உண்டு.` — short, flat, and dropped. An akaval close runs even to the end
and lands on a particle. This one lands on an anticlimax deliberately, and the metre has to be able
to shorten to do it. **[காதுக்காக]** Computed, not heard.

## 2.5 வடிவம் ஒரு பாடலில் | The whole form, demonstrated in one poem — கலி 1

The invocation to the anthology is a textbook of its own metre. **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0221`,
poem 1, lines 41–58. Its architecture:

- **lines 42–45** — address to the eight-armed god, the frame (**தரவு**)
- **three parallel stanzas**, each three lines, each on one of Śiva's dances, each ending in the
  *same question shape*: `... தருவாளோ?` — *will she give it?* — at file lines 51 and 54, and the
  matching line above (**தாழிசை**)
- **line 55** — `என ஆங்கு,` alone on its line (the **hinge** the grammar named)
- **lines 56–58** — the resolution (**அடை / சுரிதகம்**)

The resolving lines, verbatim:

> என ஆங்கு,
> பாணியும் தூக்கும் சீரும் என்று இவை
> மாண் இழை அரிவை காப்ப,
> ஆணம் இல் பொருள் எமக்கு அமர்ந்தனை ஆடி.

|---|---|---|
| பாணி | தாள அளவு, கால அளவு | the time-measure, the beat |
| தூக்கு | ஓசை நிலை, தூக்கு வகை | the metrical/rhythmic lift |
| சீர் | அளவுக் கூறு | the foot, the measured unit |
| மாண் இழை அரிவை | சிறந்த அணிகலனுடைய பெண் | the woman of splendid jewels (Umā) |
| காப்ப | காக்க, தாங்க | keeping, holding [them] |

**பொருள் (தமிழ்):** ஆங்கு — பாணியும் தூக்கும் சீரும் என்னும் இவற்றை மாண்புமிக்க அணி
அணிந்த அரிவை காக்க, எமக்கு … பொருளை விரும்பி நீ ஆடினாய்.

**Meaning (English):** And so — the beat, the lift and the measure being held by the splendidly
jewelled woman, you danced, having consented to [grant] … for us.

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** `ஆணம் இல் பொருள்` — I do not have a confident gloss.
ஆணம் can be read as *stability / firmness* (hence "the impermanent world") or as *measure /
limit* (hence "the boundless good"), and the two readings pull the line in opposite directions.
The file carries no commentary. **This is left open deliberately.** Ilam's ear, or a உரை, decides.

**Why this matters beyond the invocation.** The three technical words in line 56 — **பாணி, தூக்கு,
சீர்** — are the vocabulary of *timed performance*, and **தூக்கு** turns up again, as a formal
colophon category, on every single one of the eighty poems of பதிற்றுப்பத்து (`தூக்கு -
செந்தூக்கு`, §4.3). **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** One shared technical vocabulary runs under all
three of these books: kali opens by naming the beat, paripāṭal's colophons name the mode, and
pathitrupathu's colophons name the தூக்கு. These are not three unrelated oddities. They are three
views of a corpus that was **performed**, and whose performance apparatus survives in different
fragments in each book.

**Finding 1 — the roles are genuinely separate.** In **20 of the 21** poems with a legible

records the same man on both sides of the line, in both directions, is recording a real division of
labour, not a formula.

**Finding 2 — one house musician.** Counting `மருத்துவன் நல்லச்சுதனார்` (6 poems: 6, 8, 9, 10, 15,

person is not stated in the file. `மருத்துவன்` ("the physician") is a professional epithet, and

the largest single figure is 6 of 21, which is still the dominant hand. The same caution applies to

Checking a second edition (e.g. U.Vē. Cāminātaiyar's) against this would settle it. **This session
had one file and could not check.**

## 3.3 இசைச் சான்று: என்ன நிறுவுகிறது, என்ன நிறுவவில்லை | The music evidence, carefully

This is the point at which it is easy to overclaim, so let me separate the layers.

### What the colophons ARE NOT

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION — a correction to a common framing]** These colophons are **not
notation.** Nothing in this file records pitch, scale degree, interval, duration, ornament, or any
symbol standing for a sound. There is no Tamil equivalent of neumes or sargam here. What each
colophon gives is **three attributions**: who made the words, who made the music, and what mode it
went in. To call பரிபாடல் "notated" would be to claim something the source does not contain, and
the honest description is different and still remarkable: **this is a corpus of songs whose
attribution apparatus survived even though the melodies did not.**

### What the colophons DO establish

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The manuscript tradition that reached this edition preserved, for each poem:
a poet, a distinct music-setter, and a named mode — and preserved them consistently enough that
they form the ordered blocks in §3.2. That is a real institutional fact about how these poems were
kept.

### What is genuinely uncertain about them

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION — three separate doubts, not one]**

1. **Are the colophons contemporary with the poems?** Colophons in the eight anthologies are
   generally understood to be compiler-added, not poet-added (`../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §1
   states this for the corpus at large). If these were added centuries after composition, they
   record what a later tradition *believed* about the music, not what the poets did.
2. **Does `பண்` here mean what `பண்` means later?** பாலையாழ், நோதிறம், காந்தாரம் are mode-names,
   and the later Tamil பண் system (the one the Tēvāram inherits) uses overlapping vocabulary. It
   does **not** follow that the term denoted the same musical object in both periods. Terminological
   continuity is not musical continuity.
3. **Can the music be recovered?** No. Not from this.

### The internal evidence, which is stronger than the colophons

Here the poems corroborate their own apparatus, and this is the best evidence in the file.

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0087`, poem **11** (a வையை poem whose colophon at line 1134 reads
`பண் :: பாலையாழ்`), lines **1268–1272**, verbatim:

**Meaning (English):** Listen to the tune the bees, blowing at the flowers, have taken up on the

> இன் இயல் மாண் தேர்ச்சி இசை பரிபாடல்_____

*— the sweet-natured, finely-schooled, **sung paripāṭal**.* (The trailing underscores are the
e-text's marker for a broken or elided passage.)

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] — why this is the strongest evidence in the file.** Three things
converge inside a single poem:

1. Its **colophon** says the mode is `பாலையாழ்` (line 1134).
2. Its **text**, 136 lines later, names `பாலை இசை` (line 1270) — the same mode, from inside the
   verse, where no compiler could have put it.
3. Its **text** calls its own form `இசை பரிபாடல்` (line 1281) — *sung* paripāṭal.

Whatever doubts attach to colophons as an editorial layer, **doubt 1 above does not touch this**.
The poem is internally aware of being a mode-specific sung composition, and it is using
scale-degree vocabulary — குரல், கிளை, உழை — to describe how bees sound. That vocabulary is
technical: it names steps of a scale, and the poet expects the audience to catch a joke built on it.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** A poet does not build a conceit on குரல் / கிளை / உழை unless the
audience already knows those words as musical terms. So the internal evidence supports something
narrower and firmer than "notation": **a shared technical vocabulary for pitch and rhythm, in
circulation among a lay poetic audience, at the time of composition.** That is a substantial claim
about the state of Tamil music, and it rests on the verse itself rather than on the apparatus.

**[காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear]** My glosses of குரல் / கிளை / உழை as *scale degrees* rather
than as ordinary words (குரல் = voice; கிளை = branch/kin; உழை = a deer, or nearness) are the
crux of this reading. The poem is almost certainly punning across both senses — the bee is on a
*branch*, and *kiḷai* is also a note. **I cannot adjudicate this.** It needs a உரை and it needs
Ilam.

**Meaning (English):** O dark one! O daaark one! You of the flawless red foot that severs rebirth —

**நுட்பம் | The craft.** The poem opens with the same vocative **three times in three lines**, and
the second occurrence is stretched — `மாஅயோயே` carries the அளபெடை that `மா அயோய்` does not.

repetition with *variation in length*, and the varying element is a syllable's duration. In a poem
that its own colophon assigns to a mode, an opening built out of one word held for three different
durations reads as a **refrain** — the thing a chorus can come in on.

longer than `மா அயோயே`, and whether the three-fold call feels like a refrain or like ordinary
Sangam vocative piling. **This is precisely the judgment I cannot make.** I can see the அளபெடை on
the page; I have no idea what it does.

56 share a device — the *repeated element a group can join* — which the four-foot akaval line does
not naturally provide. If so, "நாடக வழக்கு" may point less at staged plays than at **poetry with a
part for more than one mouth.**

# 4. பதிற்றுப்பத்து

## 4.1 எஞ்சியது, இழந்தது | What survives and what was lost

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `_src/txt/pmuni0038-ettuthogai-pathitrupathu-moolam.txt`. The file opens the
book with a two-line statement of loss (lines 18–19):

> முதற் பத்து
> (கிடைத்திலது)

*The first decad — (not obtained.)*

And closes it the same way (lines 2406–2408):

> பத்தாம் பத்து
> ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
> (கிடைக்கவில்லை)

*The tenth decad — (not available.)*

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Machine count: the file contains **exactly 80 poems**, numbered `பாட்டு - 11`
through `பாட்டு - 90`, in unbroken sequence with no gaps. The lost decads are precisely poems
**1–10** and **91–100**.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The book's own name is its inventory: **பதிற்றுப்பத்து** =
"ten tens." The count 80 is not an estimate; it is the arithmetic of a titled structure with two
tenths missing from the ends. Unlike பரிபாடல், where the extent of loss is genuinely unknown,
here the loss is *exactly measurable* — because the frame survived even where the contents did not.

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The file appends **five fragments** (lines 2409–2459) recovered from
Tolkāppiyam commentaries and from the later anthology புறத்திரட்டு, with this note at line 2409:

> பதிற்றுப்பத்தில் விட்டுப்போன, முதற்பத்தையோ பத்தாம் பத்தையோ சார்ந்த சில பகுதிகள் தொல்காப்பிய
> உரைகளாலும் புறத்திரட்டாலும் தெரிய வந்தன. அவை வருமாறு:

*Some portions belonging to the lost first decad or the tenth decad of Patiṟṟuppattu have become
known through the Tolkāppiyam commentaries and through the Puṟattiraṭṭu. They are as follows:*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Note the honesty of `முதற்பத்தையோ பத்தாம் பத்தையோ` — *either the
first or the tenth*. The editor will not assign them. That is the same reconstruction mechanism that
produced the பரிபாடல் திரட்டு: **when a Sangam text dies, the commentaries are its fossil record**,
because a grammarian quoting a line to illustrate a rule preserves the line by accident.

## 4.2 பதிகம் — the dossier at the end of each decad

Each surviving decad closes with a prose-and-verse apparatus that has no parallel anywhere else in
the eight anthologies. Its parts, in fixed order:

1. a verse summarising the king's deeds and naming his parentage
2. `X பாடினார் பத்துப்பாட்டு` — *X sang ten poems*
3. `அவைதாம்: …` — **the ten poem-titles, in order**, closed by `இவை பாட்டின் பதிகம்`
4. `பாடிப்பெற்ற பரிசில்: …` — **the gift the poet received for singing**
5. `[King] N ஆண்டு வீற்றிருந்தான்` — *the king reigned N years*

**A note on the word.** This document's brief calls it **படிகம்**; the file consistently writes
**பதிகம்** (`இவை பாட்டின் பதிகம்`, e.g. line 342). Both forms circulate in the tradition. I use the
file's spelling when quoting and flag the variation rather than silently normalising it.

So this is a **catalogue system**: poem → title-phrase → line number → decad list. The apparatus is
not commentary. It is *filing*. **[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** Someone maintained பதிற்றுப்பத்து as
an indexed archive rather than as a book to be read through — which would explain why its
apparatus survived the loss of two entire decads intact enough that we can name what is missing.

(N)` **with** the line number on 19 poems (decad 2 and most of decad 3), and `பெயர்: X` **without**
it on the remaining 61. **[விளக்கம்]** That looks like a change of hand or exemplar partway
through, not a change of practice — the naming is universal; only the line-reference is not.

inside the verse instead of after it.

### பொருள் (தமிழ்)

*(என் நடை; திருத்தத்திற்கு — my rendering, for correction)*

கள்ளை உண்ணுங்கள்! சோற்றைச் சமையுங்கள்! ஊன் துண்டுகளை வெட்டுங்கள், புழுக்கலை அடுப்பில்
ஏற்றுங்கள்! வருபவர்க்கு வரம்பின்றி — பொற்கலன்கள் ஒலிக்க — இருள் போல் சுருண்டு செழித்து
முடி அவிழ்ந்த ஐம்பால் கூந்தலும், ஏந்திய அல்குலும், அரும்பு போன்ற புன்னகையும் உடைய இளம்
**கூந்தல் விறலியர்** அடுப்பை நடத்துக. பெற்றதைத் தவறின்றிக் கொடுங்கள். மேலும் —
உயிர்கள் அழியும்படிப் பல ஆண்டு உலகை அசைத்து, மண்ணுலகைப் பாதுகாக்க ஏற்றுக்கொண்ட
தண்ணிய மேகம் பொழியாது மாறி **மாரியே பொய்த்தாலும், சேரலாதன் நசையைப் பொய்யான்.**

### Meaning (English)

Drink the toddy! Cook the rice! Cut the meat, set the steaming pot on the fire! To all who come,
without stint, with the golden vessels ringing — let the **long-haired viṟaliyar**, young women with
dark luxuriant hair loosened from its coil, with lifted hips and bud-like smiles, run the hearth.
Give away what you have received, without fail. And further: though the cool cloud that undertook to
sustain this earth should shake the world for years together until the living perish, should turn
aside and not pour — though **the rain itself prove false, Cēralātaṉ does not falsify hope.**

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**1. The whole poem is in the imperative.** உண்மின், அடுமின், எறிக, ஏற்றுமின், வழங்குக,
உதவுமின் — six commands in seven lines. **[விளக்கம்]** It is not a description of a feast. It is
the **noise of one**, a voice shouting across a courtyard. The praise of the king is not stated
until the last two lines; everything before it is the evidence, delivered as sound.

**2. The turn is one word wide.** Line 11 sets up the biggest conditional available — the failure of
the monsoon, the death of everything — and line 12 answers it with a bare negated noun:
`பொய்யலன்`. **[விளக்கம்]** The rhetorical machine here is a comparison to the **only** thing in the
Tamil world more reliable than a Chera king, followed by the claim that it is the less reliable of
the two. And `நசை` is the load-bearing word: not *rain*, not *food* — **the wanting itself**. The
rain can fail your crop; the king cannot fail your *hope*. **[காதுக்காக]** Whether ending on
`நசையே` lands as devastating or merely as formula is Ilam's judgment.

**3. The title tells you the filing system is not a summary.** This poem's `பெயர்` is **கூந்தல்
விறலியர்** — taken from line 6, where the long-haired performers are told to run the kitchen. That
is *not* what the poem is about. **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The பெயர் is an **incipit-style
tag**: a phrase distinctive enough to identify the poem, chosen for uniqueness rather than aboutness
— the same logic by which a papal bull or a Vedic hymn is cited by its opening words. Confirmation
that this apparatus is a librarian's, not a critic's.

**4. The women are named as professionals.** `விறலியர்` are performers, and here they are handed the
hearth. **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Whether `வழங்குக அடுப்பே` means they *serve from* the
hearth or *are given* the hearth (i.e. the kitchen's whole yield as their fee) changes the social
picture considerably, and the bare text supports both. I have translated it the first way and flag
that the choice is mine.

*The gift received for singing: having given a hundred thousand kāṇam as a small thing besides, that
king climbed the hill called Naṉṟā, stood there, and showed and gave him every land his own eye
could see.*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The mechanism of the grant is *visual*: the boundary of the gift is
the horizon from a named hill. Whatever its historicity, this is a specific, falsifiable-in-principle
kind of statement — it names a hill.

> பாடிப்பெற்ற பரிசில்: தானும் கோயிலாளும் புறம்போந்து நின்று கோயிலுள்ள எல்லாம் கொண்மின்
> என்று காணம் ஒன்பது நூறாயிரத்தோடு அரசுகட்டில் கொடுப்ப அவர் யான் இரப்ப இதனை ஆள்க என்று
> அமைச்சுப் பூண்டார்.

*The gift received for singing: he and the lady of the palace came outside and stood there, saying
"take everything that is in the palace"; and when, with nine hundred thousand kāṇam, he offered the
throne itself — the poet said "I am the one who begs; you rule this," and took up the ministership.*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This is the only entry in the ledger where the transaction fails —
and it is also the only decad with **no regnal year recorded**. Whether those two absences are
connected is not something the file lets me determine, but it is a striking coincidence in a
document whose whole point is completeness.

### What this apparatus does and does not establish

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Summing the seven recorded reigns: 58 + 25 + 25 + 55 + 38 + 25 + 16 =
**242 years** of Chera rule, in named sequence, with named poets attached.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] — what makes it "documentary".** No other Sangam text does this. A
புறநானூறு colophon names a poet and a patron; a பதிகம் names the poet, the patron, the patron's
*parents*, the poems *by title*, the *payment*, and the *length of reign*. That is the structure of
an archive entry, not a literary note. It is why Chera dynastic reconstruction leans on this book.

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION] — what it does not establish. Four separate doubts:**

1. **The பதிகம் are not the poems.** They speak of the poet in the third person and summarise his
   decad. They are therefore **later than** the verses they close, by an unknown interval. A record
   compiled after the fact by an interested party is a source, but not a contemporary one.
2. **The regnal figures are internally strange.** Two reigns of 58 and 55 years, in an era of
   constant warfare, in a list of nine. That is not impossible; it is the kind of number a
   *tradition* produces more readily than a *register*.
3. **Nothing here is externally corroborated in this file.** No inscription, no coin, no dated
   synchronism appears. The chronology is self-referential.
4. **The gifts are also praise.** "He gave the poet everything the eye could see" is simultaneously
   a payment record and the highest available compliment. The genre of the statement contaminates
   its evidentiary value, and the two functions cannot be separated from the text alone.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS — the narrowest honest claim]** பதிற்றுப்பத்து's apparatus establishes
that a **tradition of documentary record-keeping about patronage existed and was thought worth
attaching to poetry.** That is a strong finding about Tamil literary institutions. It is a much
weaker finding about the actual dates of actual kings, and the two should not be traded for each
other.

**One thing not to miss.** **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The sixth decad is by **காக்கைபாடினியார்
நச்செள்ளையார்** (line 1531), a woman, and she receives gold "கலன் அணிக என்று" — *that she may wear
jewels* — and a seat at the king's own side (line 1534). A woman poet holds an entire decad of the
most political book in the corpus, and the record of her fee survived. `../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §5
raises the question of what the anthologists kept of the ~30 women poets. Here is one answer: they
kept the receipt.

## 5. மூன்றும் சேர்ந்து என்ன சொல்கின்றன | The three together

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Read as a set rather than as three anomalies, these books preserve
three different fragments of the same lost thing: **Sangam poetry as performance.**

| நூல் | what survived of the performance |
|---|---|
| **கலித்தொகை** | the **script** — multiple voices, asides, a hinge word the grammar named, no stage directions because the vocatives are the stage directions |
| **பரிபாடல்** | the **credits** — who wrote it, who set it, what mode; plus a poem that names its own form as *sung* |
| **பதிற்றுப்பத்து** | the **contract** — who commissioned it, what it was called, what it paid |

None of the three preserves the performance. Each preserves a different piece of paperwork around
it. **[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** The five "normal" anthologies look normal partly because their
performance apparatus did *not* survive — not because they had none. The three odd books are odd in
the way a surviving playbill is odd next to a surviving poem.

**A shared vocabulary supports this.** `தூக்கு` is a colophon category on all 80 pathitrupathu poems
*and* one of the three performance terms in the kalithogai invocation (`பாணியும் தூக்கும் சீரும்`,
file line 56). `பண்` and `தாளம்` appear in paripadal's colophons *and* inside paripadal 11's verse.
These are not three separate technical vocabularies. They are one vocabulary, showing through in
three places.

**[முன்மொழிவு | PROPOSAL] For `../ilakkiyam/sangam.md`.** The corpus table there describes eight
anthologies of akam and puram. It is accurate and it is incomplete in a specific way: it has no
column for **how a poem was delivered**. A future revision could add one, and these three books are
where the evidence for it lives.

## 6. காலம் | On dates

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Per `../murai.md` and the working rule set out in
`../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §5, this document takes **no position** on the dates of these three
works, and will not launder a remembered scholarly consensus into a citation.

What can be said from sources in hand:

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §1 gives the corpus-level range: poems
  composed roughly **300 BCE – 300 CE**, anthologised centuries later, with colophons added by
  compilers.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The kalithogai file's header note (line 32) cites Zvelebil's *Lexicon of
  Tamil Literature* p. 309 for the anthology's **structure**, and for **doubt** about its
  attribution verse. It gives no date.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** It is commonly held that கலித்தொகை and பரிபாடல் are **late** within the
  corpus and பதிற்றுப்பத்து **early**. I have not verified who holds those positions from any
  source available in this session, and therefore do not assert them here. Three lines of evidence
  *would* bear on it and can be checked later: பரிபாடல்'s Puranic material and Sanskrit-derived
  vocabulary; கலித்தொகை's metrical distance from akaval; and பதிற்றுப்பத்து's shared poets with
  புறநானூறு (**கபிலர்**, **பரணர்** both hold decads here). None of these is decisive alone.

**Whoever picks this up next: name the scholar or say the question is open. Do not do both by
implication.**

## 7. காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear — for Ilam

Everything below is **computed, not heard**. `../murai.md` §8 puts these on the organic side of the
asymmetry. Listed in descending order of how much of this document rests on them.

1. **`எல்லாம் வல் — எல்லா!` (கலி 89, line 2324).** Is this heavy sarcasm, dry mockery, or something
   affectionate? My entire reading of the poem as *comedy* rather than *bitter complaint* turns on
   this one vocative. If it lands warm, the poem is a different poem.
2. **`மாஅயோயே` vs `மா அயோயே` (பரிபாடல் 3, lines 228, 230).** Is the அளபெடை audibly a stretch? Does
   the three-fold opening feel like a **refrain a group could join**, or like ordinary Sangam
   vocative piling? §3.4's hypothesis about paripadal as choral song depends entirely on this.
3. **`குரல் · கிளை · உழை` (பரிபாடல் 11, lines 1269–1270).** Do these read to you as musical
   scale-degrees, as ordinary words (voice / branch / nearness), or as a pun holding both? This is
   the crux of the strongest music claim in §3.3.
4. **`ஆங்கு` as a hinge.** I can prove it is there 71 times. I cannot tell whether a listener *hears*
   the turn arrive — whether the one-word line is a beat of silence, a change of voice, or invisible
   in performance.
5. **கலி vs அகவல்.** The measurement says kali's line-length variance is 41% larger. Does kali
   actually *sound* interrupted, conversational, uneven — or does the ear smooth it into a different
   regularity I cannot see in syllable counts?
6. **`நசையே` as an ending (பதிற்றுப்பத்து 18, line 253).** Does closing on *the wanting itself*
   land, or is it formula? I have called it devastating. That may be my reading of an English gloss,
   not of the Tamil.
7. **`ஊராண்மைக்கு ஒத்த படிறு` (கலி 89, line 2319).** Is the collocation of a praise-word with
   *deceit* as jarring in Tamil as it is in my translation?
8. **My own Tamil throughout.** Every `பொருள் (தமிழ்)` paragraph in this document is **my prose, not
   quoted**, and the register may well be wrong — too modern, too flat, or wrong in idiom. Treat
   them as drafts. The quoted மூலம் is exact; my Tamil around it is not authoritative.

3. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Is the paripadal paṇ-block ordering (§3.2, Finding 3) original to the

4. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Are `மருத்துவன் நல்லச்சுதனார்` and `நல்லச்சுதனார்` one person? The answer
   changes "half the book" to "a quarter of the book".
5. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Why does kalithogai order its sections **பாலை first**, against the usual
   ஐந்திணை sequence?
6. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Does the low `ஆங்கு`-hinge rate in மருதம் (29%) and முல்லை (12%) track the
   kali sub-types of sutra 126, as §2.3 predicts? Testable against any edition carrying per-poem
   sub-type labels.
7. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** `ஆணம் இல் பொருள்` (கலி 1, line 58) — *unstable thing* or *boundless good*?
   Opposite readings, no commentary in file.
8. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Why does the eighth decad alone lack a regnal year — and is it related to
   its being the one decad where the gift was refused?
9. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** The traditional figure of **70** paripadal poems appears nowhere in the
   source file. Where does it actually come from, and is its itemisation by deity independent
   evidence or a back-formation?
