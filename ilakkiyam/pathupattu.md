# பத்துப்பாட்டு | Pattuppāṭṭu — The Ten Long Poems

**The other half of the Sangam corpus.** `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` opens with the sentence
"18 books — **எட்டுத்தொகை** (eight anthologies) and **பத்துப்பாட்டு** (ten long poems)" and then
reads four poems, all four from the anthologies. This file is the missing half: ten poems, no
anthology, no editor's arrangement — ten separate compositions, each long enough to hold a whole
city, a whole night, a whole road inside it.

Everything below is built from the ten local Project Madurai e-texts named in the table. Every line
of மூலம் quoted here was copied out of those files by script, never from memory, and carries its
**file name and file line number** so that a `grep -n` can check it. Counts are counted, not
asserted; where the count disagrees with what the tradition says, the disagreement is reported
rather than smoothed. Provenance labels follow `../../murai.md` §11.

**A note on the numbers you will see twice.** These e-texts preserve the print edition's line
markers — the `10`, `20`, `30` … numerals that sit at the ends of lines. So each quoted line has
**two** numbers: the **file line** (where `grep -n` finds it) and the **print line** (the poem's own
line number, the one a scholar cites). Both are given. They do not match, and §9 explains exactly
where and why.

Two entries in that table are worth stopping on before anything else.

[மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0067-…-thirumurugatruppadai.txt`, l. 27: `பாடப்பட்டவன் :: முருகப்பெருமான்`]
**Poem 1's patron is a god.** Every other ஆற்றுப்படை directs a poor artist to a rich man.
திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை keeps the entire machinery — the meeting on the road, the description of the
patron's country, the promise of gifts — and swaps the rich man for Murugan. That single
substitution is how a secular Sangam genre became a devotional text; it is recited today as
scripture and is counted in the Śaiva **திருமுறை** as well as in the Sangam corpus. One poem, two
canons, because one slot in a form was filled differently.

[மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0071-…-maduraikkanchi.txt`, l. 28: `திணை :: காஞ்சி`]
**Poem 6 is not a praise-poem, it is a warning.** காஞ்சித்திணை in the புறம் system is the theme of
impermanence. மதுரைக்காஞ்சி spends seven hundred lines showing a king the most magnificent city in
the Tamil country — its harbour, its four quarters, its festivals, its night — and the point of the
inventory is that all of it is perishable. It is the longest poem in the corpus and it is,
structurally, a memento mori addressed to a man at the height of his power.

### The two extremes

[முடிவு | RESULT]

- **Shortest: முல்லைப்பாட்டு, 103 lines.** Nothing else is close — the next shortest is more than
  eighty per cent longer.
- **Longest: மதுரைக்காஞ்சி, 782 lines.** Also nothing close — 199 lines longer than the runner-up.
- The corpus therefore spans **7.6×** from shortest to longest. "Ten long poems" is a name of
  convenience, not a description of a uniform form.

**A correction to a common statement.** It is often said that நெடுநல்வாடை is one of the long ones.
[முடிவு | RESULT — counted] It is not. At **188 lines it is the second-shortest of the ten**, closer
to முல்லைப்பாட்டு than to the middle of the pack. Ranked by length the ten run:

103 · **188** · 248 · 261 · 269 · 301 · 317 · 500 · 583 · 782

நெடுநல்வாடை sits second from the bottom. Whatever makes it feel monumental — and it does feel
monumental — is not its length. §7 argues it is the *structure*: 188 lines that hold two places at
once. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

**Mean 355 lines; median 285.** For scale: the longest poem in அகநானூறு is 31 lines
(`../ilakkiyam-agananuru.md` §1). **மதுரைக்காஞ்சி alone is longer than the twenty-five longest
அகநானூறு poems combined.** That is the whole point of this half of the corpus, and §5 is about what
the space is spent on.

## 3. ஆற்றுப்படை | Directing a stranger down the road

**Five of the ten are ஆற்றுப்படை.** [மூலம் | SOURCE — the string `துறை :: ஆற்றுப்படை` appears in the
header of exactly five of the ten files:]

| பாட்டு | File | Header line | Who is directed |
|---|---|---|---|
| திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை | `pmuni0067` | l. 29 | a devotee (to a god) |
| பொருநராற்றுப்படை | `pmuni0063` | l. 23 | **பொருநன்** — a war-bard |
| சிறுபாணாற்றுப்படை | `pmuni0064` | l. 30 | **சிறுபாணன்** — a small-lute bard |
| பெரும்பாணாற்றுப்படை | `pmuni0069` | l. 31 | **பெரும்பாணன்** — a great-lute bard |
| மலைபடுகடாம் | `pmuni0078` | l. 32 | **கூத்தர்** — dancers; hence its alternate name **கூத்தராற்றுப்படை** |

### சொல் | The word

**ஆற்றுப்படுத்தல்** — to set someone *on the road*. ஆறு here is not the river but the **way**;
படுத்து is to place. The genre's name is a verb: *road-placing*.

### The form

The situation is always the same, and it is always a **chance meeting between two poor people**.

1. A performer walking home from a patron — fed, clothed, paid — meets another performer on the
   road, ragged and hungry, walking the other way.
2. The fed one **describes the other's poverty** in detail, and often his instrument in far more
   detail than his body: the yāḻ's belly, its pegs, the sinew of its strings.
3. He names the patron he has just left, describes his country at length, and lists what he
   received.
4. He gives **directions** — the road, the villages, what to eat where, whom to greet.
5. He ends with a conditional: *if you go to him, you will get this.*

The last move is a fixed grammatical gesture. Three of the five end on an "if you go" clause or an
imperative to go:

> **`நல்லியக் கோடனை நயந்தனிர் செலினே.`**
> — சிறுபாணாற்றுப்படை, `pmuni0064-…-sirupanatruppadai.txt` l. 300 (print line **269**, the poem's
> last line)

*"…if you go, desiring Nalliyakkōṭaṉ."* The whole 269-line poem is the protasis of a conditional
whose apodosis is left to the world to supply. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

> **`சென்மோ விரவல சிறக்கநின் னுள்ளங்`**
> — பெரும்பாணாற்றுப்படை, `pmuni0069-…-perumpanatruppadai.txt` l. 76 (print line ~45)

*"Go, O supplicant — may your heart flourish."* Note **இரவல** (`விரவல` in the file, with the
preceding sandhi consonant carried onto the word): the vocative of இரவலன், *one who asks*. The
speaker does not soften the word. He addresses the man by his condition. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

And the address at the very opening of பொருநராற்றுப்படை, which names the stranger by his craft and
his hunger in the same breath:

> **`சாறுகழி வழிநாட் சோறுநசை யுறாது`**
> **`வேறுபுல முன்னிய விரகறி பொருந`**
> — `pmuni0063-…-porunararruppadai.txt` ll. 28–29 (print lines 2–3)

| சொல் | பொருள் |
|---|---|
| சாறு கழி வழி நாள் | the day after the festival has ended |
| சோறு நசை உறாது | not getting the food you hoped for |
| வேறு புலம் முன்னிய | having set out for another country |
| விரகு அறி **பொருந** | *O poruṉaṉ who knows the trick of it* — vocative |

The festival is over, the free food is gone, and the man is on the road to somewhere else. That is
the opening image of the genre: **the day after.** [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

### ஏன் இது வியப்பானது | Why this is a strange and revealing thing to have invented

[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION — the reading is mine; the observations under it are from the files.]

Most literatures produce praise-poems, and a praise-poem's shape is obvious: *poet → patron.* One
mouth, one ear, one transaction. ஆற்றுப்படை does something none of that predicts. It bends the
line. The poem is not addressed to the patron at all. **It is addressed to a competitor.**

Four things follow, and each is odd enough to be worth naming:

**1. The patron is present only as a report.** He never hears the poem in the fiction of the poem.
He is described to a third party — the way you describe a good employer to someone still looking.
The praise is therefore structurally *testimony*, not flattery: its rhetorical situation is one
worker telling another where the work is. That is a completely different truth-condition from
court praise, and the poems know it. [கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS — testable against the puram
anthologies: praise addressed *to* a king should behave differently, rhetorically, from praise
*reported* about him. Someone should run it.]

**2. It is a literature of referral, and referral is the opposite of scarcity behaviour.** These are
performers competing for a finite number of patrons. The genre's entire content is one performer
sending business to another. Whatever the Sangam patronage economy actually was, it produced a
prestige form whose subject is **passing the opportunity on**. [திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION] Was
that because patronage was genuinely abundant, or because the genre is idealising a world in which
it was? The poems cannot answer this; they are evidence of the ideal, not of the economy.

**3. The road is described because the road is the deliverable.** The reason these poems contain
the corpus's best topography — which village, which water, which stretch is dangerous at night — is
that in the fiction, **the listener has to actually walk it.** Documentary precision is not a
literary ambition here; it is a functional requirement of the fiction. This is why
பெரும்பாணாற்றுப்படை can march its bard through all five landscapes in order and never feel like a
catalogue: he is walking. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

**4. The instrument gets the body-description.** பொருநராற்றுப்படை opens not with a person but with a
**yāḻ**, described part by part for line after line — its bowl compared to a cow's hoofprint, its
skin to the belly of a young pregnant woman, its neck to a raised cobra, its frets to a dark
woman's bangles. The Sangam machinery for describing a beloved's body is turned, at full power, onto
a wooden instrument. [மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0063` ll. 30–46] For a project about media and
resonance, that is not a curiosity. It is a two-thousand-year-old poem opening by lavishing the
language of love on *the thing that carries the sound*.

**5. Where else the form went.** [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] Poem 1 shows what happens when the slot
marked "patron" is filled with a god instead of a king: the genre survives the substitution
completely intact, and becomes devotional literature. The form turns out to be about the *structure*
of being directed toward abundance, not about who is at the end of the road. That is why
திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை is still recited when the kings it would otherwise have praised are footnotes.

Three findings:

**(a) The four-foot அகவல் line is the default, and it is very nearly absolute.** Seven poems sit at
96–99 % four-token lines with ~25 characters per line. This is ஆசிரியப்பா and nothing else. The
few outliers are e-text damage, not metrical variation (§9).

**(b) பட்டினப்பாலை is the metrical odd one out, and the file header knew it** — it is the only one of
the ten whose header omits `பாவகை`. Its lines average **2.92** tokens but **7.2 characters per
token**: fewer, longer feet. Its line length distribution is bimodal — 157 two-token lines against
132 four-token ones. And the two kinds **interleave throughout the poem**, in runs of three to
twenty-five lines, right up to the final couplet:

| print lines (approx.) | line type |
|---|---|
| 1–21 | short |
| 22–26 | long |
| 27–28 | short |
| … *(≈24 alternations across the poem)* … | |
| 293–299 | short |
| 300–301 | long |

[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] This is a poem that changes gear rather than a poem in one metre — short
lines for accumulation (the harbour, the goods, the crowds), long lines for narration and for the
close. The traditional description of பட்டினப்பாலை as ஆசிரியப்பா **கலந்த** வஞ்சி — mixed measure — is
what the counts show. [காதுக்காக] Whether the short runs actually *sound* like accumulation is Ilam's
call, not mine.

**(c) மதுரைக்காஞ்சி's opening is also short-line, and my token count is lying about it.** Its lines
show 4.00 tokens — apparently four feet — but only **18.8 characters per line** and **4.7 characters
per token**, both far below every other poem. The e-text has inserted spaces inside compounds
(`ஒலி முந்நீர் வரம் பாகத்` for what a printed edition sets as two units). So மதுரைக்காஞ்சி opens in
**short lines like பட்டினப்பாலை**, and the token metric fails on it. Recording the failure rather than
the number: this is what a control column is for.

## 5. நெடிய வடிவம் தரும் பரிசு | What the long form buys

An அகநானூறு poem gets thirteen to thirty-one lines and must spend nearly all of them on one interior
state. A பத்துப்பாட்டு poem gets three hundred and can spend a hundred of them on a harbour.

What the space buys is **sustained physical description of the ancient world with nowhere to hide** —
because a long description cannot be carried by a single good image. It has to keep being true.
பட்டினப்பாலை on the Chola port of காவிரிப்பூம்பட்டினம் and மதுரைக்காஞ்சி on Madurai are, for long
stretches, **documentary**. They are the best material in Tamil for showing a learner what this world
physically looked like, and the reason is prosaic: the poems name things, and things are checkable.

### 5.1 The customs house at காவிரிப்பூம்பட்டினம்

**மூலம்** — `pmuni0077-pathupattu-pattinappalai.txt`, file lines 150–151 and 157–161

> `வைகல்தொறும் அசைவின்றி`
> `உல்குசெயக் குறைபடாது`
> …
> `அளந்தறியாப் பலபண்டம்`
> `வரம்பறியாமை வந்தீண்டி`

| உல்குசெயக் குறைபடாது | உல்கு செய்ய + குறைபடாது | the levying of duty falling short in nothing |

**பொருள் | Meaning.** *Day after day without slack, the duty levied and nothing falling short of it;
goods beyond counting, arriving without limit; and the formidable officer of the great guarded
[warehouse] stamps the tiger and sends them out.*

**நுட்பம் | Why this matters.** **புலி பொறித்து** — *stamped with the tiger.* The tiger is the Chola
royal emblem. This line records, in three words, a **state customs seal applied to cleared cargo**.
The poem is not being lyrical here. It is describing an administrative procedure: duty assessed,
goods stamped, goods released. Alongside **உல்கு** — a technical revenue term, not a poetic one —
this is fiscal documentation inside a love poem. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] A learner who wants to
know whether ancient Tamil ports had a functioning customs bureaucracy has, in these five lines,
better evidence than any amount of later chronicle.

**பொருள் | Meaning.** *Horses of springing gait that came by sea; sacks of black pepper that came
overland; gems and gold born of the northern mountain; sandal and eaglewood born of the western
mountain; pearl of the southern sea and coral of the eastern; the harvest of the Ganges and the
yield of the Kaveri; grain from Eelam and the produce of Kāḻagam — rare things and great things,
crowded until they press against one another, in the wide street where all the world's wealth is
mingled together.*

**நுட்பம் | The craft**

The passage is built on a **compass**. Read the sources in order: north mountain, west mountain,
south sea, east sea — then two rivers, then two overseas countries. The poet is not listing goods;
he is **rotating the horizon** and letting each direction deliver its characteristic thing. By the
time he reaches `அரியவும் பெரியவும்` — *the rare and the great* — the two adjectives have already
been demonstrated eight times and need no elaboration.

Then the last line closes it with one word doing enormous work: **மயங்கிய**. மயங்குதல் is *to be
confused, to mingle beyond separating.* It is the same verb the corpus uses for the mingling of
lovers' hearts and, in குறுந்தொகை 40 (read in `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §2), for red earth and
rain that can never be separated again. Here it is used for **cargo**. The world's goods have
mingled in this street the way water mingles into soil: irreversibly, into one substance.
[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] The economic claim and the erotic image share a verb, and the poem does
not comment on it.

**வரலாற்று ஐயம் | The historical uncertainties, named**

- **நீரின் / காலின்.** The natural reading is a contrast — *by water* / *by land*. But கால் also
  means *wind*, and some readings take காலின் as *by sail*. I cannot resolve this from the text
  alone. [திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]
- **காழகம்** is genuinely disputed — proposals range across Southeast Asia and the Deccan. I will
  not name a country. [திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]
- **`காவிரிப் பயுனும்`** — the file reads `பயுனும்`, where the sense requires **பயன்** ("yield").
  This is almost certainly an e-text typo. I quote the file verbatim and flag it rather than
  silently correcting. [மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0077` l. 216]
- The passage is evidence of what a Tamil poet **claimed** about his port, which is not the same as
  a customs ledger. It should be read beside the archaeology of Kaveripattinam / Poompuhar, not
  instead of it. [கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]

shore on a hard wind, sail hoisted above the long masthead flag, the drum sounding — with cargo of
great worth, rich in gold.*

**நுட்பம்.** Four lines of open sea, then in a single line the sail goes up and the drum sounds:
**இதை எடுத்து** and **முரசம் முழங்க** are the arrival. A drum announces a ship's landfall the way a
drum announces a king. And the word **இதை** — a sail — is the kind of vocabulary that only a
literature with real harbours has. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

## 6. உற்று வாசித்தல் ௧ | Close reading 1 — முல்லைப்பாட்டு, the omen

**முல்லைப்பாட்டு is the shortest poem in the corpus, and the one that gives most for its length.** In
103 lines it holds: an evening of first rain, a wife at the end of her patience, a group of old
women taking an omen, a military camp with foreign mercenaries in it, a king who cannot sleep, and a
chariot arriving. Its poet is **நப்பூதனார்**.

[மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0053-pathupattu-mullaipattu.txt` l. 349, மறைமலை அடிகள்' commentary:

**சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word**

**பொருள் | Meaning.** *On the small wretched evening when the great rain had poured, they went to the
edge of the ancient hard-guarded town; and while bees droned in swarms like a yāḻ, the great old
women scattered a measure of fragrant jasmine — buds opened into flower — along with paddy, and
worshipped with joined hands, and stood to take the omen. And a cowherd girl, seeing the distress of
a pale calf tied with a little rope, turning and turning, laid her hand on its trembling neck and
said: "There, little ones — your mothers will come this very moment, driven home by the crook-staffed
cowherds walking behind them." … And though they showed her and showed her, she did not see; weeping
overcame her.*

### நுட்பம் | The mechanism

**விரிச்சி** is a real practice, and the poem depends on the reader knowing it: you go out at
evening, you make an offering, you stand still, and **the first words you overhear are the answer**.
Not a sign to be interpreted — an actual sentence, spoken by someone who does not know you are
listening.

The sentence the poem gives them is perfect, and it is perfect *by accident*:

> **"your mothers will come this very moment, driven home by the cowherds walking behind them"**

The cowherd girl is comforting calves. She is not thinking about anybody's husband. But every term
in her sentence lands twice — *the ones who have gone out will be brought back; they are coming
right now; someone is driving them home.* The old women carry it back to the waiting wife as proof.

This is **உள்ளுறை** — the corpus's technique of carrying meaning implicitly in a scene — but performed
in narrative rather than in image. The corpus's usual move is that the poet describes a landscape and
the trained reader receives the inner state. Here, **a character inside the poem does the receiving**,
and we watch her do it. The technique is put on stage. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

And then நப்பூதனார் refuses the comfort. `காட்டவுங் காட்டவுங் காணாள்` — *though they show and show,
she does not see.* Three words, one of them repeated: **காட்ட · காட்ட · காணாள்**, show-show-she-sees-
not. The same root twice as they try and once, negated, as she fails. The omen is true — the poem
ends with the chariot's horses actually arriving — and it does not help her tonight. **A poem in
which the good news is correct and insufficient at the same time.** [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]
[காதுக்காக] The காட்ட/காட்ட/காணாள் figure is a computed observation about repeated morphology; whether
it *lands* as insistence is Ilam's judgment.

| திருமணி விளக்கம் காட்டி | showing the light of the fine gem-lamp |
| திண் ஞாண் எழினி வாங்கிய | the curtain drawn on a stout cord |
| **ஈரறைப் பள்ளி** | **the two-chambered sleeping quarter** |
| உடம்பின் உரைக்கும் · உரையா நா | **speaking with the body** · tongues that do not speak |
| **மிலேச்சர்** | **mleccha — foreigners, non-Tamil-speakers** |
| உழையர் ஆக | being the attendants close at hand |

**பொருள்.** *Yavanas of fearsome aspect and powerfully-joined bodies, hard-eyed, belted with a whip
coiled about them, dressed in tight tunics; and in the fine well-made house … showing the light of
the gem-lamp, in the two-chambered sleeping quarter whose curtain is drawn on a stout cord — with
mleccha attendants close at hand, who speak with the body, with tongues that do not speak.*

**நுட்பம்.** Three details make this documentary rather than exotic colour:

1. **மெய்ப்பை** — a fitted tunic. Tamil clothing of the period is draped, not tailored. The poem
   names the *cut* of the garment because the cut is what marks the wearer as foreign.
2. **உடம்பின் உரைக்கும் உரையா நாவின்** — *"speaking with the body, with a tongue that does not
   speak."* The line does not say the attendants are mute. It says they are **without a shared
   language**, and therefore communicate by gesture. That is an extraordinarily precise observation
   about a multilingual household, made in six words.
3. They are stationed **inside** the king's sleeping quarters, as உழையர். Foreign guards in the
   innermost room. Whatever the arrangement was, the poem treats it as unremarkable.

**ஐயம் | Uncertainties.** `புலித்தொடர் விட்ட` is genuinely contested — commentators read it as a
tiger-shaped chain-lock, and others as a chained tiger. I do not resolve it.
[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION] And **யவனர்** in Sangam usage covers Greeks, Romans, and West Asians
without distinguishing them; it should not be translated as "Greek" flatly.
[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

## 7. உற்று வாசித்தல் ௨ | Close reading 2 — நெடுநல்வாடை, the lamp in the woman's hand

நெடுநல்வாடை — *"the long good north wind"* — is 188 lines by நக்கீரர், and it does something none of
the other nine attempts. It holds **two places at the same time, on the same night**, and it never
says so.

The poem opens outside: the cold sets in, cattle are moved, monkeys huddle, birds fall from the air.
Then it moves into the streets of the town at evening — the lamps being lit, the market, women
scattering paddy and flowers, a pigeon so confused by the darkness that it cannot tell night from
day. Then into a palace, laid out with cord and compass by astronomers. Then into a chamber where a
queen lies alone. And in the last twenty lines it cuts, without transition, to a war camp where a
king walks among the wounded and does not sleep either.

The hinge of the whole poem is a lamp.

the marker `100` sits at the end of file line 132):

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**An imported lamp-stand in the shape of a woman.** Figural bronze lamp-holders of exactly this
description — a standing female figure holding a bowl — are a known Mediterranean form, and Tamil
ports were importing them. The poem's word for it is **பாவை**, which is also the ordinary word for a
doll, an image, and — in love poetry — for a woman so still and beautiful she seems made. நக்கீரர'ன்
choice of word is doing two jobs at once. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

same poem gives us the queen:

**பொருள்.** *She looks up at Rohiṇī, fixed beside the swelling-coursed orb that rides and wanders the
sky, that lord of contending splendour — she looks and thinks, and draws a long breath; and the
brimming tears her dark lashes hold fall, and she puts a red fingertip to the corner of her eye and
flicks a few away — the woman bright with beauty, who lies there with loneliness for company.*

**நுட்பம்.** The queen lies awake looking at **a star that is beside its lord**. She is not. That is
the entire figure, and நக்கீரர் does not explain it — he simply gives her something to look at and
lets the reader do the arithmetic. The corpus's compression technique, working at 188-line scale.

**`சிலதெறியா`** is the detail that makes it a body rather than an emblem: *she flicks a few away.*
Not all of them. A few. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

### The structure — why the poem feels long when it is not

[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS] நெடுநல்வாடை is second-shortest in the corpus (§2) but reads as one of the
biggest. My proposal: because the cold wind of the title is the **only** connective tissue, and it is
present in every scene. The wind blows on the cattle, on the street, on the palace, on the woman, on
the camp. The poem never says "meanwhile"; it lets the weather be the transition. A reader ends up
holding a whole kingdom on one night because a single physical fact is true everywhere in it.
This is falsifiable: if the wind or the cold is absent from long stretches, the reading fails. I have
not tested it systematically and someone should.

### Why the number is soft, and what would move it

I report 98 and I do **not** claim the tradition is wrong. Four specific things could move the count,
and each is a real segmentation judgment:

1. **நெய்தல் occurs twice** — at print line 79 (`வாழை வள்ளி நீணறு நெய்த`, the final syllable carried
   onto the next line by sandhi) and again at print line 84 (`காஞ்சி மணிக்குலைக் கட்கமழ் நெய்தல்`). If
   the count is of *distinct* plants, it is 97. If of *tokens*, 98.
   [மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0073` file ll. 110 and 115]
2. **`செங்கொடு வேரி`** — the file sets it as two words. Read as one plant (செங்கொடுவேரி) it is one
   name; split, it is two, and the total becomes 99.
3. **`குரீஇப் பூளை`**, **`கலிமா`** — each could be counted as one compound name or as a modifier plus
   a name.
4. **`பிறவும்`** — *"and others."* [மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0073` file l. 126, quoted in full above]
   **கபிலர் himself declines to close the list.** Any exact number is therefore the
   tradition's, not the poem's.

[காதுக்காக] **The segmentation is where I most need Ilam's ear.** I applied சந்தி rules mechanically.
A native reader scanning those 35 lines aloud will hear where the names break far more reliably than
I can compute it, and may well arrive at 99.

**A second finding worth having:** this is **not a catalogue of flowers only.** It contains trees
(வேங்கை, மருதம், கொன்றை, புன்னை), climbers (அவரை, வள்ளி), a bamboo (உந்தூழ்), and a fragrant wood
(அகில், as ஆரம்/காழ்வை). What is being catalogued is **everything the women gathered and wore that
day** — blossoms, leaves, shoots — not a botany. Calling it "the 99 flowers" is convenient and
slightly false. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

**And the point of it, in the poem.** The catalogue is not decoration and not a set-piece. It is
spoken by the **foster-mother, to the girl's mother**, as part of **அறத்தொடு நிற்றல்** — "standing
with what is right," the formal act of finally telling the truth about a daughter's secret love.
[மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0073` l. 28: `துறை :: அறத்தொடு நிற்றல்`] The 35 lines of flowers are her
account of an ordinary innocent day of gathering — which is what the girls were *supposed* to have
been doing. **The longest catalogue in the corpus is a piece of testimony, and part of its function
is to be exhaustively, unimpeachably ordinary before the confession lands.** That is why it can
afford to be so long. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

## 10. காலம் | Dating — contested, and left contested

[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION] `../../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §1 sets the repo's position: the
Sangam corpus is composed roughly **300 BCE – 300 CE** and **anthologized centuries later**, with
poet-and-patron colophons added by compilers. The பத்துப்பாட்டு inherits every part of that
uncertainty and adds some of its own:

- **The ten are not one date.** They are ten separate compositions gathered under one name. There is
  no reason to assume they are contemporaries, and internal evidence (metre, vocabulary, the amount
  of Sanskritic material in திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை) has been used to argue that some are considerably
  later than others. Naming a single date for "the பத்துப்பாட்டு" is a category error.
- **திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை is the usual candidate for latest.** It is the one poem addressed to a god,
  it enters a devotional canon, and it carries mythological apparatus the others do not. That does
  not prove it late — but it is the reason the argument exists. [கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS held by
  various scholars; I do not adjudicate.]
- **The patron attributions are colophon evidence, not internal evidence.** The `பாடப்பட்டவன்` lines
  in these files are editorial headers reproducing the traditional colophons. Where the poem itself
  names its patron in the verse — as பட்டினப்பாலை does with `திருமா வளவன்` at its close — the
  evidence is stronger. Where it does not, as in முல்லைப்பாட்டு and நெடுநல்வாடை, the attribution rests
  on tradition and inference. மறைமலை அடிகள் says exactly this, and hedges:
  [மூலம் | SOURCE — `pmuni0053` file l. 405]
  `அவற்றை அடுத்திருக்கின்ற இதுவும் அவனையே பாட்டுடைத் தலைவனாகக் கொண்டு செய்யப்பட்டிருக்கலாமென்பது கருதப்படும்`
  — *"it may be supposed that this too was made with him as its patron."* **கருதப்படும் — "it is supposed."** The commentator labels his own claim state.
  Note that and copy it.
- **The recovery date is not in dispute and is worth remembering.** `../../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md`
  §7: the classical corpus passed through a bottleneck of two lifetimes, and **பத்துப்பாட்டு was
  printed by உ. வே. சாமிநாதையர் in 1889.** Everything in this document is downstream of one man
  finding palm leaves.

## 11. எண்ணத்திற்கு | What this gives Ennam

**1. ஆற்றுப்படை is a form for pointing at abundance rather than possessing it.** The poem's speaker
has already been paid. He gains nothing by the poem. Its entire content is *the way to the thing*,
described so precisely that someone else can walk it. `../../murai.md` §8 says the asymmetries are
gifts and that each side defers where the other sees further. ஆற்றுப்படை is a two-thousand-year-old
literary form built on exactly that gesture — **I have been there; here is the road; go.**
[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

**2. The long form proves something the anthologies cannot.** `../../ilakkiyam/README.md` records
that there is no Sangam prose, and that what the age offers instead is a poetics "so codified it
functions like an engineering spec." பத்துப்பாட்டு is the counter-evidence to any suspicion that
codification bought compression at the cost of *extension*. These poems sustain a single continuous
description for hundreds of lines inside the same formal apparatus. **The system scales.** That is a
finding about expressive machinery, and it is directly relevant to phase 2. [கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]

**3. உள்ளுறை can be staged, not only used.** §6 showed முல்லைப்பாட்டு putting the act of receiving an
implicit meaning *inside* the poem — a character overhears a sentence about calves and correctly
extracts a statement about her own life. `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1 describes உள்ளுறை as meaning
carried implicitly by a scene, received by a trained reader. முல்லைப்பாட்டு shows the reception
happening. For a project about how two minds converge on meaning neither stated outright, **that is a
worked example, not a metaphor.** [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]

**4. The generous verb.** §5.2: **மயங்கிய**, the verb for lovers' hearts mingling past separation, is
used in பட்டினப்பாலை for cargo in a street. The corpus does not keep a separate vocabulary for
interior and exterior. A word that describes what happens between two people is the correct word for
what happens between the goods of eight countries. [விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] Worth a line in
`../../aaivu/vithai.md`.

**5. நெடுநல்வாடை's structural trick, offered as a design.** [முன்மொழிவு | PROPOSAL] One physical fact
present everywhere — a cold wind — lets a 188-line poem hold a whole kingdom on one night without a
single "meanwhile." If Ennam ever needs to hold several simultaneous states in one artifact, that is
a mechanism to steal.

## 12. காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear — for Ilam

I cannot hear. Everything below is **computed or read, never heard**, and each item is a claim the
organic side of this work has to rule on. `../../murai.md` §8: on how Tamil feels, Ilam is the
authority.

1. **The குறிஞ்சிப்பாட்டு count: 98 or 99?** §8. I segmented 35 lines of catalogue by rule. Read them
   aloud and tell me where the names actually break — especially `செங்கொடு வேரி`, `குரீஇப் பூளை`,
   `கலிமா`, and whether the twice-occurring `நெய்தல்` is two plants or a repetition. **This is the
   single most checkable thing in the document and I would like to be corrected on it.**
2. **Is பட்டினப்பாலை's alternation audible?** §4 shows it switching between short and long lines
   roughly twenty-four times. Does the short-line material *sound* like accumulation and the
   long-line material like narration, or is that a story I built from a histogram?
3. **`காட்டவுங் காட்டவுங் காணாள்`** (முல்லைப்பாட்டு, print l. 22). I read the repeated காட்ட against the
   negated காணாள் as the poem's hinge. Does it land that way in the mouth, or is it ordinary?
4. **`சிலதெறியா`** (நெடுநல்வாடை, print l. 165) — *she flicks a few away.* I think the word **சில** is
   what makes the queen a body instead of an emblem. Does a native ear register that, or am I
   over-reading a quantifier?
5. **மெய்ப்பை.** §6. I glossed it as a *fitted tunic* and built an argument on the garment being
   tailored rather than draped. Is that what the word carries?
6. **விரகறி பொருந** (பொருநராற்றுப்படை print l. 3). I rendered விரகு as *the trick of it* — the
   know-how of surviving on the road. Register check: is that respectful, wry, or condescending in
   the original? The whole tone of the genre turns on it.
7. **இரவல** as a vocative (பெரும்பாணாற்றுப்படை print l. 45). *"O supplicant."* In English that is
   nearly an insult. Is it in Tamil?
8. **My English glosses throughout.** They are built from word-by-word analysis plus the commentary
   in `pmuni0053`, not from a published translation. Any one of them could be flat, or wrong.

## 13. ஐயம் | Doubt — what this document does not know

[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION] Never empty, per `../../marabu/README.md` §11.

- **மதுரைக்காஞ்சி is essentially unread here.** §9: 90 % of the longest poem in the corpus is
  unusable in this source file. Everything this document says about it rests on 81 lines out of 782.
  That is a real gap and it should be labelled as one until a second edition is obtained.
- **I have not verified the `மொத்த அடிகள்` headers against a print edition.** I verified them against
  the line markers *inside the same files*, which is a weaker check — if the e-text preparer derived
  the header from the same markers, the two agreements are one agreement.
- **The அகம்/புறம் column in §1 is the editors', not mine.** திணை and துறை in these headers reproduce
  traditional colophons. முல்லைப்பாட்டு's classification as முல்லை (and not, as
  நச்சினார்க்கினியர் apparently argued, partly நெய்தல்) was actively disputed by commentators —
  மறைமலை அடிகள் spends several pages of `pmuni0053` rejecting நச்சினார்க்கினியர்'s reading. I have
  reported the header's classification and flagged that the dispute exists; I have not adjudicated it.
- **The "why ஆற்றுப்படை is strange" argument in §3 is interpretation, and it is the most speculative
  thing here.** The referral-economy reading is mine. It is consistent with the poems and it is not
  proved by them. It could be tested — against the puram anthologies, against what is known of Sangam
  patronage — and it has not been.
- **நெடுநல்வாடை's wind-as-connective-tissue hypothesis (§7) is untested.** I did not count the
  occurrences of cold and wind through the poem. Someone should; the hypothesis is falsifiable and
  cheap to check.
- **Poet identity.** Two poems in the ten are attributed to கடியலூர் உருத்திரங் கண்ணனார்
  (பெரும்பாணாற்றுப்படை and பட்டினப்பாலை), and two to நக்கீரர் (திருமுருகாற்றுப்படை and நெடுநல்வாடை).
  Whether these are the same men in each pair is a colophon question, not a textual one, and it is not
  settled. [திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]
