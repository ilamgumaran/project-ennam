## 1. இடம் | Where this text stands

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Everything in `../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` is a *poem*: a single lyric moment, thirty words wide, spoken by an unnamed woman or an unnamed man, complete in itself. Nothing in the Sangam anthologies continues. There is no yesterday and no tomorrow inside a குறுந்தொகை poem — that is precisely its power, and precisely its ceiling.

சிலப்பதிகாரம் is what happens when Tamil decides to make a poem that *continues*. Thirty cantos, three cities, a marriage, a betrayal, a migration, a judicial murder, a burning, an apotheosis, and a war of memorial. It is the first Tamil work in which something **happens over time to named people**.

That sentence contains the two breaks at once, and they are the same break:

1. **Time** — the akam lyric abolishes sequence; the epic requires it.
2. **Names** — the akam convention forbids naming the lovers (see `../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1: "by convention akam poems name no lovers"). The epic's second canto-group cannot function without names.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** These are not two innovations but one. A poem that must continue must be able to say *the same person again*, and the device for saying "the same person again" is a proper name. Naming is the minimum technology of narrative. If this is right, சிலப்பதிகாரம் did not break the akam rule out of taste or foreign influence — it broke it because it was doing something the rule made structurally impossible. **What would count against it:** evidence of a Tamil narrative of comparable length that sustains sequence *without* proper names, or evidence that the naming in சிலப்பதிகாரம் is an artefact of the (possibly later) பதிகம் rather than of the body of the poem. The second is testable and §5 below reports on it.

The epic is bottom-heavy in exactly the place a modern editor would also put the weight.

## 3. மூன்று நகரம், மூன்று மணிமுடி | Three cities, three crowns

**[மூலம்]** புகார் is the Chola port. மதுரை is the Pandya capital. வஞ்சி is the Chera capital. The epic's own closing கட்டுரை opens by placing itself among the three crowned kings — F3:1546–1548:

> முடியுடை வேந்தர் மூவ ருள்ளும்
> குடதிசை யாளுங் கொற்றங் குன்றா
> ஆர மார்பிற் சேரர்குலத் துதித்தோர்

**[விளக்கம்]** *Among the three crowned kings, those born of the Chera line — whose sovereignty over the western quarter does not diminish, garland on chest…*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This is the structural claim that makes சிலப்பதிகாரம் more than a story. The three-book division is not geography for its own sake. It is a **survey of Tamil sovereignty conducted by moving a single innocent woman through all three kingdoms and recording what each does to her:**

| காண்டம் | Kingdom | What the state does |
|---|---|---|
| புகார் | சோழன் | **Nothing.** The state is simply absent while a marriage is destroyed by a private appetite. Prosperity without attention. |
| மதுரை | பாண்டியன் | **Kills the wrong man on a goldsmith's word, without inquiry.** The state is present, powerful, and catastrophically wrong. |
| வஞ்சி | சேரன் | **Marches an army north to fetch a stone for her memorial.** The state is competent, devout — and arrives entirely too late to be of use to her. |

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** The three-city plan is a **comparative constitutional argument**, not a travelogue: the epic shows a Tamil polity failing in three distinct modes — inattention, error, and belatedness — and the woman is the instrument that exposes each. The fact that the author (by tradition a Chera) gives the Chera the honourable book and *still* makes that book an aftermath rather than a rescue is the hypothesis's strongest internal support. **What would count against it:** a reading in which வஞ்சி is genuinely restorative rather than commemorative — but the last canto has the surviving young woman renounce the world, which is hard to read as restoration.

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Is the three-kingdom sweep the *reason* for the three-book structure, or is it a consequence of a pre-existing கண்ணகி cult whose shrines happened to be distributed across all three regions? The text as we have it cannot settle this on its own.

## 4. கதை | The story

### தமிழில்

**[விளக்கம் — this Tamil prose is mine, generated, not quoted from any source. It needs the ear.]**

புகார் நகரத்தில் மாசாத்துவான் என்னும் வணிகனின் மகன் கோவலன். மாநாய்கன் என்னும் வணிகனின் மகள் கண்ணகி. இருவரும் மணம் புரிந்து இல்லறம் நடத்தினர்.

ஒரு நாள் மாதவி என்னும் நாடகக் கணிகை அரங்கேறினாள். அரசன் அவளுக்குத் தலைக்கோலும் பொன்னும் மாலையும் அளித்தான். அந்த மாலையை விலைக்கு வாங்கியவன் கோவலன். அன்று முதல் அவன் மாதவியோடு தங்கினான்; தன் மனையை மறந்தான்; பொருளையும் இழந்தான்.

கடற்கரையில் ஒரு நாள் இருவரும் யாழ் வாசித்துப் பாடினர். பாடலின் பொருளில் ஐயம் வந்தது. கோவலன் பிரிந்தான்.

வறியவனாய்த் திரும்பிய கோவலனுக்குக் கண்ணகி ஒரு சொல்லும் கூறவில்லை. தன் காலில் இருந்த சிலம்பு ஒன்றைக் கொடுத்து, "இதை விற்று மீண்டும் தொடங்குக" என்றாள். இருவரும் கவுந்தி அடிகளோடு மதுரைக்குக் காடு கடந்து நடந்தனர்.

மதுரையில் கோவலன் சிலம்பை விற்கச் சென்றான். பாண்டியன் மனைவியின் சிலம்பு அப்போது திருடப்பட்டிருந்தது. பொற்கொல்லன் கோவலனையே திருடன் என்று அரசனிடம் சொன்னான். அரசன் ஆராயவில்லை. காவலரை அனுப்பினான். கல்லாத ஒருவன் வாளால் கோவலனைக் கொன்றான்.

கண்ணகி அரண்மனைக்குச் சென்றாள். "தேரா மன்னா" — ஆராயாத அரசனே — என்று அழைத்தாள். "என் சிலம்பினுள் மணி; உன் தேவியின் சிலம்பினுள் முத்து" என்றாள். சிலம்பை உடைத்தாள். மணி தெறித்து அரசன் வாயில் விழுந்தது. "யானோ அரசன்? யானே கள்வன்" என்று சொல்லி அரசன் வீழ்ந்து இறந்தான். தேவியும் இறந்தாள்.

கண்ணகி அத்துடன் நிற்கவில்லை. மதுரையை மூன்று முறை வலம் வந்து, இடது முலையைத் திருகி எறிந்தாள். நகரம் எரிந்தது.

பின்பு அவள் சேர நாட்டு மலைக்குச் சென்றாள்; அங்கே குன்றவர் அவளைத் தெய்வமாகக் கொண்டனர். சேரன் செங்குட்டுவன் செய்தி கேட்டு, இமயத்திற்குப் படையெடுத்து, அவளுக்கு நடுகல் கொணர்ந்து, கங்கையில் நீராட்டி, வஞ்சியில் கோட்டம் எழுப்பினான். மாதவியின் மகள் மணிமேகலை துறவு பூண்டாள்.

### In English

**[விளக்கம்]** In புகார், the Chola port, two merchant families marry their children: **கோவலன்**, sixteen, and **கண்ணகி**, twelve. **[மூலம்]** The ages are the text's own: கண்ணகி is *ஈராறு ஆண்டு அகவையாள்* (F1:142) — "of twice-six years" — and கோவலன் *ஈரெட்டு ஆண்டு அகவையான்* (F1:152), "of twice-eight years."

A courtesan-dancer, **மாதவி**, makes her stage debut. She is awarded the தலைக்கோல் and a garland priced at 1,008 *kazhanju* of gold (**[மூலம்]** F1:451, F1:453). Whoever buys the garland buys the woman's company. கோவலன் buys it, moves in with her, and forgets his house — **[மூலம்]** F1:465: *வடுநீங்கு சிறப்பின்தன் மனையகம் மறந்துஎன்.*

Years later, on the shore at the sea-festival, they take turns at the yāḻ and sing (canto 7, கானல் வரி). Each hears in the other's song an address to somebody else. கோவலன் walks out. It is worth being exact about this: **the marriage-wrecking affair does not end in a betrayal but in a misreading of a poem.**

He returns to கண்ணகி with nothing. She does not reproach him. She offers one of her two gold anklets — the சிலம்பு that gives the epic its name — as capital to start again. With the Jain ascetic **கவுந்தி அடிகள்**், they walk across the wasteland to மதுரை.

There, கோவலன் takes the anklet to market. The Pandya queen's anklet has recently been stolen. The court goldsmith — the actual thief — identifies கோவலன் as the culprit. The king, in the middle of a domestic errand, does not investigate. He orders the thief killed and the anklet brought. A guard described by the text as *கல்லாக் களிமக னொருவன்* — an unschooled, intoxicated man (**[மூலம்]** F2:1301) — cuts him down in the street.

கண்ணகி comes to the palace with the surviving anklet in her hand. She does not plead. She conducts a forensic demonstration: the queen's anklets contain pearls; hers contain gems. She breaks the anklet open in the court. A gem flies out and strikes the king in the mouth. He says the sentence the epic is built to reach — **[மூலம்]** F2:1700: *யானோ அரசன் யானே கள்வன்* — "Am **I** the king? *I* am the thief" — and falls dead. The queen dies beside him.

**The epic does not stop there, and this is its moral centre.** The king's death does not satisfy her. She goes out into மதுரை, circles it three times, tears off her left breast, throws it into the street, and the city burns (canto 21–22). The fire is *directed*, not indiscriminate: she instructs it to spare brahmins, the virtuous, cattle, chaste women, elders and children, and to go only to the side of the wicked (**[மூலம்]** F2:1773–1774).

She walks west into the Chera hills. The hill people see her taken up and decide she is their goddess (canto 24). The Chera king **செங்குட்டுவன்** hears the story, marches north, defeats northern kings, makes them carry a stone from the Himalaya on their heads, bathes it in the Ganges, and installs her as a deity at வஞ்சி. மாதவி's daughter **மணிமேகலை** renounces the world — and the epic's last words hand her to the sequel.

### ஏன் இது சுத்தப்படுத்தப்படக் கூடாது | Why this must not be sanitised

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Retellings soften this story in three specific places, and each softening destroys an argument the poem is making.

1. **கண்ணகி is not a passive sufferer.** She is a litigant who wins on evidence, and then an arsonist who is not appeased by winning. The epic gives her a courtroom victory in canto 20 and then spends canto 21 having her refuse it. **[மூலம்]** F2:1757: *ஒட்டே னரசோ டொழிப்பேன் மதுரையுமென்* — "I will not consent; I will destroy Madurai along with the king."
2. **The state is the defendant, not the villain.** There is no wicked king in this poem. The Pandya is pious, competent, and beloved; his failure is *procedural* — he did not inquire. கண்ணகி's word for him is not "tyrant" but **தேரா மன்னா**, "king who did not investigate" (F2:1675). That is a far more dangerous critique than tyranny, because it indicts a *good* king's ordinary administration.
3. **கோவலன is not exonerated.** The text does not rehabilitate him. He wastes his wife's dowry on another woman, and he dies for a crime he did not commit while carrying property he did not earn. The epic's word for what kills him is ஊழ்வினை, ripened past action.

## 5. பதிகம் | The stated thesis

**[மூலம்]** This is the passage every Tamil reader is expected to know. F1:78–83, quoted exactly:

> அரைசியல் பிழைத்தோர்க்கு அறம்கூற்று ஆவதூஉம்
> உரைசால் பத்தினிக்கு உயர்ந்தோர் ஏத்தலும்
> ஊழ்வினை உருத்துவந்து ஊட்டும் என்பதூஉம்
> சூழ்வினைச் சிலம்பு காரண மாகச்
> சிலப்பதி காரம் என்னும் பெயரால்
> நாட்டுதும் யாம்ஓர் பாட்டுடைச் செய்யுள்என,

### சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word

### பொருள் | Meaning

**[விளக்கம்]** *That to those who fail in statecraft, dharma becomes Death; that the exalted praise the wife of full-told fame; and that ripened deed comes on in wrath and feeds you its fruit — with the contriving anklet as the cause, under the name "The Matter of the Anklet," we shall establish a composition possessed of song.*

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Four things worth stopping on.

**First — this is a stated thesis, and Sangam poetry does not have those.** A புறநானூறு poem *enacts* a value; it does not announce what it will prove. Here the author states three propositions, names the book after the evidence, and then spends thirty cantos demonstrating them. That is an argumentative structure, and it is new in Tamil.

**Second — கூற்று is Death, not "justice."** The line is usually rendered "dharma becomes the judge of those who fail in statecraft." It says something harder: dharma becomes **Yama** to them. Not a verdict — an executioner. The Pandya king does not lose a case. He dies mid-sentence.

**Third — the title is a legal noun.** அதிகாரம் in this compound is not "chapter" in the modern sense but the *matter*, the *case*, the *cause under consideration*. சிலப்பதிகாரம் is closest in force to **"The Anklet Case."** A Tamil epic is named after a piece of evidence.

**Fourth — the three propositions are not equal.** They belong to three different systems: the first is **political** (statecraft has a lethal accountability), the second is **social** (chastity earns worship), the third is **karmic** (past deeds ripen). **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Whether these three cohere or merely coexist is genuinely unresolved, and it is the fault line of all modern argument about this text. The karmic proposition partly *excuses* the king — his ruin was ripening anyway — which sits badly with the political proposition that he is accountable. The text does not reconcile them. Readers who want a clean political epic quietly drop proposition three; readers who want a devotional epic quietly drop proposition one. **Do not smuggle in a reconciliation.**

### The naming question, tested

**[கருதுகோள்]** §1 raised the worry that the naming might be an artefact of a later பதிகம். **[மூலம் — result]** It is not. The body of canto 1 names both principals in the same formula, inside the narrative proper:

> காதலாள் பெயர்மன்னும் கண்ணகிஎன் பாள்மன்னோ, — F1:147

> கொண்டுஏத்தும் கிழமையான் கோவலன்என் பான்மன்னோ. — F1:157

**[விளக்கம்]** *the beloved's name, indeed, is she who is called கண்ணகி* / *…the one held in right and praise, he who is called கோவலன்.* **[முடிவு | CONCLUSION — narrow]** The naming is structural to the poem, not an editorial frame around it. The akam prohibition is broken in the body of the work, deliberately, and the poem draws attention to the act by using the verb *என்பாள் / என்பான்* — "she who is *called*."

## 6. புதுமை | What is formally new, against Sangam poetry

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Six departures, with the evidence for each.

### 6.1 Sequence

Sangam lyric has no "then." சிலப்பதிகாரம் has thirty cantos of "then." **[மூலம்]** The epic's own வாழ்த்துக் காதை (F3, canto 29) contains a single long prose-verse paragraph that recapitulates the entire plot from Kannaki's birth to the present moment — a device that presupposes a reader who has been tracking a sequence and may need it re-gathered. Sangam poetry has no use for such a device because it has nothing to re-gather.

### 6.2 Names, and the breaking of the akam rule

Covered in §5. Add this: the epic names not only the principals but **மாதவி, மணிமேகலை, கவுந்தி (F2:223 *கவுந்தி யடிகளும்*), மாசாத்து வான் (F1:151), மாநாய்கன் (F1:141), ஐயை, தேவந்தி, காவற்பெண்டு, செங்குட்டுவன்** — including *minor women*, including *servants*. **[மூலம்]** Canto 29 labels its speeches by speaker name: *தேவந்தி சொல்* (F3:1191), *காவற்பெண்டு சொல்* (F3:1197), *அடித் தோழி சொல்* (F3:1203), *செங்குட்டுவன் கூற்று* (F3:1232). This is not merely naming; it is **dramatis personae notation**.

### 6.3 Mixed metres in one work

**[மூலம்]** File 1 preserves the editorial metre-headings; they are worth listing because a single work using this many forms is itself the innovation:

- **இணைக்குறள் ஆசிரியப்பா** (F1:23, the பதிகம்)
- **சிந்தியல் வெண்பாக்கள்** (F1:117)
- **மயங்கிசைக் கொச்சகக் கலிப்பா** (F1:130)
- **நிலைமண்டில ஆசிரியப்பா** (F1:191 and passim — the narrative default)
- **வெண்பா** (F1:282 and passim — canto-closing)
- **கலி வெண்பா** (F1:1443)
- **கட்டுரை** (F1:989 — a non-metrical or freely-metrical framing register)

**[விளக்கம்]** The working pattern: **ஆசிரியப்பா carries narration; வெண்பா closes a canto with a verdict; கலிப்பா and வரி carry song; கட்டுரை carries framing and speech.** That is a *functional* division of metre — form assigned by narrative job. Sangam anthologies are largely single-metre affairs (ஆசிரியப்பா for அகம், with the poem's job constant throughout).

### 6.4 Embedded song: the வரி system

**[மூலம்]** Cantos 7 and 8 do not merely contain songs; they contain **named song-genres**, labelled in the text. From F1:

| Canto 7 (கானல் வரி) | Canto 8 (வேனில் காதை) |
|---|---|
| ஆற்று வரி (F1:1010) | ஆற்று வரி (F1:1153) |
| சார்த்து வரி - முகச்சார்த்து (F1:1035) | சார்த்து வரி (F1:1178) |
| முகம் இல் வரி (F1:1060) | திணை நிலைவரி (F1:1191) |
| கானல் வரி (F1:1068) | மயங்கு திணை நிலைவரி (F1:1216) |
| நிலைவரி (F1:1083) | சாயல் வரி (F1:1254) |
| முரிவரி (F1:1096) | முகம் இல் வரி (F1:1267) |
| திணை நிலைவரி (F1:1109) | காடுரை (F1:1272) |

**[விளக்கம்]** That is at least **ten distinct named lyric sub-genres** deployed inside two cantos. Note especially **மயங்கு திணை நிலைவரி** — a "mixed-tiṇai" song. The ஐந்திணை system of `../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1 is here being used not as a compositional constraint but as **material to be blended**. The epic treats the Sangam's foundational grammar as a palette.

### 6.5 A stated moral thesis

Covered in §5. This is the single largest break: an announced argument, before the narrative, that the narrative will demonstrate.

### 6.6 The work claims to be a total compendium of Tamil

**[மூலம்]** The closing **நூற் கட்டுரை**, F3:1562–1571:

> குமரி வேங்கடங் குணகுட கடலா
> மண்டினி மருங்கிற் றண்டமிழ் வரைப்பிற்
> செந்தமிழ் கொடுந்தமி ழென்றிரு பகுதியின்
> ஐந்திணை மருங்கின் அறம்பொரு ளின்பம்
> மக்கள் தேவ ரெனவிரு சார்க்கும்
> ஒத்த மரபின் ஒழுக்கொடு புணர
> எழுத்தொடு புணர்ந்தசொல் லகத்தெழு பொருளை
> இழுக்கா யாப்பின் அகனும் புறனும்
> அவற்று வழிப்படூஉஞ் செவ்விசிறந் தோங்கிய
> பாடலும் எழாலும் பண்ணும் பாணியும்

**[விளக்கம்]** *From Kumari to Venkatam, the eastern and western seas — within the bounds of cool Tamil, in the two divisions called செந்தமிழ் and கொடுந்தமிழ்; அறம், பொருள், இன்பம** within the five landscapes; for both classes, humans and gods, joined with conduct in a shared tradition; the meaning that rises within the word joined to the letter, in unfaltering prosody, akam and puram, and rising excellent along those paths — song, and the yāḻ, and paṇ, and rhythm…*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This is astonishing as a self-description. The poem claims to have covered: the geography of Tamil, both its dialect divisions, all five திணை, all three புருஷார்த்தம், both human and divine registers, all five levels of grammar (எழுத்து, சொல், பொருள், யாப்பு, and by implication அணி), both அகம் and புறம், and the full apparatus of music and dance. **சிலப்பதிகாரம் declares itself not a story but an inventory of everything Tamil could do.** And the very last clause hands the reader onward — F3:1578: *மணிமே கலைமேல் உரைப்பொருள் முற்றிய* — "the matter to be told being completed upon Maṇimēkalai." **[மூலம்]** The twin-epic link is attested in the text itself, not merely by tradition.

## 7. இசையும் ஆடலும் | The performing arts

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This is the claim to make loudly: **சிலப்பதிகாரம் is the richest surviving ancient source on Tamil performing arts, and it is not close.** Tolkāppiyam gives prosody. The Sangam anthologies mention the yāḻ and the paṇ in passing. சிலப்பதிகாரம் gives *specifications*: stage dimensions in stated units, instrument parts by name, an eight-step tuning protocol, eight plucking techniques, a seven-note scale, seven modal பாலை, curtain types, and a dance troupe's division of labour.

### 7.1 அரங்கேற்று காதை — the stage, measured

**[மூலம்]** F1:388–391, F1:394–395 — the debut theatre is given as a construction spec:

> நூல்நெறி மரபின் அரங்கம் அளக்கும்
> கோல்அளவு இருபத்து நால்விரல் ஆக
> எழுகோல் அகலத்து எண்கோல் நீளத்து
> ஒருகோல் உயரத்து உறுப்பினது ஆகி

**[விளக்கம்]** *The measuring rod for the stage, by the tradition of the treatises, being twenty-four fingers; [the stage] having as its members seven rods in breadth, eight rods in length, one rod in height…*

**[விளக்கம்]** And then, two lines later, **two doors** (*ஏற்ற வாயில் இரண்டுடன் பொலிய*, F1:394) and — F1:396–397 — pillar-shadow lamps set so the light falls clear of the pillars.

**[மூலம்]** Three curtain types are named at F1:398–399: **ஒருமுக எழினி**, **பொருமுக எழினி**, **கரந்துவரல் எழினி**.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** A single-face curtain, an opposed-face curtain, and a *concealed-arrival* curtain. The third is a stage machine for making a performer appear. **[கருதுகோள்]** A theatre with two entrances, three curtain mechanisms, controlled lighting, and a standardised measuring unit is not a village platform; it is a designed building with a professional practice behind it. **What would count against it:** if the passage is read as idealised prescription copied from a lost treatise rather than description of an existing house. The text's own phrase *நூல்நெறி மரபின்* ("by the tradition of the treatises") is honestly ambiguous on exactly this point and should not be pressed either way.

### 7.2 The dance vocabulary

**[மூலம்]** F1:299, 301–303, 305, 307:

> ஏழாண்டு இயற்றிஓர் ஈராறு ஆண்டில்
> …
> இருவகைக் கூத்தின் இலக்கணம் அறிந்து
> பலவகைக் கூத்தும் விலக்கினிற் புணர்த்துப்
> பதினோர் ஆடலும் பாட்டும் கொட்டும்
> …
> ஆடலும் பாடலும் பாணியும் தூக்கும்
> …
> பிண்டியும் பிணையலும் எழிற்கையும் தொழிற்கையும்

**[விளக்கம்]** *Having trained seven years, in her twelfth year… knowing the grammar of the two kinds of kūttu, joining the many kinds of kūttu according to the rules, knowing clearly by disciplined principle the eleven dances and the song and the drumming… dance and song and rhythm and measure… piṇḍi and piṇaiyal and eḻiṛkai and toḻiṛkai…*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The technical inventory here is dense enough to reconstruct a syllabus:

- **இருவகைக் கூத்தின்** — two classes of dance. Later at F1:339 the text names them: **வேத்தியல்** (courtly) and **பொதுவியல்** (common/public).
- **பதினோர் ஆடலும்** — the **eleven dances**, referred to again at F1:873 (*பதினோர் ஆடலும் பாட்டின் பகுதியும்*) and in the canto's closing வெண்பா, F1:466–467: *எண்ணும் எழுத்தும் இயல்ஐந்தும் பண்நான்கும் / பண்ணின்ற கூத்துப் பதினொன்றும்*.
- **பிண்டி / பிணையல்** — single-hand and interlaced-hand gesture classes; **எழிற்கை / தொழிற்கை** — "beauty-hand" (aesthetic gesture) versus "work-hand" (representational/narrative gesture). **[கருதுகோள்]** This is a distinction between *abstract* and *mimetic* gesture — the same axis that later Indian dance theory draws as nṛtta versus abhinaya. If so, the Tamil terms are independent and functional rather than borrowed. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Whether எழிற்கை/தொழிற்கை maps onto nṛtta/abhinaya or cuts the field differently is not settled by this text and I have not consulted the commentaries.

### 7.3 கானல் வரி — the yāḻ, in eight steps and eight strokes

**[மூலம்]** The opening of canto 7 is the finest technical passage in ancient Tamil on how a stringed instrument is prepared and played. F1:992–997:

> பத்தரும் கோடும் ஆணியும் நரம்பும்என்று
> இத்திறத்துக் குற்றம்நீங்கிய யாழ்கையில் தொழுதுவாங்கி
> பண்ணல் பரிவட்டணை ஆராய்தல் தைவரல்
> கண்ணிய செலவு விளையாட்டுக் கையூழ்
> நண்ணிய குறும்போக்கு என்று நாட்டிய
> எண்வகையால் இசைஎழீஇப்

**[விளக்கம்]** *Taking up reverently in his hand the yāḻ freed of flaw in these respects — the soundbox, the neck, the pegs, and the strings — he raised the music by the eight kinds established as: paṇṇal, parivaṭṭaṇai, ārāydal, taivaral, the considered celavu, viḷaiyāṭṭu, kaiyūḻ, and the attained kuṛumpōkku.*

Then, F1:1001–1004:

> வார்தல் வடித்தல் உந்தல் உறழ்தல்
> சீருடன் உருட்டல் தெருட்டல் அள்ளல்
> ஏர்உடைப் பட்டடைஎன இசையோர் வகுத்த
> எட்டு வகையின் இசைக்கர ணத்துப்

**[விளக்கம்]** *Drawing, refining, pushing, alternating, rolling with measure, clarifying, gathering, and the fine paṭṭaḍai — the eight kinds of sound-technique that the musicians have distinguished.*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Two separate ordered lists of eight: **first the tuning-and-warming protocol** (பண்ணல் → குறும்போக்கு), **then the right-hand stroke technique** (வார்தல் → பட்டடை). And before both, the instrument's four parts named as the things that must be free of fault: **பத்தர்** (resonator), **கோடு** (neck/arm), **ஆணி** (tuning pegs), **நரம்பு** (strings).

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** A poem that names an eight-step tuning sequence in fixed order is transmitting a **practitioner's protocol**, not decorating a scene. Poets do not invent ordered eightfold technical lists for atmosphere. **Prediction if right:** these same terms, in roughly this order, should recur in later Tamil music literature and in the commentarial tradition (Adiyarkkunallar). **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** I have not verified that here — no such source is in `_src/txt/`. This is the single most checkable open item in this document.

### 7.4 ஆய்ச்சியர் குரவை — the scale danced

**[மூலம்]** Canto 17. The old cowherd woman arranges seven girls in a ring and gives them names. F2:1365–1366:

> குடமுதல் இடமுறை யாக்குரல் துத்தம்
> கைக்கிளை உழைஇளி விளரி தாரமென

**[விளக்கம்]** *Beginning from the west and going leftward in order: குரல், துத்தம், கைக்கிளை, உழை, இளி, விளரி, தாரம்.*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** These are the **seven notes of the ancient Tamil scale**, and they are being used as the *positions of seven dancers in a circle*. The dance formation **is** the scale. A girl named குரல் stands where குரல் belongs; the ring, read leftward from the west, is a playable octave.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** This is a **mnemonic technology**: the round-dance encodes and transmits the scale to people who will never read a treatise. Music theory is stored in choreography, and children learn the octave by learning where to stand. **What would count against it:** if the note-names are here merely as decorative personal names with no positional logic — but the text explicitly gives the ordering rule (*குடமுதல் இடமுறை*, "west-first, leftward"), which is hard to explain as decoration.

**[மூலம்]** The canto is further sub-labelled with performance-structure headings inside itself: **உரைப்பாட்டு மடை** (F2:1322), **கருப்பம்** (F2:1329), **கொளு** (F2:1341), **கட்டு** (F2:1344), **எடுத்துக் காட்டு** (F2:1358), **கூத்துள் படுதல்** (F2:1382), **பாட்டு** (F2:1392). **[விளக்கம்]** These are the named *sections of a performance* — prelude, theme, cue, binding, illustration, entry-into-the-dance, song. The epic is preserving a running order.

### 7.5 வேட்டுவ வரி and குன்றக் குரவை — the folk registers

**[மூலம்]** Canto 24 opens the வஞ்சி book with the hill people resolving to take கண்ணகி as their god. F3:75–83:

> சிறுகுடி யீரே சிறுகுடி யீரே
> தெய்வங் கொள்ளுமின் சிறுகுடி யீரே
> நிறங்கிளர் அருவிப் பறம்பின் தாழ்வரை
> நறுஞ்சினை வேங்கை நன்னிழற் கீழோர்
> தெய்வங் கொள்ளுமின் சிறுகுடி யீரே
> தொண்டகம் தொடுமின் சிறுபறை தொடுமின்
> கோடுவாய் வைம்மின் கொடுமணி இயக்குமின்
> குறிஞ்சி பாடுமின் நறும்புகை எடுமின்
> பூப்பலி செய்ம்மின் காப்புக்கடை நிறுமின்

**[விளக்கம்]** *O you of the small settlement, O you of the small settlement — take a god, you of the small settlement! On the lower slope of the hill with its bright-coloured waterfall, those beneath the good shade of the fragrant-branched vēṅkai — take a god, you of the small settlement! Strike the toṇḍakam, strike the small drum; set the horn to your mouth, sound the curved bell; sing the kuṛiñci, raise the fragrant smoke; make the flower-offering, set up the guard at the gate.*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Eight imperatives in three lines, each naming an instrument or a ritual act: **தொண்டகம்** (a hill drum), **சிறுபறை** (small drum), **கோடு** (horn), **கொடுமணி** (curved bell), then the mode to sing (**குறிஞ்சி** — the mountain paṇ, and simultaneously the mountain landscape of the ஐந்திணை), then incense, flower-offering, and the setting of a protective gate. **This is a liturgy being founded in real time**, and the epic records its equipment list. **[கருதுகோள்]** The passage is a plausible witness to how a local deity-cult was actually instituted in the Tamil hills — the text preserves the procedure because the procedure was the news.

Canto 12 (**வேட்டுவ வரி**) does the parallel job for the hunters' goddess கொற்றவை / ஐயை. **[விளக்கம்]** Both canto 12 and canto 24 place a *folk-religious song cycle* at a structural hinge of the narrative — one just before the entry into மதுரை, one just after the flight from it.

## 8. உற்றுநோக்கல் ஒன்று | Close reading I — the trial (canto 20)

**[மூலம்]** F2:1675, then F2:1687–1688:

> தேரா மன்னா செப்புவ துடையேன்

> கொலைக்களப் பட்ட கோவலன் மனைவி
> கண்ணகி யென்பதென் பெயரேயெனப்; பெண்ணணங்கே

### சொல்லுக்குச் சொல்

| சொல் | பிரிப்பு | பொருள் |
|---|---|---|
| தேரா | தேர் + ஆ (எதிர்மறை) | not-having-investigated, un-inquiring |
| மன்னா | மன்னன் + விளி | O king (vocative) |
| செப்புவது உடையேன் | — | I have a thing to say |
| கொலைக்களப் பட்ட | கொலை + களம் + பட்ட | fallen on the killing-ground |
| மனைவி | — | wife |
| கண்ணகி என்பது என் பெயரே | — | Kaṇṇaki — that is my name |
| பெண்ணணங்கே | பெண் + அணங்கு + ஏ | O woman-spirit / O terrible woman |

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** Four moves, in fourteen words.

1. **தேரா மன்னா.** The vocative is the indictment. She does not call him unjust, cruel, or false — all available and all weaker. She calls him *the one who did not check*. **[விளக்கம்]** In a monarchy whose ideological centre is the செங்கோல் (the straight sceptre), the failure named is not malice but **absence of procedure**. That is a jurisprudential claim, made in two words, by a merchant's widow, standing in a throne room.

2. **She gives her husband's status before her own name.** "The wife of Kōvalaṉ who fell on the killing-ground" comes first; "Kaṇṇaki is my name" second. **[விளக்கம்]** She establishes *standing* before *identity* — she is not a petitioner but a party with an interest. This is how one enters a case.

3. **She names herself at all.** Recall §5: akam poetry forbids this. Here the naming is the assertion of legal personality. **[கருதுகோள்]** The epic's break with the naming convention and its interest in justiciability are the same gesture: **a person who cannot be named cannot bring a case.** Anonymity is fine for love and fatal for law.

4. **The king's answering vocative — பெண்ணணங்கே.** He does not address her as a subject or a widow. அணங்கு is the word for a dangerous, possessing, numinous female power. **[விளக்கம்]** He recognises what has walked into his court before he understands his error. The poem lets the reader see the king's dread precede the king's guilt.

**[மூலம்]** The verdict follows immediately — F2:1697, then F2:1700–1702:

> மன்னவன் வாய்முதல் தெறித்தது மணியே, மணி கண்டு

> யானோ அரசன் யானே கள்வன்
> மன்பதை காக்குந் தென்புலங் காவல்
> என்முதற் பிழைத்தது கெடுகவென் ஆயுளென

**[விளக்கம்]** *The gem sprang out against the king's very mouth. Seeing the gem: "Am I the king? I am the thief. The guardianship of the southern land, which protects the multitude, has failed at me — let my life perish."*

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The gem strikes his **mouth** — the organ that gave the order. **[காதுக்காக]** Whether Tamil marks this as pointed or whether *வாய்முதல்* is simply idiomatic for "full in the face," I cannot hear; flagged below.

And the confession's grammar is exact: *யானோ அரசன்* is a question, *யானே கள்வன்* is an emphatic identification. He interrogates his own title and then convicts himself with the -ஏ particle. **[விளக்கம்]** He does not say "I have acted like a thief." He says **I am the thief** — accepting the identity he assigned to Kōvalaṉ. The judicial error is repaired by the judge changing places with the condemned.

**[மூலம்]** Canto 16 had already closed on the same image, in its நேரிசை வெண்பா — F2:1310:

> வளையாத செங்கோல் வளைந்ததே பண்டை

**[விளக்கம்]** *The straight sceptre that never bends — it bent.* And canto 19 (F2:1556) repeats it as the city's own bewildered question: *வளையாத செங்கோல் வளைந்த திதுவென்கொல்* — "the never-bending sceptre has bent; what is this?"

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** செங்கோல் — literally the *red/straight rod* — is the standing Tamil metaphor for just rule, and this epic breaks it in three separate cantos, in three different voices: the narrator's verdict (16), the citizens' astonishment (19), the king's self-conviction (20). **[கருதுகோள்]** The repetition is deliberate architecture: the same fact is passed through narrator, crowd, and defendant, so that the political claim of the பதிகம் is established by three independent witnesses inside the poem.

## 9. உற்றுநோக்கல் இரண்டு | Close reading II — the sea-song (canto 7)

**[மூலம்]** F1:1110–1113 — from the திணை நிலைவரி sequence, sung on the shore:

> கடல்புக்கு உயிர்க்கொன்று வாழ்வர்நின் ஐயர்
> உடல்புக்கு உயிர்க்கொன்று வாழ்வைமன் நீயும்
> மிடல்புக்கு அடங்காத வெம்முலையோ பாரம்
> இடர்புக்கு இடுகும் இடைஇழவல் கண்டாய்

### சொல்லுக்குச் சொல்

| சொல் | பிரிப்பு | பொருள் |
|---|---|---|
| கடல் புக்கு | — | having entered the sea |
| உயிர்க் கொன்று | உயிர் + கொன்று | having killed life |
| வாழ்வர் | — | they live (by it) |
| நின் ஐயர் | — | your elders / your menfolk |
| உடல் புக்கு | — | having entered the body |
| வாழ்வை மன் நீயும் | — | you too live (by it), indeed |
| மிடல் | — | strength, force |
| அடங்காத | — | unsubdued, not contained |
| வெம்முலை | வெம் + முலை | the fierce breast |
| பாரம் | — | burden, weight |
| இடர் புக்கு இடுகும் | — | entering distress, it grows thin/pinched |
| இடை இழவல் | — | do not lose your waist |

### பொருள்

**[விளக்கம்]** *Your menfolk go into the sea, kill life, and live by it. You too go into the body, kill life, and live by it. That fierce breast, unsubdued in its force, is a burden — do not lose the waist that is being pinched thin by the distress of it. Look.*

### நுட்பம் | The craft

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This quatrain is doing something the Sangam lyric had already perfected and the epic is now *aiming*.

**The mechanism is a single substituted noun.** Line 1 and line 2 are the same sentence with one word changed: **கடல் புக்கு → உடல் புக்கு**. The fishermen enter the *sea* and kill for a living; the girl enters the *body* and kills for a living. The rhyme is not ornamental — it is the argument. Her beauty is placed in the same economic category as her family's trade: **both are killing occupations, and hers is the one practised at home.**

**[விளக்கம்]** The நெய்தல் landscape does the rest. The seashore is the திணை of anxious separation (`../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1). A love-song set among drying fish and killing-nets converts the whole apparatus of the fishing village into an image of what love does. The உள்ளுறை machinery of Sangam poetry is intact and running — but it is now running **inside a plot**, sung by a named character, to another named character, and the reason the poem exists is that its addressee will *misunderstand it* and walk away.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** This is the epic's most sophisticated formal move and it deserves naming: **சிலப்பதிகாரம் makes the ambiguity of akam convention into a plot event.** The Sangam lyric's greatest strength — that the speaker is anyone, that meaning is carried implicitly and completed by the listener (`../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §3, on shared codebooks) — becomes, in a narrative with named persons, a *hazard*. கோவலன் and மாதவி each hear the other's conventional love-song as addressed to a real third party. **The marriage of the epic's second act is destroyed by the reader-completion property of Tamil love poetry.**

**What would count against it:** if the commentaries read the கானல் வரி separation as caused by something external (an omen, a quarrel, ஊழ்வினை alone) rather than by the songs' ambiguity. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** I cannot settle this from the மூலம் alone; the framing கட்டுரை at F1:1140–1144 says மாதவி concluded *மன்னும்ஓர் குறிப்புஉண்டுஇவன் / தன்நிலை மயங்கினான்* — "there is surely some intent in this; he has confused his own state" — which supports the misreading account but is not decisive.

## 10. காலமும் ஆசிரியரும் | Dating and authorship

### The tradition

**[மூலம்]** The e-text title pages of all three files read *இளங்கோ அடிகள் இயற்றியது* (F1:6, F1:20; F3:26). The பதிகம' opens by addressing him — F1:24–25:

> குணவாயில் கோட்டத்து அரசுதுறந்து இருந்த
> குடக்கோச் சேரல் இளங்கோ வடிகட்குக்

**[விளக்கம்]** *To Kuḍakkō Cheral Ilaṅkō Aḍigaḷ, who dwelt at the Kuṇavāyil shrine having renounced rule…*

**[முடிவு | CONCLUSION — narrow]** What the local source actually attests is this and no more: a **western (குடக்கு) Chera named Ilaṅkō Aḍigaḷ, who had renounced sovereignty and was living at a shrine called குணவாயில் கோட்டம்**, to whom hill-dwellers reported the marvel, and who was asked by the merchant சாத்தன் to compose the work.

**[விளக்கம் — important precision]** The passage I quoted does **not** say he was செங்குட்டுவன்'s brother. I searched all three files: the string இளங்கோ appears only at F1:6, F1:20, and F1:25 — the two title-page lines and the பதிகம் address. செங்குட்டுவன் is named in the வஞ்சி book (F3:1156, 1188, 1232, 1237) as the reigning Chera, son of சேரலாதன் and a Chola king's daughter (F3:1152–1154), but **no line in these three e-texts links the two men as brothers.**

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** The brother-relationship is **commentarial and traditional, not textual.** It is usually derived by combining (a) the பதிகம்'s "renounced-rule Chera prince" with (b) a தமிழ்நாடு tradition, elaborated by the commentator அடியார்க்கு நல்லார், that Ilaṅkō renounced the throne in favour of his elder brother after an astrologer predicted the younger would rule. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** I have not verified the commentarial source from a local file — no commentary is in `_src/txt/`. **This is an open item and should not be stated as fact anywhere in this repo.**

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** A further caution: the பதிகம் itself is regarded by many scholars as a **later addition** to the poem — it speaks *about* Ilaṅkō in the third person and narrates the circumstances of the work's commissioning, which is a colophon's job, not an author's. **[கருதுகோள்]** If the பதிகம் is later, then the *only* evidence for Ilaṅkō's identity is evidence about what a later generation believed, not about the poet. This does not make the attribution false; it changes what kind of claim it is.

**[விளக்கம்]** Note also who is *inside* the frame: **சாத்தன்**, the grain-merchant of Madurai (*மதுரைக் கூல வாணிகன் சாத்தன்*, F1:87–88), is named as the one who heard the work. Tradition identifies him with சீத்தலைச் சாத்தனார், the author of மணிமேகலை. **[கருதுகோள்]** The two epics are thus presented as a matched pair with a shared frame — and the closing நூற் கட்டுரை's handover to மணிமேகலை (§6.6) is textual support for the pairing, whatever one concludes about the two authors.

### The date — contested, and to be left contested

**[விளக்கம்]** Per `../murai.md` §6 and the repo's standing practice, no single date is given. The positions, and who holds them:

| Position | Approx. date | Principal grounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Early / traditional** | 2nd century CE | Takes செங்குட்டுவன் as a historical contemporary of the poet, and reads the epic as near-contemporary reportage. Associated with the Tamil traditional chronology and with scholars aligning சிலப்பதிகாரம் with the late Sangam. |
| **Middle** | 4th–5th century CE | Language and metre are post-Sangam; the epic's Jain and Buddhist framing, the Sanskrit narrative material, and the developed bhakti-adjacent devotional registers suggest a later composition drawing on an older story. |
| **Late** | 5th–6th century CE or later | Emphasises the developed prosodic variety, the வரி system's sophistication, and links to the post-Kalabhra period. Some hold that a core narrative is old but the text as we have it was assembled or finished late. |

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** The dating problem has a specific structural cause worth naming: the epic contains a **historical anchor** (செங்குட்டுவன's northern expedition) which, if genuine, dates the *events*; but nothing forces the composition to be contemporary with the events it narrates. An epic about a 2nd-century king can be written in the 5th. Arguments that slide from one to the other should be treated with suspicion in either direction.

**[விளக்கம்]** A second complication, honestly stated: the strongest arguments on all sides are *linguistic*, and linguistic dating of Tamil is itself contested. I am not in a position to adjudicate and this document does not.

## 11. மூலப் பதிவு பற்றிய குறிப்பு | Notes on the source e-texts

**[மூலம் — observations made directly on the files]** Findings that matter for anyone working with these files later:

1. **Mixed Tamil encoding.** The files mix precomposed and **legacy-decomposed/reordered** Tamil vowel signs. In F3:76, the syllable **கொ** is stored as க + ெ + ா (KA + vowel sign E + vowel sign AA) rather than as க + ொ. **Consequence: `grep` with a typed pattern will silently fail** on lines that a visually identical typed string should match. Verified by `od -c` on F3:76. **[முன்மொழிவு | PROPOSAL]** Always build search patterns by copying text *out of the file* (via `sed -n 'Np'`), never by typing them. Consider a normalisation pass (NFC) before any systematic study, but keep the originals.

    **[விளக்கம் — reported honestly]** I fell into this trap while writing this very document. Twelve blockquote lines drafted here initially failed grep-verification against their own cited source lines. Every one was an encoding mismatch, not a misquotation: the source held the decomposed form and my draft held the precomposed one. The lines were repaired by re-extracting the exact bytes from the files, and the document now verifies at 66/66. **The failure mode is silent and looks exactly like correct work** — the two forms are visually identical in most renderers. Any future session quoting from these files should run a programmatic verification pass rather than trusting the eye.

2. **Metre annotations exist only in F1.** File 1 (pmuni0046, prepared by a different volunteer team in 2000–2001) carries editorial metre headings such as `(நிலைமண்டில ஆசிரியப்பா)` and `(வெண்பா)`. Files 2 and 3 (pmuni0111_01/02) carry none. **[விளக்கம்]** The metre labels are therefore **editorial apparatus, not part of the poem**, and the மதுரை and வஞ்சி books' metres would have to be determined by scansion, not by reading a label. Any claim in this document about metre in cantos 11–30 would be computed, and I have made none.

3. **An internal inconsistency in F2.** The table of contents at F2:45 reads `13. புரஞ்சேரியிறுத்த காதை` (with ர) while the body heading at F2:453 reads `13. புறஞ்சேரியிறுத்த காதை` (with ற). The பதிகம் at F1:104 has *புறஞ்சேரி இறுத்த காதையும்*. **[முடிவு — narrow]** The TOC spelling is a typo; ற is correct.

4. **F3 retains raw HTML.** The வஞ்சி file begins with an undeleted `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration and CSS block (F3:1–21) and ends with a mailto link. Strip before processing.

5. **Line-number caution.** These files interleave the poem with the e-text's own in-canto verse numbering (`5`, `10`, `15`…) at line ends. Do not confuse the *canto-internal* number printed at the end of a line with the *file* line number. All citations in this document are file line numbers.

## 12. காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear

**[முன்மொழிவு]** Addressed to Ilam. Every item below is a claim about **how something sounds or feels**, which per `../murai.md` §8 and the standing rule of this repo I compute and cannot hear. None of these should be treated as settled until you have ruled.

1. **The பதிகம' triad's rhythm.** Lines F1:78–80 all end in the pattern *-ஆவதூஉம் / -ஏத்தலும் / -என்பதூஉம்*, with அளபெடை (the stretched ஊ) in the first and third. Computed: two of the three propositions are lengthened and the middle one is not. **Does the middle line sit shorter in the mouth, and is that felt as the social claim being lighter than the political and the karmic?** I can see the asymmetry; I cannot hear whether it means anything.

2. **தேரா மன்னா (F2:1675).** Computed: two words, both ending in the long ஆ, forming an assonant pair. **Does this land as a slap in spoken Tamil?** My reading of the whole trial scene depends on it.

3. **யானோ அரசன் யானே கள்வன் (F2:1700).** Computed: யானோ / யானே — identical stem, contrasting particles (-ஓ interrogative, -ஏ emphatic), in symmetrical position. **Is the ஓ/ஏ switch audible as the hinge, or does it read flat?** This is the most famous line in the epic and I have built §8 on the claim that the particle contrast is the point.

4. **வளையாத செங்கோல் வளைந்ததே (F2:1310).** Computed: வளையாத / வளைந்தது — negative and positive of the same verb root adjacent. **Is this heard as wordplay, as a groan, or as neither?**

5. **கடல் புக்கு / உடல் புக்கு (F1:1110–1111).** Computed: single-consonant substitution, க→உ, at line-head, in consecutive lines. **Does the near-identity register on the ear as a pun, and is a pun the right register for this song, or does it read as cruel?** My whole reading in §9 assumes the substitution is deliberate and audible.

6. **சிறுகுடி யீரே (F3:75–79).** Computed: threefold repetition of the vocative in five lines, with *தெய்வங் கொள்ளுமின்* twice. **Is this a chant, i.e. does it feel like something a group actually sang together?** I have called it a liturgy.

7. **The eight yāḻ terms (F1:994–996, F1:1001–1003).** Computed: each list fits an ஆசிரியப்பா line-shape and the terms are grouped four-and-four. **Do the lists sound like verse or like a list bolted into verse?** If the latter, that strengthens the "practitioner's protocol" hypothesis in §7.3.

8. **My Tamil prose in §4.** Entirely generated. **Register, verb choice, and whether it reads as Tamil or as translated English — please strike out anything that is wrong.** I flag particularly: *இல்லறம் நடத்தினர்*, *ஐயம் வந்தது*, *மீண்டும் தொடங்குக*, and whether *ஆராயாத அரசனே* is a defensible gloss of தேரா மன்னா in modern prose.

9. **All English translations in this document** are working glosses. Where the Tamil is doing something my English flattens, the English is wrong and should be corrected rather than kept.

## 13. ஐயம் | Doubt

**[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Never empty, per `../marabu/README.md`.

1. **The unverified commentarial chain.** §10's brother-tradition, §7.3's prediction about later music literature, and the நுட்பம் readings that would be tested against அடியார்க்கு நல்லார் and அரும்பதவுரையாசிரியர் are all **unchecked** — no commentary exists in `_src/txt/`. Every reading here is of the bare மூலம், which is a real limitation on a text this heavily commented.

2. **The three propositions may not cohere (§5).** I have declined to reconcile them. Someone should decide whether the failure to reconcile is the poem's or the reader's.

3. **முலை and the burning.** I have described canto 21 plainly. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** What the tearing-off of the breast *means* within the text's own symbolic system — as the destruction of the nurturing body, as the conversion of the பத்தினி's stored power into fire, as something else — I have not attempted. It deserves its own entry and probably its own argument in `aaivu/karuthu/`.

4. **The pathigam's authenticity (§10) changes §5's status.** If the பதிகம் is later, then the "stated moral thesis" that §6.5 counts as a formal innovation may be a *later reader's* summary rather than the poet's declaration. **This weakens one of my six innovation claims and I want it on the record.** The naming innovation (§6.2) survives, because it is attested in the body; the thesis innovation may not.

5. **I have not read cantos 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 18, 19, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 28 closely.** This document is built on close reading of cantos 1, 3, 7, 16, 17, 20, 21, 24, 29, 30, the பதிகம், and the two கட்டுரை. The unread cantos include the entire Himalayan expedition and the whole of the aftermath. Claims about the shape of the வஞ்சி book (§3) rest on canto headings plus cantos 24, 29, 30, and should be treated as provisional.

6. **Whether கவுந்தி அடிகள் being a Jain ascetic makes this a "Jain epic"** is a live scholarly dispute I have stepped around. The text's own devotional material is strikingly plural — a Jain guide, Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva hymnody in cantos 12 and 17, a Korravai cult in canto 12, a Buddhist ending in canto 30. **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Whether this is syncretism, an author's ecumenism, or accretion across recensions is not something the bare text settles.
