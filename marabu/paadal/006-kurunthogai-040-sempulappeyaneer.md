# 006 · குறுந்தொகை 40 — செம்புலப் பெயனீர் | Red Earth and Pouring Rain

> **நூல்** குறுந்தொகை (எட்டுத்தொகை) · **எண்** 40 · **புலவர்** **செம்புலப் பெயனீரார்** — and that is
> not a name (§9) · **காலம்** சங்கம், ~300 BCE–300 CE
> **திணை** குறிஞ்சி — mountain, union · **கூற்று** தலைவன், to her, at first union
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0110](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0110.html)

Five lines. Probably the most loved short poem in Tamil. A close reading exists at
[`../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md`](../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md) §2; this entry re-cuts it for teaching.

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

> yā-yum ñā-yum yā-rā ki-ya-**rō**
> en-tai-yum nun-tai-yum em-mu-ṟaik **kē-ḷir**
> yā-num nī-yum ev-va-ḻi ya-ṟi-**tum**
> cem-pu-lap pe-ya-**nīr** pō-la
> aṉ-pu-ṭai neñ-cam tāṅ-ka-lan ta-ṉa-**vē**

Listen to the first three lines before you know a word. Each is built out of **pairs bound by -உம்**:
யாயு**ம்** ஞாயு**ம்** · எந்தையு**ம்** நுந்தையு**ம்** · யானு**ம்** நீயு**ம்**. Mother-and-mother,
father-and-father, you-and-I. The poem *sounds* like two things being set beside each other, three
times, before it ever says so.

And line 1 chimes: **யா**யும் · ஞாயும் · **யா**ரா · கியரோ — first and third feet on **யா**, the
**பொழிப்பு** interval (செய்யுளியல் 94).

## 2. படம் | The picture

Red earth, dry. Then rain falls on it.

Now try to take the rain back out of the soil.

## 3. மூலம் | The text

> யாயும் ஞாயும் யாரா கியரோ
> எந்தையும் நுந்தையும் எம்முறைக் கேளிர்
> யானும் நீயும் எவ்வழி யறிதும்
> செம்புலப் பெயனீர் போல
> அன்புடை நெஞ்சம் தாங்கலந் தனவே.
>
> — செம்புலப் பெயனீரார்

**Source:** `pmuni0110`, poem 40, quoted exactly. Five அடி, **ஆசிரியப்பா**.

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
என் தாயும் உன் தாயும் ஒருவருக்கு ஒருவர் யார்? என் தந்தையும் உன் தந்தையும் எந்த முறையில் உறவினர்?
நானும் நீயும் எந்த வழியில் அறிந்துகொண்டோம்? — செம்மண்ணில் பெய்த மழைநீர் போல, அன்புள்ள நெஞ்சங்கள்
தாமாகவே கலந்துவிட்டன.

**In English:**
My mother and yours — what were they to each other? My father and yours — kin in what way? You and
I — by what road did we even come to know each other? And yet, like red earth and the rain that fell
on it, our loving hearts have mingled of themselves.

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**Four archaic kin words in two lines.** **யாய்** (my mother), **ஞாய்** (your mother), **எந்தை**
(my father), **நுந்தை** (your father). Notice what they carry: **the possessor is inside the word.**
Not "my mother" as two words but a single form meaning *mother-of-mine*, with a matching form for
*mother-of-yours*. Modern Tamil has lost these and says என் அம்மா / உன் அம்மா — **two words where
the classical had one.** The fourth instance of the drift this repo keeps logging
([`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md) §1).

**எந்தை / நுந்தை and the எ- / நு- pair.** எம் = our/my; நும் = your. That opposition is still alive
in நீ / நான், எங்கள் / உங்கள். A fluent speaker already owns the distinction; the poem just shows it
older and tighter.

**-உம் … -உம் again.** Three pairs, six -உம். Same coordinator as entry 007's யாதும் / யாவரும்.
Tamil has no word for *and*; it puts **-உம்** on each member. Here that grammatical fact **is** the
poem's form.

**தாம் — of their own accord.** தாம் கலந்தன = *they, of themselves, mingled.* No agent, no arranger.
The whole argument of the poem sits in one word, and it is a word that means *nobody did this*.

**-ஏ, the Sangam close.** தனவ**ே**. The same ending-particle that closes குறுந்தொகை 2 (பூவ**ே**) and
புறம் 192 (இலம**ே**). Closure you can hear.

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

**பா:** ஆசிரியப்பா, five அடி — 4 · 4 · 4 · 3 · 4 சீர். Line 4 is a **குறளடி**, the short line, and
it is the line carrying the image. The poem physically narrows at the moment it stops asking
questions.

**மோனை:** line 1, **யா**யும் · ஞாயும் · **யா**ரா — சீர் 1 and 3, **பொழிப்பு** interval.

> **Computed, not heard.** Whether the three -உம் pairs actually *sound* like paired things to a
> Tamil ear, or whether that is a reader's construction, is yours to rule on. `../../murai.md` §8.

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**Three absences, then one presence.** Lines 1–3 are three questions, and every one has the same
answer: *nothing.* No kinship between our mothers. No kinship between our fathers. No road by which
we met. The poem builds a case that this love has **no cause, no arrangement, and no precedent** —
and only then gives the image.

**And the image answers all three at once.** Red earth is of the ground. Rain is of the sky. They
have no kinship, no arranger, and no prior acquaintance — and once they meet they cannot be
separated, ever, by anyone. The poem does not say *therefore*. It does not need to.

**தாம் — the word that refuses a matchmaker.** In a world of arranged marriage and caste and family
negotiation, the poem's last claim is that the hearts mingled **by themselves**. Everything the three
questions dismantle — lineage, arrangement, prior connection — is the machinery of a marriage. This
is a love poem that is quietly also an argument.

**Irreversibility is the whole point.** Not *like two rivers joining* — you could still say which
water came from where. Red earth and rain make **mud**: a third substance, from which neither
original can be recovered. The poem chose the one mixture that cannot be undone.

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

**The poet has no name.** Tradition calls him **செம்புலப் பெயனீரார்** — *"he of the red-earth-and-
pouring-rain."* That is not a name. It is **his own image**, attached where a name used to be.
Whoever he was is gone; the phrase he made survives and now stands in for the man.

Sangam anthologies do this often — poets are called after a striking phrase from their one surviving
poem. It is a naming convention born of the palm-leaf economy: what survives is the line, not the
life. `../../varalaru/thonmai-pathivu.md` and `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §2 carry more; the earlier
instance of this project found the fact worth recording in `../../isaivu.md` on 2026-07-08, and it is
still the sharpest thing in the anthology's apparatus.

**அகம் convention forbids naming the lovers.** They are தலைவன் and தலைவி — *anyone*. So the poem is
not one man and one woman. It is every first meeting that ever bypassed family, arrangement and
caste — which is why it has survived two thousand years of being quoted at weddings.

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 5+ for the activity, 8+ for the poem, older for what it is really saying.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"I'm going to mix two things, and then I want you to un-mix them."*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Where does red earth come from? Where does rain come from? Are they anything to each other?
2. Once the rain has gone into the soil, can you get it back out? Can anyone?
3. The man who wrote this — nobody knows his name. We only call him "the red-earth-and-pouring-rain
   man," after his own poem. Is that a sad thing or a good thing?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Get some dry soil in a bowl — red if you can find it. Look at it. Pour a little water on it. Mix it.

Now **try to separate them again.**

When you have properly failed, learn line 4: **செம்புலப் பெயனீர் போல.** That is the whole poem in
four words, and you now know what it means with your hands.

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Text, poet-name, திணை and கூற்று as printed in `pmuni0110`, quoted exactly.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §8's reading — that the three questions dismantle *the machinery of
  an arranged marriage* — is ours. The poem asks about kinship and acquaintance; that these are
  specifically marriage-brokering criteria is inference from context, well supported by அகம்
  convention but not stated in the five lines.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** "Red earth and rain make mud, a third substance from which neither
  can be recovered" is our gloss on why this image and not another. The poem says only **கலந்தன**,
  *they mingled*.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** **செம்புலம்** — is the redness doing work, or is it simply the
  soil of that country? Laterite is red across much of Tamil Nadu, so it may be plain description
  rather than chosen colour. We take no position; the reading in §8 does not depend on it.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** No commentary consulted. There is no old commentary on குறுந்தொகை comparable
  to those on the Kural, but modern commentaries (உ.வே.சா.'s edition) exist and are not in `../../_src/`.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - Do the three -உம் pairs land as *pairing*, or is that only visible on the page?
  - Line 4 is the short line (**குறளடி**) and carries the image. Does the poem audibly *narrow*
    there, or is that a scansion artefact?
  - **தாங்கலந் தனவே** — how much weight does தாம் actually carry in the mouth? The entire reading in
    §8 rests on it.
