# 004 · ஆத்திசூடி · உயிர்மெய் வருக்கம் — ஙப் போல் வளை | Bend Like ங

> **நூல்** ஆத்திசூடி · **எண்** 14–31 (உயிர்மெய் வருக்கம்) · **புலவர்** ஔவையார் — the later one, not
> the Sangam poet (entry 002 §9) · **வகை** நீதிநெறி நூல் · **கூற்று** the poet, to a child
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0002](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0002.html)
> **இலக்கண மூலம்** தொல்காப்பியம், எழுத்ததிகாரம், **மொழிமரபு** நூற்பா 5, 26–34
> ([pmuni0100](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0100.html))

Entry 002 took the thirteen vowel lines and noticed one thing at the end: **ஃ cannot begin a word,
so line 13 hides it inside one.** That looked like a single clever compromise.

It is not a compromise. It is a **system**, it runs through all eighteen consonant lines, and the
rule it obeys is written down in Tolkāppiyam. This entry is the one where the alphabet-poem and the
grammar turn out to be the same object.

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

The eighteen lines, and what each one's letter is doing:

|---|---|---|---|

| 15 | **ங** | **ஙப் போல் வளை** | **named outright** — see §6 |

Nine start on their letter. Nine cannot. **The nine that cannot are not a coincidence and not a
failure of craft.**

## 2. படம் | The picture

Some Tamil letters can start a word and some can never. **ங** is the shyest of all — it never stands
alone anywhere. It always comes holding on to **க**: த**ங்க**ம், ச**ங்க**ம், பொ**ங்க**ல், இ**ங்கே**.

So when Auvaiyar needed a rule for ங, she could not begin the line with it. Instead she pointed at
the letter and said: **be like that. Bend. Stay with your people.**

## 3. மூலம் | The text

The line this entry turns on:

> **ஙப் போல் வளை.**

And the grammar underneath it — தொல்காப்பியம், எழுத்ததிகாரம், மொழிமரபு:

> பன்னீர் உயிரும் மொழி முதல் ஆகும். (26)
> உயிர் மெய் அல்லன மொழி முதல் ஆகா. (27)
> க த ந ப ம எனும் ஆவைந்து எழுத்தும்
> எல்லா உயிரொடும் செல்லுமார் முதலே. (28)
> சகரக் கிளவியும் அவற்று ஓரற்றே
> அ ஐ ஔ எனும் மூன்று அலங்கடையே. (29)
> ஆ எ ஒ எனும் மூ உயிர் ஞகாரத்து உரிய. (31)
> ஆவொடு அல்லது யகரம் முதலாது. (32)

And, for entry 002's ஃ claim, which that entry asserted without a source:

> குறியதன் முன்னர் ஆய்தப் புள்ளி
> உயிரொடு புணர்ந்த வல் ஆறன் மிசைத்தே. (மொழிமரபு 5)

**Source:** ஆத்திசூடி from `pmuni0002`; தொல்காப்பியம் from `pmuni0100`, both quoted exactly.

## 4. சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word

**ஙப் போல் வளை**

| சொல் | பிரிப்பு | பொருள் | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| ஙப் | ங + ப் | 'ங' என்னும் எழுத்து | the letter **ங** — named the way you name a letter |
| போல் | — | போன்று | like |
| வளை | — | வளைந்து கொடு | bend · be pliant · curve |

**The grammar நூற்பா, plainly:**

| நூற்பா | பொருள் |
|---|---|
| 26 | All twelve vowels may begin a word. |
| 27 | Anything that is not a உயிர்மெய் may not begin a word — so a bare மெய், and ஃ, are barred. |
| 28 | **க த ந ப ம** — these five may begin a word with *any* vowel. |
| 29 | **ச** too, except with அ, ஐ, ஔ. |
| 31 | **ஞ** takes only ஆ, எ, ஒ. |
| 32 | **ய** begins a word only with **ஆ** — hence யாழ், யானை, யாது, and no others. |
| 5 | The **ஃ** dot stands after a short vowel and before one of the six hard consonants. |

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
ங என்னும் எழுத்து தனித்து நிற்பதில்லை; எப்போதும் தன் இனத்தோடு — கவுடன் — சேர்ந்தே வரும். அதைப்
போல நீயும் வளைந்து கொடு; உன்னைச் சார்ந்தவரோடு இணைந்து நில்.

**In English:**
The letter ங never stands on its own; it always comes joined to its own kin, க. Be like that —
bend, give way, stay joined to your people.

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**Nine consonants cannot begin a Tamil word.** From நூற்பா 26–32, the letters that *can* stand at
the head of a word are **க ச ஞ த ந ப ம ய வ** — and even those are constrained (ய only before ஆ;
ஞ only before ஆ, எ, ஒ). The nine that cannot are **ங ட ண ர ல ழ ள ற ன**.

Now look back at §1. Every single line whose letter sits in *second* position is one of those nine.
Auvaiyar is not being inconsistent. **She is obeying Tolkāppiyam, line by line, for eighteen
lines** — and letting the acrostic bend rather than let the language break.

**And ங is the hardest case of all.** The other eight barred consonants can at least sit second in
an ordinary word — இ**ட**ம், அ**ர**வம், இ**ள**மை. ங effectively cannot even do that: it appears in
Tamil almost exclusively as **ங்** before **க**, inside the cluster ங்க. There is no ordinary word
with a usable ங for an acrostic.

So Auvaiyar does the only thing left: she **names the letter itself** — ஙப், "the letter ங" — and
makes *the letter's own distributional rule* into the moral. ங has no independent existence; it
survives by attaching to its own class. **Bend like that.**

That is a phonotactic constraint converted into an ethic, in three words. It is the most compressed
thing in the book and possibly in Tamil didactic literature.

**What a child is actually learning here.** Not "ங makes the *ng* sound." A child learning this line
is learning: *letters have company they keep, and some cannot stand alone.* That is distribution —
a real linguistic idea — delivered before the word "grammar" is ever used.

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

As with entry 002, these are sutra-like didactic lines rather than metrical பா, and the organising
principle is the syllabary rather than the metre. Two sonic notes:

- The **-ஏல்** negative imperative continues: சொல்லேல், எடேல், மறவேல், பேசேல், செய்யேல் (×2),
  ஆட்டேல், உண்ணேல் — eight of the eighteen.
- **ஙப் போல் வளை** is the shortest line in the entire book at three words and five syllables, and
  it is the only line that takes a **letter** as its subject rather than a thing or an act.

> **Computed, not heard.** Whether ஙப் போல் வளை has the epigrammatic snap in a Tamil ear that its
> structure predicts is Ilam's call — `../../murai.md` §8.

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**The constraint is doing the composing.** Entry 002 §8 noticed this at line 12 — ஔ is so rare that
the alphabet effectively chose the vice (ஔவியம், envy). Here the same pressure operates nine more
times, and at ங it produces the book's best line. A form tight enough to squeeze is a form that
generates.

**The moral and the mechanism agree.** This is what raises ஙப் போல் வளை above cleverness. The
instruction is *bend, join, don't stand apart* — and it is delivered by the one letter in Tamil that
has no choice but to bend and join. The vehicle and the tenor are the same fact. Most metaphors
compare two things; this one **is** the thing it compares to.

**Line 30 breaks the pattern, and that is interesting rather than embarrassing** — see §11.

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

**Why Tolkāppiyam legislates this at all.** எழுத்ததிகாரம் spends its first two இயல் (நூன்மரபு,
மொழிமரபு) on what letters *are* and where they may *stand*. That is phonotactics — the rules for
which sounds may occupy which positions — and it is written down in Tamil at least a millennium and
a half before European linguistics gave the phenomenon a name. The rules were not invented by
Tolkāppiyar; they were observed and recorded. Auvaiyar, centuries later, composes inside them
without ever citing them, the way a native speaker obeys rules they were never taught.

**What the barred nine tell you about the language.** Tamil words open on vowels or on a small set
of consonants and close on a small set of sonorants. That is a language with a strongly constrained
syllable shape — no consonant clusters at the start of a word, ever. `../../aaivu/mudhal-oli.md`
(வி-014) already measured this from the other direction and found Tamil sitting unusually close to
the sounds an infant makes first. நூற்பா 26–32 is that same finding, stated as law, two thousand
years earlier.

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 5–9.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"There's one letter in Tamil that is never, ever alone. Want to go find it?"*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Say these: த**ங்க**ம், ச**ங்க**ம், பொ**ங்க**ல், இ**ங்கே**, எ**ங்கே**. What comes right after ங
   every single time? *(க — always.)*
2. Can you think of a word that **starts** with ங? *(You can't. Nobody can. That's the point.)*
3. She says *bend like ங*. What does it mean to bend, when you're a person and not a letter?
   Is bending the same as giving up?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Go on a **ங hunt**. Take ten minutes and find as many words as you can with ங in them — say them out
loud, write them down. Then check every single one: is க always sitting right there after it?

When you've proved it for yourself, you have discovered a rule of Tamil grammar by looking, which is
how the rule got written down in the first place.

> Then try the same hunt with **ஃ** — அஃது, எஃகு, அஃகம். Only three or four exist. Ask why.

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** All eighteen ஆத்திசூடி lines are quoted exactly from `pmuni0002`. All
  தொல்காப்பியம் நூற்பா are quoted exactly from `pmuni0100`, மொழிமரபு, with the numbers as printed.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** This entry closes an open item in **entry 002 §11**: the claim there that ஃ
  occurs only after a short vowel and before a hard consonant is now sourced to மொழிமரபு நூற்பா 5
  rather than asserted. Entry 002 has been updated to cite it.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** **Line 30 — அரனை or அறனை?** The acrostic requires **ற** at
  this position. Project Madurai prints **அரனை மறவேல்** ("do not forget Aran" — Śiva), whose second
  letter is **ர**, not ற. Other editions are reported to read **அறனை மறவேல்** ("do not forget
  virtue"), which fits the slot exactly. **The acrostic is itself evidence about the text.** We have
  not consulted a critical edition and take no position, but note that the structural argument
  favours அறனை, and that அரனை would make line 30 the only line in 109 that misses its letter.
  This should be checked against the ஆறுமுக நாவலர் உரை and `pmuni0777`.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** **Line 17 — ஞயம் or ஞாயம்?** நூற்பா 31 says ஞ may take only
  ஆ, எ, ஒ as its vowel. Project Madurai prints **ஞயம்பட** — ஞ + அ — which the நூற்பா disallows.
  **ஞாயம்பட** would be legal. Same shape of problem as line 30, and the two together suggest the
  acrostic can be used as a systematic textual check on the transmitted ஆத்திசூடி. That is a real
  method and worth a `../../aaivu/sothanai-*`; it is not a finding yet.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** *Closed the same day it was opened.* ங's distribution **is** stated in the
  grammar — **நூன்மரபு நூற்பா 25**: *ங ஞ ண ந ம ன எனும் புள்ளி முன்னர் / **தம்தம் இசைகள் ஒத்தன
  நிலையே*** — after each of those dotted nasals stands **its own matching sound**. The rule is
  wider and more precise than this entry's paraphrase: *every* nasal is followed by its class-mate
  (ங்→க, ஞ்→ச, ண்→ட, ந்→த, ம்→ப, ன்→ற), and ங is simply the one for whom that class-mate is the
  only thing that ever follows. **ஙப் போல் வளை is a restatement of a phonotactic law.** Full
  reading: [`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md) §1.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** That Auvaiyar was *consciously* obeying Tolkāppiyam is inference.
  A native composer obeys phonotactics whether or not they have read the grammar. The observable
  fact is the agreement; the intention is not recoverable.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - Does **ஙப் போல் வளை** land as epigram, or does it read as a schoolroom mnemonic?
  - Is **வளை** here felt as *bend* (yielding) or as *curve* (shape)? The moral changes with it, and
    the English gloss in §5 commits to "bend" without warrant.
  - Line 24, **இயல்பு அலாதன செய்யேல்**, and line 28, **அழகு அலாதன செய்யேல்**, are near-identical in
    shape. Is the repetition heard as emphasis or as filler?
