# 001 · திருக்குறள் 1 — அகர முதல | The Letter A, and the First of Things

> **நூல்** திருக்குறள் · **எண்** 1 · **புலவர்** திருவள்ளுவர் · **காலம்** ~450–500 CE is a common
> scholarly range; traditional dating is far earlier and the question is not settled (§11)
> **அதிகாரம்** 1 — கடவுள் வாழ்த்து (பாயிரவியல், அறத்துப்பால்) · **கூற்று** the poet, opening the book
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0001](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0001.html)

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

> a-ka-ra mu-ta-la e-ḻut-tel-lām ā-ti
> pa-ka-vaṉ mu-taṟ-ṟē u-la-ku

Almost every syllable here is **short**. The line runs light and fast — `a-ka-ra mu-ta-la` is six
short beats in a row — and then it hits **ஆதி** and stops on a long vowel, right at the end of the
first line, with the sense unfinished. You have to cross the gap to get the rest of the name.

Two sounds to notice with the ear, not the eye:

- **அகர / பகவன்** — both lines begin on a short syllable, and both have **க** as their *second*
  letter. That is the rhyme. It arrives one syllable in, while you are still starting the word.
- **முதல / முதற்று** — the same word, முதல், in both lines, in two different shapes. The couplet's
  whole argument is that repetition.

## 2. படம் | The picture

Every letter in Tamil starts with the same sound: **அ**. Say க, ச, ட, த, ப — listen — *a* is inside
every one of them. The world is like that too: everything in it starts from one thing.

## 3. மூலம் | The text

> அகர முதல எழுத்தெல்லாம் ஆதி
> பகவன் முதற்றே உலகு.

**Source:** Project Madurai e-text `pmuni0001`, திருக்குறள் மூலம், verse 1. Quoted exactly.

## 4. சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word

| சொல் | பிரிப்பு | பொருள் | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| அகர | அ + கர(ம்) | 'அ' என்னும் எழுத்து | the letter **அ** — *akaram*, the tradition's name for it |
| முதல | முதல் (பலவின்பால் பயனிலை) | முதலாக உடையன | *have as their first* — plural predicate, agreeing with எழுத்துக்கள் |
| எழுத்தெல்லாம் | எழுத்து + எல்லாம் | எல்லா எழுத்தும் | all letters |
| ஆதி | — | முதன்மையான, ஆதியான | primal, the origin |
| பகவன் | — | இறைவன் | the god, the blessed one |
| முதற்றே | முதல் + து + ஏ | முதலாக உடையது தான் | *has-as-its-first*, indeed |
| உலகு | — | உலகம் | the world |

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
எழுத்துக்கள் எல்லாவற்றுக்கும் அகரம் முதல். அது போலவே, இந்த உலகத்திற்கு ஆதிபகவன் முதல்.

**In English:**
As the letter **அ** is the first of all letters, so the primal God is the first of the world.

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**அ is not merely first in the list — it is inside every letter.** This is the load-bearing fact and
most translations lose it. Tamil consonants carry **அ** by default: க *is* **ka**, not *k*. To strip
the vowel out you must put a dot on top — **க்** — the புள்ளி. So அ is both the first letter *and*
the element present in all of them, unless it is explicitly removed. The analogy Valluvar is drawing
is therefore exact, not decorative: the first is not merely earliest, it is *in* everything after it.
(§11 — this reading is ours, marked as interpretation.)

**எழுத்து + எல்லாம் → எழுத்தெல்லாம் · குற்றியலுகரம்.** The உ at the end of எழுத்**து** is a
*short-u* (குற்றியலுகரம்) — a vowel that is already half-swallowed, and drops entirely when the next
word begins with a vowel. Tolkāppiyam gives this one behaviour an entire chapter
(எழுத்ததிகாரம் 1.9, **குற்றியலுகரப் புணரியல்**). So the very first word-join in the first couplet of
the most-copied Tamil book demonstrates a rule the grammar thought worth a chapter of its own.
*You already do this in speech.* Say "வீடு இருக்கு" fast — you say வீட்டிருக்கு. Same rule, still
running, two thousand years later.

**முதல் → முதற்று.** The suffix **-து** makes "the thing that has X": முதல் + து → முதற்று,
"that which has a first." Same machinery as உடையது, "that which is owned."

**No verb.** There is no *is* anywhere in this couplet. Tamil states identity by putting two things
next to each other and letting the **-ஏ** in முதற்**றே** do the asserting. See `../../paadam/03-vinai.md`.

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

**பா:** குறள் வெண்பா — the shortest legal வெண்பா: **4 சீர் + 3 சீர்**, seven metrical feet total.

| அடி | சீர் 1 | சீர் 2 | சீர் 3 | சீர் 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | அகர | முதல | எழுத்தெல்லாம் | ஆதி |
| 2 | பகவன் | முதற்றே | உலகு | — |

**எதுகை** (the Tamil rhyme, on the **second** letter): அ**க**ர / ப**க**வன். The opening letters
differ (அ / ப) but match in quantity — both குறில், short — and the second letters are the same **க**.
This is the rhyme Tamil actually uses, and note where it lands: not at the end of the line, but one
syllable in. A Tamil rhyme is not a closing; it arrives as the word is *opening*.

> **Refined 2026-08-23.** This entry first called எதுகை "near-universal in the Kural." Measured, it
> is **56.8 %** between the two அடி — dominant, not near-universal. The miss-inspection explains
> why, and is the more interesting fact: in **குறள் 21** the எதுகை sits *inside* line 1 —
> ஒ**ழு**க்கத்து / வி**ழு**ப்பத்து — with line 2 not participating at all. **Valluvar anchors எதுகை
> on the opening சீர் but is free about where its partner sits.** So: the dominant organising tie,
> though not always between the two அடி. [`../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md`](../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md).

**மோனை** (alliteration, on the first letter), line 1: **அ**கர … **ஆ**தி — first and fourth சீர்.
அ and ஆ are the same vowel long and short, so this counts as **இனமோனை**, same-family alliteration.
That this is a *within-line* reading is now corroborated rather than assumed: across seven corpora,
மோனை between அடி sits **at chance** (0.9–1.0×), while within an அடி it runs 1.5–2.4×. Tamil
alliterates foot-to-foot inside a line — which is also what நூ. 88's `அடிதொறும்` must mean.

**ஈற்றுச்சீர்.** A வெண்பா must end on one of four patterns, named by exemplar words —
நாள் · மலர் · காசு · பிறப்பு — and **உலகு** is usually scanned as நிரை + உ, the **பிறப்பு** pattern.

> **Corrected 2026-08-23.** This entry gave that as near-fact with a "verify" note. Two things came
> back and both cut against it.
>
> **First: those four names are not Tolkāppiyam's.** Grep of the whole மூலம் (`pmuni0100`):
> **காசு — 0 hits. ஈற்றுச்சீர் — 0 hits.** Nor are the சீர் names every Tamil schoolchild learns —
> **தேமா, புளிமா, கூவிளம், கருவிளம்: 0 hits each.** Nor **வஞ்சித்தளை**; Tolkāppiyam names only
> கலித்தளை and வெண்தளை. **School Tamil prosody is a later pedagogical layer** — யாப்பருங்கலம் and
> after — laid over a more austere engineering document. The four ending-names are real and standard
> terminology; they are simply **not the founding grammar's**, and this entry implied they were.
>
> **Second: the scansion of உலகு is genuinely open.** It turns on செய்யுளியல் நூற்பா 4's exception
> clause, *குறில் இணை உகரம் அல் வழியான*, which is ambiguous and decides whether உலகு is one அசை
> (நிரைபு) or two (நிரை + நேர்). It is **not** settled here.
>

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**The metaphor is the alphabet.** Of everything Valluvar could have opened 1,330 couplets with —
sun, seed, king, mountain, the sea — he chose **letters**. The first gesture of the book is to model
the origin of the world on the origin of *writing*. That is a literate civilisation stating what it
thinks the world is like, and it is not a neutral choice.

**The god's name is broken across the line.** ஆதி ends line 1; பகவன் begins line 2. The reader must
cross the break to complete the name. The form performs the thing it is describing — a first that is
only completed by what follows it.

**முதல் twice is the entire argument.** எழுத்துக்களுக்கு முதல் அகரம்; உலகுக்கு முதல் ஆதிபகவன்.
Two clauses, one repeated word, and the analogy is done. There is no *like* and no *as* in the Tamil —
the comparison is made by parallel structure alone. Remove the repetition and the couplet collapses
into two unrelated assertions.

**No sect is named.** ஆதிபகவன் belongs to no particular god. Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Jain, and Christian
readers have all claimed the Kural, and this word is the main reason they can. It is the opening
move of a book that wants to be everyone's.

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

The Kural belongs to **பதினெண்கீழ்க்கணக்கு**, the "eighteen lesser texts" — a post-Sangam collection
of short-metre works. 1,330 couplets, 133 chapters of exactly ten, three books: **அறம்** (right
living), **பொருள்** (wealth and statecraft), **இன்பம்** (love). It is the most copied, most
translated, most quoted Tamil text — the one work that never came close to being lost in the
palm-leaf centuries, because too many people had it by heart.

Every chapter of ten opens a topic and closes it; this couplet opens the whole book and the chapter
**கடவுள் வாழ்த்து**, "praise of god" — a conventional opening that almost every Tamil work of the
period carries. Valluvar keeps the convention and empties it of sect.

The alphabet he is pointing at is a *generated* system, not a memorised list:
12 உயிர் × 18 மெய் = 216 உயிர்மெய், plus the 18 மெய் and the 12 உயிர் alone, plus ஃ = **247**.
See `../../paadam/01-ezhuthu.md`. A child is not learning 247 shapes; a child is learning a rule.

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 4+ for the activity, 8+ for the couplet itself.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"Every letter in Tamil has a secret sound hiding inside it. Want to see me take it out?"*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Say க, ச, ட, த, ப. What sound is in all of them? *(அ)*
2. If அ is inside every letter, is it really the *first* letter — or is it something else?
3. The poem says the world has a first, the way letters have a first. What do you think is first
   in *your* day? What has to happen before anything else can?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Write **க** big on paper. Say it: *ka*. Now put a dot on top — **க்**. Say it again: *k*. The dot
took the அ out. Do it with ச, ட, த, ப. Then take the dots off and hear அ come back into all of them.

> That dot is the **புள்ளி**, and it is the whole logic of the Tamil script in one mark: a letter
> with its உயிர் (soul) and a letter with only its மெய் (body). `../../paadam/01-ezhuthu.md`.

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The text as given in §3 is quoted exactly from Project Madurai `pmuni0001`.
  We have not yet collated it against a critical edition; the Kural's text is unusually stable, but
  the collation is unfinished work.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** ஆத்திசூடி's 109 lines and the Kural's 1,330 couplets are counted from the
  source e-texts, not from memory.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** *Upgraded 2026-08-23 — this was our interpretation and is now the
  tradition's own.* §6's central reading — that அ is *inside* every consonant, making the analogy
  ontological rather than merely ordinal — is stated outright in **தொல்காப்பியம், எழுத்ததிகாரம்,
  நூன்மரபு நூற்பா 17**: *புள்ளி இல்லா எல்லா மெய்யும் / உரு உரு ஆகி **அகரமொடு உயிர்த்தலும்*** —
  every மெய் without the dot **comes alive** (உயிர்த்தல்) with அகரம். The grammar puts it in the
  vocabulary of *life*, not of pronunciation. See
  [`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md) §1.
  **And the commentators have now been read.** Verdict: the repo's reading is **not contradicted,
  not stated, and half-anticipated** — see
  [`../../ilakkiyam/thirukkural-urai.md`](../../ilakkiyam/thirukkural-urai.md) §1.
  **மணக்குடவர்** says only that all letters "have அகரம் as their first" and gives no reason at all.
  **பரிமேலழகர்** does give a reason — but a different one: அ is first because of its *manner of
  production*, arising "by nature, which is mere நாதம்," not by modification. He reads the couplet
  as an **எடுத்துக்காட்டு உவமை** turning on **தலைமை**, primacy. So the entry's larger claim — that
  Valluvar's "first" means more than "earliest" — is exactly Parimēlaḻakar's instinct, reached on
  other ground; the **inherent-vowel mechanism is in neither commentary**, and its authority remains
  தொல்காப்பியம் நூன்மரபு 17. **Novel in mechanism, traditional in instinct.** This entry should not
  be cited as reporting a commentator's reading.
  *(They also differ on ஆதிபகவன்: மணக்குடவர் reads two words in apposition; பரிமேலழகர் reads one
  compound and names its Sanskrit-grammar type — the Sanskritic frame `../../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md`
  §5 flags, visible in the first couplet.)* `pmuni0450_01` (Parimēlaḻakar) and `pmuni0650` (Maṇakkuṭavar) are on
  Project Madurai and this should be checked before the reading is repeated as traditional.
  See `../../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §5 on exactly this bias.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** "No sect is named" is a standard observation, but the claim that
  it was a deliberate ecumenical *choice* by Valluvar is inference about intent, and intent is not
  recoverable.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** The date. ~450–500 CE is a common scholarly range; Tamil
  tradition places the Kural far earlier; the ta.wikipedia survey records the spread of positions.
  No date is settled and this entry does not settle one.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - Does the எதுகை on அ**க**ர / ப**க**வன் actually *land* for a native ear — is it heard as rhyme,
    or is it structural and felt only on analysis?
  - Is அகர / ஆதி heard as இனமோனை, or is that a scansion artefact?
  - **உலகு** as the **பிறப்பு** ஈற்றுச்சீர் pattern — flagged here on 2026-08-22 as *"the single
    most likely error in the entry."* **It was.** Not because the scansion is wrong (it may well be
    right) but because the entry attributed later terminology to Tolkāppiyam and called a genuinely
    open question merely unverified. See the correction in §7. The scansion itself is now an
    **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** turning on நூற்பா 4's exception clause, and still wants
    your ear and a யாப்பு authority.
