# 002 · ஆத்திசூடி · உயிர் வருக்கம் | The Twelve Vowels, One Rule Each

> **நூல்** ஆத்திசூடி · **எண்** 1–13 (உயிர் வருக்கம்) · **புலவர்** ஔவையார் — *which* Auvaiyar is a
> real question (§11) · **காலம்** uncertain; considerably later than the Sangam Auvaiyar (§11)
> **வகை** நீதிநெறி நூல் — a didactic alphabet-poem · **கூற்று** the poet, to a child
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0002](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0002.html)

For something like eight centuries this was the first Tamil text a child met. Not a grammar, not an
alphabet chart — **a poem, one line per letter.** The child learns the letter and a rule for living
in the same breath. This is the tradition's own answer to "where do you start," and it is why
`marabu/` starts from poems.

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

Each line is two or three words and ends hard. Eight of the thirteen end in the same sound: **-ஏல்**.
A child hears that ending and knows, before knowing any word, that this one is a *don't*.

> a-ṟam ce-ya vi-rum-pu
> ā-ṟu-va-tu ci-ṉam
> i-yal-va-tu ka-ra-**vēl**
> ī-va-tu vi-lak-**kēl**
> u-ṭai-ya-tu vi-ḷam-**pēl**
> ū-kka-ma-tu kai-vi-**ṭēl**
> eṇ e-ḻut-tu i-ka-**ḻēl**
> ēṟ-pa-tu i-kaḻ-cci
> ai-yam iṭ-ṭu uṇ
> op-pu-ṟa-vu o-ḻu-ku
> ō-tu-va-tu o-ḻi-**yēl**
> au-vi-yam pē-**cēl**
> aḵ-kam cu-ruk-**kēl**

And each line **begins on its own letter** — அ, ஆ, இ, ஈ, உ, ஊ, எ, ஏ, ஐ, ஒ, ஓ, ஔ, ஃ — in order.
The alphabet is the spine. Say them down the column and you have recited the vowels.

## 2. படம் | The picture

Thirteen little rules, one for each of the twelve vowels, and one more for the odd letter ஃ.
Each one begins with the letter it teaches. Learn the rules and you have learned the alphabet
without ever being made to sit down and learn the alphabet.

## 3. மூலம் | The text

**கடவுள் வாழ்த்து**

> ஆத்தி சூடி அமர்ந்த தேவனை
> ஏத்தி ஏத்தித் தொழுவோம் யாமே.

## 4. சொல்லுக்குச் சொல் | Word by word

| வரி | சொல் | பொருள் | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | அறம் · செய · விரும்பு | நல்லறத்தைச் செய்ய விரும்பு | **Want** to do what is right. |
| 2 | ஆறுவது · சினம் | சினம் ஆறக்கூடியது | **Anger** is a thing that cools — so let it. |
| 3 | இயல்வது · கரவேல் | உன்னால் இயலுவதை மறைக்காதே | Do not **hide** what you are able to do (or give). |
| 4 | ஈவது · விலக்கேல் | பிறர் கொடுப்பதைத் தடுக்காதே | Do not **block** giving. |
| 5 | உடையது · விளம்பேல் | உன்னிடம் உள்ளதைப் பறைசாற்றாதே | Do not **advertise** what you own. |
| 6 | ஊக்கமது · கைவிடேல் | ஊக்கத்தைக் கைவிடாதே | Do not **let go** of your drive. |
| 7 | எண் · எழுத்து · இகழேல் | கணக்கையும் எழுத்தையும் இகழாதே | Do not **despise** number and letter. |
| 8 | ஏற்பது · இகழ்ச்சி | பிறரிடம் ஏற்பது இழிவு | To **receive** (to beg) is degradation. |
| 9 | ஐயம் · இட்டு · உண் | பிச்சை இட்ட பிறகு உண் | **Give** before you eat. (ஐயம் here = alms) |
| 10 | ஒப்புரவு · ஒழுகு | ஒப்புரவோடு நட | **Live** with benevolence — the public good. |
| 11 | ஓதுவது · ஒழியேல் | ஓதுவதை விடாதே | Do not **stop** reciting — do not stop studying. |
| 12 | ஔவியம் · பேசேல் | பொறாமையைப் பேசாதே | Do not **speak** envy. |
| 13 | அஃகம் · சுருக்கேல் | தானியத்தைக் குறைக்காதே | Do not **shrink** the grain. (§11 — two readings) |

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
நல்லது செய்ய ஆசைப்படு. கோபத்தை ஆறவிடு. உன்னால் முடிவதை மறைக்காதே. கொடுப்பதைத் தடுக்காதே.
உன்னிடம் உள்ளதைப் பெருமையாகச் சொல்லாதே. முயற்சியை விடாதே. கணக்கையும் எழுத்தையும் இகழாதே.
பிறரிடம் கையேந்துவது இழிவு. கொடுத்துவிட்டு உண். எல்லாரோடும் நல்லபடி நட. படிப்பதை நிறுத்தாதே.
பொறாமை பேசாதே. அளக்கும்போது குறைக்காதே.

**In English:**
Want to do right. Let anger cool. Don't hide what you can do. Don't stand in the way of giving.
Don't boast of what you have. Don't drop your effort. Don't look down on arithmetic or on letters.
Living off others is degrading. Feed someone before you eat. Live for the common good. Never stop
studying. Don't speak out of envy. Don't cheat the measure.

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**-ஏல் · the classical negative imperative.** Eight of these thirteen lines end in it.
`கரவு` (hide) → **கரவேல்** · `விலக்கு` (block) → **விலக்கேல்** · `பேசு` (speak) → **பேசேல்**.

This is the ஏவல் எதிர்மறை — "do not X" — and it has almost entirely left spoken Tamil. You already
say it a different way:

| ஆத்திசூடி | இன்றைய பேச்சு | English |
|---|---|---|
| பேசேல் | பேசாதே / பேச வேண்டாம் | don't speak |
| இகழேல் | இகழாதே | don't despise |
| ஒழியேல் | நிறுத்தாதே / விடாதே | don't stop |

Nothing was lost in meaning — but notice what was lost in *length*. **பேசேல்** is three syllables.
**பேச வேண்டாம்** is five. The classical negative was a suffix; the modern one is a separate word.
Tamil traded compression for transparency. That is a real, measurable change and it belongs in
`../../aaivu/saaram.md`.

**-வது · turning a verb into a thing.** Six of the thirteen do it: ஆறு**வது**, இயல்**வது**,
ஈ**வது**, உடைய**து**, ஏற்**பது**, ஓது**வது**. "That which cools," "that which is possible,"
"that which is given." Tamil does not need an abstract noun where it can nominalise the verb itself —
one of the language's most productive habits, and it is doing half the work in this little poem.

**ஃ cannot start a word — so line 13 hides it inside one.** ஆய்தம் (ஃ) occurs only in one
environment in Tamil: after a short vowel and before a hard consonant — அஃது, எஃகு, **அஃகம்**.
This is not our observation; it is law, and the law is quotable — தொல்காப்பியம், எழுத்ததிகாரம்,
மொழிமரபு நூற்பா 5: *குறியதன் முன்னர் ஆய்தப் புள்ளி / உயிரொடு புணர்ந்த வல் ஆறன் மிசைத்தே.*
Nor is ஃ alone in being barred from the front of a word — நூற்பா 26–32 bar nine consonants as well,
and **entry 004** shows the whole உயிர்மெய் section of this same poem bending around them.
It can never begin a word. So the alphabet-mnemonic that needs a line for ஃ *cannot* follow its own
rule of starting on the letter. Auvaiyar's solution is to pick a word with ஃ inside it and let the
pattern bend. **The poem obeys the phonotactics rather than the mnemonic.** That is a grammar lesson
smuggled into a formatting compromise, and it is the sharpest thing in the thirteen lines.

**Two of thirteen are not commands.** Lines 2 and 8 — ஆறுவது சினம், ஏற்பது இகழ்ச்சி — are flat
statements, no imperative anywhere. Anger, and living off others: those two she does not order,
she simply names.

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

These are not பா in the classical sense — they are **குறள் வெண்செந்துறை**-adjacent didactic lines,
closer to sutra than to verse, and the tradition does not scan them the way it scans a வெண்பா.
What organises them is not meter but **the syllabary**: the alphabet supplies the order, and the
line supplies the content. The form is an index.

The one sonic device doing real work is the repeated **-ஏல்** ending — an end-rhyme
(**இயைபு**), which is the *least* used of the three Tamil rhymes in classical verse and here becomes
the whole texture, because it is a mnemonic device for a child rather than an ornament for a court.

> **Computed, not heard.** Whether the -ஏல் drumbeat actually reads as pleasure or as scolding to a
> Tamil ear is exactly the kind of judgment the inorganic side cannot make — `../../murai.md` §8.

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**The constraint generated the content.** She needed a line for every letter, in order. The letter
comes first and the moral has to fit it. So the choice of virtue at each rung is partly forced by
the alphabet — which means the poem is not a systematic ethics, it is an ethics *shaped by the
script*. Read line 12: **ஔவியம்**, envy. Why envy, there? Because ஔ is one of the rarest letters in
Tamil, native words beginning with it can be counted on one hand, and ஔவியம் was available.
The alphabet chose the vice.

**Two or three words is a design decision.** A four-year-old's working memory holds these. The whole
109-line book is roughly 250 words. It is a compression problem solved for a specific reader, and
the same instinct that produces the குறள் — `../../ilakkiyam/thirukkural.md` §1 — is at work here at
child scale.

**எண் எழுத்து இகழேல்** — line 7 pairs **number** and **letter** as the two literacies, refuses to
rank them, and forbids contempt for either. (This project is named **எண்ணம்**, and எண் is its root.
Noticed, not claimed — §11.)

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

**ஆத்தி சூடி** means "he who wears the āthi garland" — ஆத்தி is the *Bauhinia* flower, and the one
who wears it is Śiva. The book is named after the first two words of its own invocation, which is
the ordinary Tamil way of naming a text.

**Which Auvaiyar?** "ஔவையார்" is not one poet. It is a name — roughly "the venerable woman" — borne
by at least three or four different poets across more than a thousand years. The **Sangam** Auvaiyar
wrote in புறநானூறு and was the friend of the chieftain அதியமான் நெடுமான் அஞ்சி. The Auvaiyar of
**ஆத்திசூடி, கொன்றைவேந்தன், மூதுரை, நல்வழி** is a much later figure. Conflating them is the single
most common error in popular Tamil literary history, and this repo should not repeat it (§11).

**Why this book survived.** It was memorised, not read. Palm-leaf manuscripts rot in a century in
that climate and must be recopied; what survives is what enough people carried in their heads to
force the recopying. A 250-word book that every child knew was structurally immortal in a way a long
manuscript was not. See `../../varalaru/thonmai-pathivu.md`.

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 4–8. One line a day; thirteen days covers every vowel in Tamil.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"There's a rule for every letter. Today's letter is அ, and its rule is three words long."*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Which letter does today's line start with? Can you find that letter somewhere else in the room?
2. Line 7 says don't look down on **numbers** or on **letters**. Why would anyone look down on either?
3. Line 13 starts with ஃ — but ஃ is *inside* the word, not at the front. Why couldn't she put it at
   the front? *(Because no Tamil word starts with ஃ. Let them try to think of one.)*

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Make thirteen cards. On the front, the letter, big. On the back, its line. Put them in order on the
floor and walk down the row saying them. When all thirteen are up, you have said every vowel in
Tamil out loud — and you never once sat down to "learn the alphabet."

Then keep going: the next section (உயிர்மெய் வருக்கம், lines 14–31) starts on க and includes
**ஙப் போல் வளை** — "bend like ங" — which is entry 004.

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** All thirteen lines and the invocation are quoted exactly from Project Madurai
  `pmuni0002`. The 109-line total is counted from that e-text.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** *Closed 2026-08-23.* The ஃ distribution claim in §6 was asserted without a
  source when this entry was written. It is now cited to தொல்காப்பியம் மொழிமரபு நூற்பா 5, quoted
  from `pmuni0100`. See [entry 004](004-aathichudi-uyirmei-nga.md), which found it.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The e-text prints the poet's name as **ஒளவையார்** — an older typographic
  convention for ஔ, not a different name.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** **The date, and which Auvaiyar.** That the ஆத்திசூடி Auvaiyar
  is not the Sangam Auvaiyar is widely held; the actual date is not settled, and this entry
  deliberately gives none. The "eight centuries as a first text" claim in the header is a common
  assertion we have **not** yet sourced to a datable manuscript or school record. Treat it as
  unverified until it is.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** **Line 13, அஃகம் சுருக்கேல்.** Two readings are current:
  (a) do not short-measure grain when trading, (b) do not hoard grain. We have not checked which the
  commentaries support. `pmuni0777` and the ஆறுமுக நாவலர் உரை on Project Madurai are the places to look.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §8's claim that *the alphabet chose the vice* at line 12 is our
  reading. The rarity of word-initial ஔ is a fact; that it *caused* the choice of ஔவியம் is inference.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §6's compression comparison (பேசேல் 3 syllables vs பேச வேண்டாம் 5)
  is a real count on two examples, not a measured claim about the language. If it is going to be used
  as evidence of a historical shift, it needs a sample — that is a `../../aaivu/sothanai-*` job, not
  an assertion here.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** எண்ணம் / எண் in §8 is noticed, not claimed. It is a coincidence of
  naming that pleases us; it argues nothing.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - Does the repeated **-ஏல்** land as memorable rhythm or as monotony?
  - Are the English glosses in §4 and §5 in the right *register* — these are meant to sound like a
    grandmother, not a statute. Correct any that read as legal.
  - Line 2, ஆறுவது சினம்: is "let anger cool" the natural sense to a native ear, or does the
    predicative reading ("anger is that which cools") carry a different weight?
