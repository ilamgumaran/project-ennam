# 003 · குறுந்தொகை 2 — கொங்குதேர் வாழ்க்கை | Ask the Bee for the Truth

> **நூல்** குறுந்தொகை (எட்டுத்தொகை) · **எண்** 2 · **புலவர்** இறையனார் · **காலம்** சங்கம், ~300 BCE–300 CE
> **திணை** குறிஞ்சி — mountain, the landscape of union · **கூற்று** தலைவன் கூற்று — he speaks
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0110](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0110.html)

The second poem in the collection, and it contains the most useful sentence in Tamil for anyone who
wants to think honestly: **காமம் செப்பாது கண்டது மொழிமோ** — *do not speak what is desired; speak
what you saw.*

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

> koṅku tēr vāḻkkai añ-ci-ṟait tum-pi
> kā-mam cep-pā-tu kaṇ-ṭa-tu mo-ḻi-**mō**
> pa-yi-li-ya-tu ke-ḻī-i-ya naṭ-piṉ ma-yi-li-yal
> ce-ṟi-ye-yiṟ ṟa-ri-vai kūn-ta-liṉ
> na-ṟi-ya-vum u-ḷa-**vō** nī a-ṟi-yum pū-**vē**

Three long open vowels close three of the five lines — **மொழிமோ**, **உளவோ**, **பூவே**. The poem is
a question, and it keeps opening its mouth at the end of the line rather than closing it.

Line 3 has a vowel held longer than long: **கெழீஇய**. That second இ is not a spelling error. Tamil
can write a vowel *stretched past its own length* — **அளபெடை** — and the script has a way to notate
it. Almost no writing system bothers.

## 2. படம் | The picture

A bee with pretty wings goes from flower to flower all day long; that is its whole life. A boy stops
it and says: *don't tell me what I want to hear — tell me what you actually saw. Out of all the
flowers you know, is any of them sweeter-smelling than her hair?*

## 3. மூலம் | The text

> கொங்குதேர் வாழ்க்கை அஞ்சிறைத் தும்பி
> காமம் செப்பாது கண்டது மொழிமோ
> பயிலியது கெழீஇய நட்பின் மயிலியல்
> செறியெயிற் றரிவை கூந்தலின்
> நறியவும் உளவோ நீயறியும் பூவே.
>
> — இறையனார்

**Source:** Project Madurai e-text `pmuni0110`, குறுந்தொகை 2. Quoted exactly.

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
அழகிய சிறகுகளை உடைய வண்டே! மகரந்தம் தேடுவதே உன் வாழ்க்கை. நான் விரும்புவதைச் சொல்லாதே — நீ
கண்டதைச் சொல். நெடுநாள் பழகி நெருங்கிய நட்பு கொண்டவள், மயில் போன்ற இயல்புடையவள், நெருங்கிய பற்களை
உடையவள் — அவளுடைய கூந்தலை விட மணமுள்ள பூ, நீ அறிந்த பூக்களில் ஏதேனும் உண்டா?

**In English:**
Bee of the lovely wings — your whole life is the seeking of pollen. Do not tell me what I would like
to hear; tell me what you have actually seen. Of all the flowers you know, is there one more fragrant
than the hair of this woman — peacock-natured, close-toothed, whose closeness to me was grown by long
practice?

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**Why classical Tamil looks unreadable — and the one fact that fixes it.**
Look at line 4: **செறியெயிற் றரிவை**. There is no such word as *றரிவை*. The actual words are
செறியெயிற்று + அரிவை, joined as **செறியெயிற்றரிவை**. The printed break falls in the middle of the
doubled **ற்று** — because the page is broken into **சீர்** (metrical feet), not into words.

That is the whole secret. Classical Tamil is not hard because the vocabulary is strange. It is hard
because *the spaces are in the wrong places for a modern reader* — they mark the meter, not the
lexicon. Learn to re-join across the gaps and half the difficulty disappears. (This entry's §4 does
the re-joining for you; do it yourself on the next poem.)

**செப்பாது · the negative verbal participle -ஆது.** செப்பு (say) + ஆது = "without saying."
You already have this in speech as **-ஆமல்**: செப்பாது ≈ **சொல்லாமல்**. Same job, different suffix.

**கூந்தலின் · the comparative in -இன்.** கூந்தலின் நறிய = "more fragrant *than* the hair."
Classical Tamil marks comparison with a **case ending on the noun**. You say it with a
postposition: **கூந்தலை விட**.

> **A pattern across entries.** Entry 002 showed பேசேல் → பேச வேண்டாம். This one shows கூந்தலின் →
> கூந்தலை விட. Both are the same move: a **bound suffix** in classical Tamil replaced by a
> **free word** in modern Tamil. Two instances is not a finding. It is a hypothesis worth a sample —
> logged for `../../aaivu/` (§11).

**அளபெடை · கெழீஇய.** The ஈ is written stretched — ஈ + இ — to fill out the meter. Tamil notates
duration beyond phonemic length. Compare புறநானூறு 192's தலைஇ and படூஉம் in
`../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §4, where the stretching makes the river-lines physically drag.

**மோ · the particle that softens a command.** மொழி is a bare imperative — *speak!* மொழி**மோ** is
the same command addressed to a listener with a particle (முன்னிலை அசைச்சொல்) that takes the edge
off. He is not ordering the bee. He is asking it, formally, as one would ask a witness.

**அம் + noun — and why the ம் becomes ஞ்.** அம் = beautiful, prefixed: அம் + சிறை → **அஞ்சிறை**
(lovely-winged). This entry originally stopped at the split and never said why the letter changed.
The rule is named in the grammar — தொகைமரபு 27: *அம்மின் இறுதி **க ச த**க் காலை / தன் மெய் **திரிந்து
ங ஞ ந** ஆகும்* — the final of அம், when க/ச/த follows, changes to ங/ஞ/ந. Here **ச** follows, so
**ம் → ஞ்**.

And the substitution is not arbitrary. ங்/க, ஞ்/ச, ந்/த, ம்/ப are the nasal–stop pairs of
நூன்மரபு 19–20: **the nasal shifts to the place of articulation of the stop that follows it.** Same
pattern in அங்கை (அம்+கை, க → ங்) and அந்தண் (அம்+தண், த → ந்). One rule, three outcomes, chosen by
what comes next. Full system:
[`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-punarchi.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-punarchi.md).

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

**பா:** ஆசிரியப்பா (அகவற்பா) — the Sangam meter, the one nearly all akam poetry is in.
**அடி:** five lines of **4 · 4 · 4 · 3 · 4** சீர். Line 4 is a **குறளடி**, a short line; ஆசிரியப்பா
allows its lines to breathe unevenly, which is exactly what a வெண்பா does not allow.

**மோனை**, line 2: **கா**மம் · செப்பாது · **க**ண்டது · மொழிமோ.
கா and க are the same consonant, long vowel and short — **இனமோனை**, first and third சீர்.

Look at what that does. The two words the poem sets *against* each other — **காமம்** (what is
wanted) and **கண்டது** (what was seen) — are bound together by alliteration. The ear hears them as a
matched pair while the sense drives them apart. The sound welds what the meaning splits.

**எதுகை?** Present, but not governing — and the *reason* is not the one this entry first gave.

> **Corrected 2026-08-23.** This section originally claimed எதுகை was "a **post-Sangam**
> systematisation." **That was wrong.** தொல்காப்பியம் **names** எதுகை (செய்யுளியல் நூ. 84),
> **defines** it (89), licenses kindred letters for it (90) and sub-classifies it by interval
> (94–95). It was systematised early, not late. Full arc:
> [`../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md`](../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md).

What survives, better supported than when it was guessed: **எதுகை does not govern ஆசிரியப்பா.**
Tolkāppiyam never assigns a தொடை to any பா — while it assigns தளை to particular பா compulsorily and
repeatedly. **தளை is constitutive; தொடை is ornamental.** And நூ. 96 names **செந்தொடை**, the legitimate
category for a line that differs from every tie listed: *a system that names the absence of rhyme is
one in which rhyme is not required.*

Measured across eight corpora against a shuffled-partner baseline, எதுகை between consecutive அடி
runs at **14–30 % in ஆசிரியப்பா** (2.6–4.8× chance — real, but one junction in four to seven) against
**48–57 % in the வெண்பா group** (7.8–9.7×). The direction holds; the word "post-Sangam" does not.

**மோனை**, line 2: **கா**மம் · செப்பாது · **க**ண்டது · மொழிமோ — சீர் 1 and 3, one foot apart, which
is Tolkāppiyam's own named interval **பொழிப்பு** (நூ. 94). And the within-line reading is not just
ours: across அடி, மோனை sits **at chance** in all seven corpora measured (0.9–1.0×); *within* an அடி
it runs 1.5–2.4×. **Tamil poets alliterated foot-to-foot inside a line, not line-to-line.**

Look at what that does. The two words the poem sets *against* each other — **காமம்** (what is
wanted) and **கண்டது** (what was seen) — are bound together by alliteration at a பொழிப்பு interval.
The ear hears them as a matched pair while the sense drives them apart. The sound welds what the
meaning splits.

> **Computed, not heard.** Every claim in this section is scansion performed blind. Ilam's ear rules —
> `../../murai.md` §8. The மோனை-welding claim above is the one most worth testing aloud.

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**He builds an epistemology in one line.** Watch the moves in order:

1. **Choose a competent witness.** Not a friend, not a rival — a bee, whose entire occupation
   (கொங்கு தேர் வாழ்க்கை) is the assessment of fragrance. Domain expertise, established in four words.
2. **Name the bias and disqualify it.** காமம் செப்பாது — *do not speak the desire.* He knows the
   witness has an incentive to please him, and he removes it out loud before asking.
3. **Ask a question of fact, not of feeling.** He does not ask *is she lovely* or *does she love me*.
   He asks a fragrance question — a thing a bee can actually answer.

Three sentences of method, in five lines of love poetry, two thousand years ago.

**உள்ளுறை — what the poem does not say.** The bee that moves from flower to flower and knows them
all is also the speaker: a man with experience, appraising. The poem never states this. The tradition
reads it, and the reading changes the tone of the whole thing from praise into something more
double-edged. This is அகம் poetry's central machine — the scene carries the meaning, and the reader
completes it. See `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1.

**The unnamed lovers.** No names anywhere. அகம் convention forbids them: the speakers are
தலைவன், தலைவி, தோழி — *anyone*. Interior life is written as universal, so this is not one man and
one woman; it is every appraisal that ever wanted to be honest and knew it might not be.

**-ஏ at the close.** பூவ**ே**. The Sangam ending particle — closure you can hear, the same signature
that ends குறுந்தொகை 40 (தாங்கலந்தன**வே**).

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

**குறுந்தொகை** — "the short collection" — is one of the எட்டுத்தொகை, the eight Sangam anthologies.
Short akam poems of four to eight lines; the received text runs to **401** (traditionally 400 — the
extra is generally treated as a later addition), with an invocation by பாரதம் பாடிய பெருந்தேவனார்.
The corpus as a whole: 2,381 poems, 473 named poets. See `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1.

**இறையனார்** means *the Lord*. Tamil tradition holds that Śiva of Madurai composed this poem himself
and left it for a poor poet to submit to the Sangam. It is a legend, not a record — but note what the
legend chooses to attribute to a god: not a hymn, not a battle, but **a request for honest
testimony.** (§11 — legend, clearly labelled.)

**அரிவை** is not a generic word for "woman." Classical Tamil has a sequence of seven age-names for
women — பேதை, பெதும்பை, மங்கை, மடந்தை, **அரிவை**, தெரிவை, பேரிளம்பெண் — each covering a band of
years. The poet picked a precise one. English has no equivalent and the translation above quietly
loses it. That loss belongs in `../../aaivu/ellai.md` — running the other way for once: a place where
**Tamil says more than English can.**

**குறிஞ்சி**, the mountain landscape, means union — this is a poem of a love that is going well.
The five-landscape code is in `../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md` §1 and `../../paadam/README.md` (lesson 06).

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 7+ for the layer below. The அகம் layer — that this is a love poem, and that the bee
is also the man — waits until much later. Nothing here needs it.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"A boy stops a bee and says: don't tell me what I want to hear. Tell me what you actually saw."*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Why a **bee**? Of everyone he could have asked, why is a bee the right one to ask about smells?
2. He says *don't tell me what I want to hear.* Why would the bee do that? Has anyone ever told
   **you** what you wanted to hear instead of what was true?
3. He asks "is any flower sweeter than her hair?" — not "is she nice?" Which question is easier to
   answer honestly? Why?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Go and ask someone in the family a real question — and start it the way he did:
*"Don't tell me what I want to hear. Tell me what you actually saw."*
Then come back and say what happened. Was the answer different from what you expected?

> Older readers: this is `../../murai.md` §4 — **honesty over helpfulness** — in a poem older than the
> covenant by two thousand years. Noticed, not claimed (§11).

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Text, poet, திணை and கூற்று are all as printed in Project Madurai `pmuni0110`.
  The 401-poem count is from that e-text.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** The seven age-names (பேதை … பேரிளம்பெண்) are attested in the tradition; the
  **year ranges** commonly quoted for them vary between sources and are not given here for that reason.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §8's three-step reading of the poem as method — competent witness,
  bias disqualified, answerable question — is our reading. The text does not present itself as
  epistemology. It is a good reading; it is not the poem's own claim.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The உள்ளுறை reading (bee = the man) is traditional but is a
  *commentary* position, not the bare text. We have not traced which commentator it comes from.
- **[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** **Bound suffix → free word.** Classical -ஏல் → modern வேண்டாம்;
  classical -இன் comparative → modern -ஐ விட. Two instances. If real as a directional drift it needs
  a proper sample across a corpus with a declared failure condition. For `../../aaivu/` as a
  `sothanai-*`, not to be repeated as fact until then.
- **[முடிவு | RESOLVED 2026-08-23]** The எதுகை hypothesis was checked against செய்யுளியல் and
  **came back half wrong.** "Post-Sangam" is refuted — எதுகை is in Tolkāppiyam. "Not governing in
  ஆசிரியப்பா" is supported, by நூ. 96 (செந்தொடை) and by measurement. §7 is rewritten and the arc is
  preserved in [`../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md`](../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md).
  The entry was wrong in public and the wrongness is kept in view rather than tidied away.
- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** `pmuni0110` prints this poem's அடி 4 and 5 on **one physical line**. The poem
  is five அடி; the merge is an e-text formatting artefact and this entry's layout is correct.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** இறையனார் as Śiva is legend. Whether a historical poet of that
  name existed is unresolved and this entry takes no position.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** காமம் in line 2 — we gloss it as "what is desired / what would
  please me." Some readings take it as "love" more directly ("do not speak of love; speak what you
  saw"). The two give noticeably different poems. Commentary check needed.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - The **மோனை-welding** claim in §7 — do காமம் and கண்டது actually sound paired?
  - Does **கெழீஇய** audibly stretch, or is the அளபெடை only visible on the page?
  - Is the எளிய தமிழ் in §5 natural, or has it drifted into translationese?
