# 008 · திருக்குறள் 391–393 — கற்க கசடற | Learn Without Dross, Then Stand

> **நூல்** திருக்குறள் · **அதிகாரம்** 40, **கல்வி** (பொருட்பால்) · **எண்** 391, 392, 393
> **புலவர்** திருவள்ளுவர் · **பா** குறள் வெண்பா
> **மூலம்** [pmuni0001](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0001.html) ·
> **உரை** மணக்குடவர், [pmuni0650](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0650.html)

The chapter this repo answers to. And its second couplet turns out to be the top of a ladder that
[entry 002](002-aathichudi-uyir-varukkam.md) and [entry 005](005-konraiventhan-the-ladder.md)
started climbing — the same claim, three times, at three difficulties, always at the letter **எ**.

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

> kaṟ-ka ka-ca-ṭa-ṟak kaṟ-pa-vai kaṟ-ṟa-**piṉ**
> niṟ-ka a-taṟ-kut **ta-ka.**

Four **க**-sounds in the first line and the whole couplet is seven feet. Then it turns on one word —
**நிற்க** — which answers **கற்க** and rhymes with nothing else in it.

And 393, where the rhyme is the argument:

> **கண்**ணுடையர் என்பவர் கற்றோர் முகத்திரண்டு
> **புண்**ணுடையர் கல்லாதவர்.

**கண்** / **புண்**. Eye and sore. Both open on a short letter, both have **ண்** as their second —
textbook **எதுகை** — and the two words it welds are the two the couplet sets against each other.

## 2. படம் | The picture

Your two eyes let you see what is in front of you.

Numbers and letters let you see what is *not* in front of you — what happened before you were born,
what is happening far away, what someone thought a thousand years ago.

The poem says: those are your real eyes. And if you never learn them, the two things on your face
are not eyes at all. They are just sores.

## 3. மூலம் | The text

> **391** கற்க கசடறக் கற்பவை கற்றபின்
> நிற்க அதற்குத் தக.
>
> **392** எண்ணென்ப ஏனை எழுத்தென்ப இவ்விரண்டும்
> கண்ணென்ப வாழும் உயிர்க்கு.
>
> **393** கண்ணுடையர் என்பவர் கற்றோர் முகத்திரண்டு
> புண்ணுடையர் கல்லாதவர்.

**Source:** `pmuni0001`, lines 847–852, quoted exactly.

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
**391** — கற்கத் தக்கவற்றைக் குற்றமின்றிக் கற்க; கற்ற பின், அதற்குத் தகுந்தபடி நில்.
**392** — எண் என்பதும், மற்றது எழுத்து என்பதும் — இவ்விரண்டும் வாழும் உயிர்களுக்குக் கண் என்பர்.
**393** — கண் உடையவர் என்று சொல்லப்படுபவர் கற்றவரே; கல்லாதவர்க்கு முகத்தில் இருப்பவை இரண்டு புண்கள்.

**In English:**
**391** — Learn what is worth learning, flawlessly. Then **stand** in it, to its measure.
**392** — Number, they say; and the other, letter, they say — these two are the **eyes** of living beings.
**393** — Those said to have eyes are the learned. The unlearned have two **sores** on their face.

**மணக்குடவர்'s gloss on 391**, quoted from `pmuni0650` l. 2237–2238:
> *"கற்கப்படுவனவற்றைக் குற்றமறக் கற்க; கற்றபின்பு அக்கல்விக்குத் தக ஒழுக… இது கற்கவும் வேண்டும்;
> அதனைக் கடைப்பிடிக்கவும் வேண்டும் என்றது."*
> — *Learn what is to be learned, faultlessly; after learning, **conduct yourself** to the measure of
> that learning. This says: one must learn, and one must also **hold fast to it**.*

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**வியங்கோள் — the mood the whole couplet is in.** **கற்க** and **நிற்க** are not commands to *you*.
The -க ending is the **வியங்கோள்**, which has no clean English equivalent: *let it be that one
learns.* Impersonal, timeless, addressed to no one and to everyone. Modern Tamil keeps it in
வாழ்க, வெல்க, வளர்க. English must choose between "learn!" and "one should learn," and loses the
register either way.

**கசடு is a metalworker's word.** Not *fault*, not *gap* — **slag**, the impurity that floats off
molten metal. Learning has dross to be **smelted out**, not holes to be filled. A whole theory of
education inside one noun.

**கற்க → நிற்க.** Two verbs in the same mood, one per line, and the second is the pivot. Learning
that does not become **standing** — posture, conduct, how you hold yourself — is disqualified as
learning. `../../ilakkiyam/thirukkural.md` §2 says our பாடம் track answers to this verse; so does
every document in `../../ilakkanam/`.

**என்ப — the impersonal witness.** *They say.* Valluvar states 392 three times over as reported
speech rather than as his own claim. He is citing consensus, not asserting. The Kural does this often
and it is easy to translate away.

**எதுகை doing the argument.** In 393, **கண்** and **புண்** are bound by எதுகை on **ண்** (both first
letters குறில், second letters identical). The sound pairs *eye* and *sore* while the sense opposes
them — the same device entry 003 found welding **காமம்** and **கண்டது** in குறுந்தொகை 2. And it sits
between the two அடி, which the measurement in
[`../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md`](../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md) found is
where வெண்பா puts it 57 % of the time, against 14–30 % in akaval.

## 7. நுட்பம் | The craft — and the ladder closing

**392 is the top of a ladder this repo has been climbing for three entries.** The same claim, at
three difficulties, and always at the letter **எ**:

| rung | text | the line | words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [ஆத்திசூடி 7](002-aathichudi-uyir-varukkam.md) | **எண் எழுத்து இகழேல்** | 3 — *don't despise them* |
| 2 | [கொன்றைவேந்தன் 7](005-konraiventhan-the-ladder.md) | **எண்ணும் எழுத்தும் கண் எனத் தகும்** | 5 — *they deserve to be called eyes* |
| 3 | **திருக்குறள் 392** | **எண்ணென்ப ஏனை எழுத்தென்ப இவ்விரண்டும் / கண்ணென்ப வாழும் உயிர்க்கு** | 7 feet — *they **are** the eyes of the living* |

A prohibition, then a comparison, then a definition. Entry 005 measured the ladder's rungs by word
count (2.4 → 3.9 → வெண்பா) and found the tradition had built a graded curriculum. **This is the same
ladder carrying the same idea up it** — and Auvaiyar's line at rung 2 is recognisably an echo of the
Kural. The child's book is not simpler material. It is the Kural's claim, cut to a size a child can
hold.

**And 393 is where Valluvar stops being gentle.** 392 is generous — number and letter are eyes.
393 withdraws the metaphor's comfort: then the *unlearned* do not have eyes, they have **புண்**,
open sores, in the sockets. It is one of the harshest lines in the Kural and it is aimed at anyone
who declines to learn. This repo is named **எண்ணம்**, from **எண்**, and it should not quote 392
without 393.

## 8. உலகம் | The world behind it

**கல்வி** is chapter 40 of the Kural, and it opens **பொருட்பால்** — the book of *wealth and
statecraft*, not the book of virtue. Valluvar files learning under **the means of living in the
world**, alongside kingship, ministers, fortresses and wealth. That placement is an argument.

**எண் and எழுத்து as the pair.** Arithmetic and literacy, ranked equal and never separated, in a
society where each had its own specialists. `../../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` carries how Tamil
knowledge was organised; this couplet is the tradition refusing to choose between the two literacies
a thousand years before anyone worried about it.

## 9. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 7+ for 392. 9+ for 393, which is harsh and should be met as harsh.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"How many eyes do you have? The poem says four — and that two of them are ones you have to make."*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Your eyes show you what is here. Name one thing you know that your eyes **never** showed you.
   How did you get it? *(A number, a letter, a story someone wrote down.)*
2. **கசடு** means the scum that floats off melting metal. Why did he say learning has *dross* rather
   than saying it has *gaps*? What do you do with dross?
3. 391 says learn, then **stand**. What is the difference between knowing something and standing in
   it? Can you know a thing and not stand in it?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Write the numbers 1–10 in Tamil — ௧ ௨ ௩ ௪ ௫ ௬ ௭ ௮ ௯ ௰ — on one side of a card. On the other,
write the vowels அ ஆ இ ஈ உ ஊ எ ஏ ஐ ஒ ஓ ஔ.

**எண் on one face, எழுத்து on the other.** That card is குறள் 392. Keep it.

## 10. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** All three couplets quoted exactly from `pmuni0001` ll. 847–852; the
  மணக்குடவர் gloss from `pmuni0650` ll. 2237–2238.
- **[எல்லை | LIMIT]** **பரிமேலழகர்'s உரை on this chapter was not found** — `pmuni0450_01` covers only
  the earlier chapters, and the later part-files (`_02`–`_04`) are in `../../_src/` and unsearched
  for it. So §5 gives one commentator, not two, unlike [entry 001](001-kural-001-agara-mudhala.md).
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §7's ladder — that ஆத்திசூடி 7 → கொன்றைவேந்தன் 7 → குறள் 392 is
  one claim at three difficulties — is our reading. **What is source:** the three lines exist, all
  three sit at the letter எ in their alphabet-ordered texts, and all three pair எண் with எழுத்து.
  **What is inference:** that Auvaiyar is echoing the Kural. She is later, and the resemblance is
  strong, but direct dependence is not shown.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** "கசடு is a metalworker's word" — the sense *slag/dross* is standard,
  and the pedagogical reading built on it in §6 is ours.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Chapter 40 sits in **பொருட்பால்**, not அறத்துப்பால். We read
  that placement as an argument about what learning is *for*. But the two commentators differ on
  chapter order generally (`../../ilakkiyam/thirukkural-urai.md`), so the placement may not be
  Valluvar's own. Unchecked.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - **கண் / புண்** in 393 — does the எதுகை land hard enough to feel like a blow? The whole force of
    the couplet is in that pairing.
  - **கற்க / நிற்க** — the வியங்கோள். Does it read as commandment, as blessing, or as something
    English has no register for at all? Every translation in §5 had to pick one.
