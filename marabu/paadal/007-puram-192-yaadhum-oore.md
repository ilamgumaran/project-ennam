# 007 · புறநானூறு 192 — யாதும் ஊரே | Every Town Is Ours

> **நூல்** புறநானூறு (எட்டுத்தொகை) · **எண்** 192 · **புலவர்** **கணியன் பூங்குன்றன்** — கணியன் =
> astronomer · **காலம்** சங்கம், ~300 BCE–300 CE
> **திணை** பொதுவியல் · **துறை** **பொருண்மொழிக் காஞ்சி** — and that matters (§9)
> **தலைப்பு** as the edition prints it: **பெரியோர் சிறியோர்!** — *not* "யாதும் ஊரே" (§8)
> **மூலம்** [Project Madurai pmuni0057](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0057.html)

The two most quoted words in Tamil open this poem. Almost nobody quotes the other eleven lines, and
the poem is not what the two words alone suggest. A close reading exists at
[`../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md`](../../ilakkiyam/sangam.md) §4; this entry re-cuts it for teaching.

---

## 1. ஓசை | The sound

*Say it before you understand it.*

> yā-tum ū-**rē** ; yā-va-rum kē-**ḷir** ;
> tī-tum naṉ-ṟum pi-ṟar-tar vā-**rā** ;
> nō-ta-lum ta-ṇi-ta-lum a-vaṟ-ṟō ran-**ṉa** ;

Line 1 has a hook you can hear before you know a word of it: **யா**தும் · ஊரே · **யா**வரும் · கேளிர்.
The first and third feet both open on **யா**. That is **மோனை**, and the gap of one சீர் between them
is the interval Tolkāppiyam names **பொழிப்பு** (செய்யுளியல் 94). The same shape as குறுந்தொகை 2's
காமம் / கண்டது in [entry 003](003-kurunthogai-002-kongu-ther.md) §7 — and, as measured there,
Tamil's alliteration lives *inside* the line, never across lines.

Then in the middle the poem slows down on purpose: **தலைஇ**, **படூஉம்**. Vowels written stretched
past their own length — **அளபெடை**. The river lines physically drag.

## 2. படம் | The picture

A raft on a big river. It is raining hard, there is lightning, the water is roaring and knocking
against rocks. The raft does not steer. It goes where the river goes.

That is what a life is, the poem says. So: every town is our town, and everyone is our family.

## 3. மூலம் | The text

> யாதும் ஊரே ; யாவரும் கேளிர் ;
> தீதும் நன்றும் பிறர்தர வாரா ;
> நோதலும் தணிதலும் அவற்றோ ரன்ன ;
> சாதலும் புதுவது அன்றே ; வாழ்தல்
> இனிதுஎன மகிழ்ந்தன்றும் இலமே; முனிவின்,
> இன்னா தென்றலும் இலமே; 'மின்னொடு
> வானம் தண்துளி தலைஇ, ஆனாது
> கல்பொருது இரங்கும் மல்லற் பேர்யாற்று
> நீர்வழிப் படூஉம் புணைபோல, ஆருயிர்
> முறைவழிப் படூஉம்' என்பது திறவோர்
> காட்சியின் தெளிந்தனம் ஆகலின், மாட்சியின்
> **பெரியோரை வியத்தலும் இலமே;**
> **சிறியோரை இகழ்தல் அதனினும் இலமே.**

**Source:** `pmuni0057`, poem 192, quoted exactly. Thirteen அடி, **ஆசிரியப்பா**.

## 5. பொருள் | The meaning

**எளிய தமிழில்:**
எல்லா ஊரும் நம் ஊரே; எல்லாரும் நம் உறவினரே. தீமையும் நன்மையும் பிறர் தருவதால் வருவதில்லை.
துன்பமும் அது தணிவதும் அப்படியே. சாவதும் புதிதல்ல. வாழ்வது இனிது என்று மகிழ்வதும் இல்லை;
வெறுத்து இது கொடியது என்பதும் இல்லை. மின்னலோடு வானம் குளிர்த்துளியைப் பொழிய, ஓயாமல் கல்லில்
மோதி முழங்கும் பெரிய ஆற்றின் நீர்வழியே செல்லும் தெப்பம் போல, அரிய உயிரும் தன் முறைவழியே செல்லும் —
இதைத் தெளிந்தவர்களின் காட்சியால் நாங்கள் தெளிந்தோம். ஆகவே, பெரியவர்களைக் கண்டு வியப்பதும் இல்லை;
**சிறியவர்களை இகழ்வது அதைவிடவும் இல்லை.**

**In English:**
Every town is our town; everyone is our kin. Harm and good do not come to us from others' giving;
pain and its easing are of the same kind. Death is nothing new. We do not rejoice that living is
sweet, nor in revulsion call it bitter. As the sky pours cold rain through lightning, and a raft
rides the current of a great river that roars and beats against the rock — so this precious life
goes the way it is ordered. Because we have seen this clearly, through the sight of the
clear-seeing: **we are not awed by the great — and still less do we scorn the small.**

## 6. உள்ளே இருக்கும் இலக்கணம் | The grammar inside

**-உம் … -உம், and how Tamil says *every*.** யாது**ம்** = *whatever + உம்* → everything. யாவரு**ம்** =
whoever + உம் → everyone. Tamil builds universals by suffixing **-உம்** to a question word. You do
this constantly: எங்கும், எப்போதும், யாரும். The poem's first four words are two question-words made
universal, and that *is* the claim.

**இலமே — the first person plural that includes you.** இல் (not-be) + **அம்** (we) + **ஏ**
(emphatic). Not *I am not*; **we are not**. The poem never says *I*. It is a communal verdict spoken
in the plural, four times over (இலமே ×3, தெளிந்தனம்).

**அளபெடை.** தலை**இ**, படூ**உ**ம் — vowels written held past their length, to fill the metre and slow
the line. Tamil notates duration beyond phonemic length; see
[`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-ezhuthathikaram.md) §1,
where நூன்மரபு 6 licenses exactly this — *நீட்டம் வேண்டின் … கூட்டி எழூஉதல்*, if you need length,
combine letters.

**அதனினும் — the comparative, doing moral work.** அதனின் (than that, the **இன்** case of
[`../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md`](../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md) §1)
+ உம். The last line does not merely add a second refusal. It **ranks** it. See §8.

## 7. யாப்பு | The music

**பா:** ஆசிரியப்பா (அகவல்) — the Sangam metre, 13 அடி.
**மோனை:** line 1, **யா**தும · ஊரே · **யா**வரும் · கேளிர் — சீர் 1 and 3, the **பொழிப்பு** interval.
**எதுகை:** not systematically, which is normal for akaval — measured at 14–30 % across the akaval
corpora against 48–57 % in the வெண்பா group ([`../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md`](../../aaivu/karuthu/002-ethugai-thodai.md)).

> **Computed, not heard.** The அளபெடை claim in §1 — that தலைஇ and படூஉம் make the river lines
> physically drag — is the one most worth testing aloud. `../../murai.md` §8.

## 8. நுட்பம் | The craft

**The famous line is the premise, not the conclusion.** Everyone quotes யாதும் ஊரே. The poem spends
eleven lines *earning* it, from a hard premise — **தீதும் நன்றும் பிறர்தர வாரா**, good and harm do not
come from other people. If nothing anyone does to you originates with them, then no one is a
stranger and no one is an enemy. The kinship is **derived**, not asserted. It is a proof, not a
slogan.

**The edition titles it by its ending.** This e-text heads the poem **பெரியோர் சிறியோர்!** — *Great
Ones, Small Ones.* Not by its famous opening. Whoever set that title read the poem as being about
its **last two lines**, and they were right.

**And the last two lines are ranked.** Read the order:

> பெரியோரை **வியத்தலும்** இலமே;
> சிறியோரை **இகழ்தல் அதனினும்** இலமே.

*We are not awed by the great; and to scorn the small, we are that **even less**.* Refusing to
flatter power comes first and is the easier half. Refusing to despise the powerless is marked
**greater** — அதனினும். A poem that opens on universal kinship closes by saying which failure of it
is worse. Two thousand years on, that ranking is still the uncomfortable one.

**A poem that sounds like its own argument.** The river lines are the longest and slowest in the
poem, weighted with அளபெடை, and they describe a current that cannot be resisted. Equanimity
described in a rhythm that enacts it.

## 9. உலகம் | The world behind it

**கணியன் பூங்குன்றன்** — **கணியன்** is an astronomer-astrologer, one who reckons. The raft that
follows the river's course, and life that follows **முறைவழி** — its ordained way — is a metaphor
chosen by a man whose profession was calculating the courses of things.

**And the classification cuts against the slogan.** The colophon reads **திணை: பொதுவியல்,
துறை: பொருண்மொழிக் காஞ்சி**. **காஞ்சி** is the புறத்திணை of **impermanence** — the mode of
transience and the mutability of the world. So the tradition did not file this as a poem about
brotherhood. It filed it as a **meditation on transience**, whose famous opening follows from the
fact that nothing lasts. `../../ilakkanam/tholkappiyam-thinai.md` has the seven புறத்திணை.

**One poem, two national afterlives.** These words are inscribed at the United Nations and are
recited in Tamil schools as a statement of universal humanism. That reading is not wrong, but it
takes the first line and leaves the argument. The full poem is colder and harder than the slogan,
and better.

## 10. குழந்தைக்கு | For a child

**வயது | Age:** 7+ for lines 1–2 and the raft. 11+ for the argument.

**தூண்டில் | The hook:**
*"There are two words every Tamil person knows. Want to learn them — and then find out that almost
nobody knows the rest of the poem?"*

**மூன்று கேள்வி | Three questions**
1. Say **யாதும் ஊரே; யாவரும் கேளிர்.** Listen — which sound comes twice? *(யா — twice, in the first
   and third words.)*
2. The raft doesn't steer. Is that a sad thing to say about a life, or a calm one? Which do you
   think he meant?
3. The last two lines say: *we're not impressed by big important people — and we look down on small
   people **even less**.* Why do you think he put them in that order? Which one is harder?

**ஒரு செயல் | One thing to do with your hands**
Find moving water — a stream, a drain after rain, even a tap into a bucket. Put something light on
it: a leaf, a twig, a paper boat. Watch it go. Don't push it. **That is the புணை**, the raft, and
that's the picture at the centre of the most famous poem in Tamil.

Then learn just the first line. It is four words, and it will be with you for life.

## 11. ஐயம் | Doubt

- **[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Text, poet, திணை, துறை and the edition's title all as printed in `pmuni0057`.
  The poet's name appears here as **கணியன் பூங்குன்றன்**; **கணியன் பூங்குன்றனார்** is the more common
  form elsewhere. Not a different poet, and we have not collated editions.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** §8's reading — that the kinship is *derived* from the premise in
  line 2 rather than asserted — is ours. The poem juxtaposes; it does not supply a *because*.
  It is a good reading and it is not the text's own connective.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** That the editor's title **பெரியோர் சிறியோர்!** reflects a
  *reading* of where the poem's weight lies is inference about an editor's intent. What is source is
  only that this edition so titles it.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The gloss of **முறைவழி** as "ordained way" leans fatalistic.
  **முறை** is order, sequence, propriety — "the way of order" or "its proper course" are equally
  available, and they give a different poem. The Tamil is not committed; the English has to be.
  This is a real translation choice and it should be reopened with the ear.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** No commentary consulted. ஔவை துரைசாமிப் பிள்ளை's commentary on
  புறநானூறு exists and is not in `../../_src/`; his reading of முறைவழி would likely settle the
  question above.
- **காதுக்காக | Awaiting the ear:**
  - Do **தலைஇ** and **படூஉம்** actually slow the line, or is the அளபெடை only visible on the page?
  - Is the **யா / யா** of line 1 heard as chime, or is it invisible in ordinary recitation?
  - Does **இலமே**, repeated three times, land as calm or as flatness? The whole tone of the poem
    turns on it, and the English "we are not" cannot carry the ஏ.
