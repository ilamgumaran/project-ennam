## 1. வேற்றுமை | The eight relationships

> **வேற்றுமைதாமே ஏழ் என மொழிப.** (1)
> **விளி கொள்வதன்கண் விளியொடு எட்டே.** (2)
> அவைதாம்,
> **பெயர் ஐ ஒடு கு**
> **இன் அது கண் விளி** என்னும் ஈற்ற. (3)

Seven cases — and with the vocative counted, eight.

**Look at how they are named.** Not *nominative, accusative, dative*. Tolkāppiyam names each case by
**the suffix itself**: the ஐ case, the ஒடு case, the கு case. A Tamil child learning வேற்றுமை is
learning eight *sounds they can hear on the end of a word*, not eight Latin abstractions they must
map onto forms. The name is the form.

| # | வேற்றுமை | ஈறு | நூற்பா | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **எழுவாய்** | *(none)* | 4 | பெயர் தோன்று நிலை — the naming form; the bare noun |
| 2 | **ஐ** | -ஐ | 10 | the thing a **verb** falls on — வினையே வினைக்குறிப்பு |
| 3 | **ஒடு** | -ஒடு / -ஓடு | 12 | **வினைமுதல் கருவி** — doer and instrument, together |
| 4 | **கு** | -கு / -க்கு | 14 | எப் பொருள் ஆயினும் கொள்ளும் — the recipient, the purpose |
| 5 | **இன்** | -இன் | 16 | **இதனின் இற்று இது** — *than this, this is so*: source and comparison |
| 6 | **அது** | -அது / -ன் | 18 | **இதனது இது** — belonging, kinship, ownership |
| 7 | **கண்** | -கண் / -இல் | 20 | **இடத்தின் நிலத்தின் காலத்தின்** — place, ground, **and time** |
| 8 | **விளி** | — | ch. 4 | calling; given a whole இயல் of its own (விளிமரபு) |

### Three of these repay a close look

**The third case fuses doer and instrument.** நூற்பா 12: *ஒடு எனப் பெயரிய வேற்றுமைக் கிளவி /
**வினைமுதல் கருவி** அனை முதற்று அதுவே.* One case for *by whom* and *by what*. European grammar
splits agent from instrument; Tamil files them together, and also uses -ஒடு for accompaniment
(*with*). Doer, tool, companion — one relationship, seen three ways.

**The seventh is spatial and temporal at once.** நூற்பா 20: *வினை செய் **இடத்தின் நிலத்தின்
காலத்தின்*** — the place of the act, the ground, **and the time**. Then the text lists the words
that carry it: *கண் கால் புறம் அகம் உள் உழை கீழ் மேல்* — eye, foot, outside, inside, within, near,
below, above. The locative vocabulary is built out of **body parts and body-relative positions**.
*கண்* is both "eye" and "at". Tamil locates things the way a standing body does.

**The fifth case is comparison — and this closes an open item.**
[`../marabu/paadal/003`](../marabu/paadal/003-kurunthogai-002-kongu-ther.md) §6 read **கூந்தலின்**
in குறுந்தொகை 2 as "more fragrant *than* her hair," calling it "the comparative in -இன்" without a
source. நூற்பா 16 states it: the இன் case means **இதனின் இற்று இது** — *compared to this, this is
so.* And the text goes on to list the dimensions along which the comparison runs:

> வண்ணம் வடிவே அளவே சுவையே
> தண்மை வெம்மை அச்சம் என்றா …

Colour, shape, measure, **taste**, coolness, heat, fear. A closed catalogue of comparable
qualities — the same shape of device as வி-020's seven age-names: a fixed series doing precise work
at no syntactic cost.

### What a learner should take

You already use all eight. What the classical forms give you is the **bare** ending where modern
speech has grown a word around it:

| வேற்றுமை | classical | இன்றைய பேச்சு |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (ஒடு) | அவனொடு | அவனோட / அவனுடன் |
| 5 (இன்) | கூந்தலின் நறிய | கூந்தலை **விட** நறுமணம் |
| 7 (கண்) | ஊர்க்கண் | ஊர்**ல** / ஊரில் |

That is the **bound-suffix → free-word** drift logged as a hypothesis in entries 002, 003 and 005.
Case 5 is now its clearest instance: a case *ending* replaced by a *postposition* (விட). Still
unsampled, still a hypothesis — but the ground under it is firmer.

## 2. நான்கு வகைச் சொல் | The four kinds of word — and what it costs the purists

> **இயற்சொல் திரிசொல் திசைச்சொல் வடசொல்** என்று
> அனைத்தே செய்யுள் ஈட்டச் சொல்லே. (எச்சவியல் 1)

*These four, and no others, are the words that gather into poetry.*

- **இயற்சொல்** — the plain word, meaning what it ordinarily means
- **திரிசொல்** — the turned word: a word used in a sense other than its common one, or a rare
  poetic synonym
- **திசைச்சொல்** — the **regional** word, from the country beyond the centre
- **வடசொல்** — the **northern** word: Sanskrit

Read the last two again. The founding grammar of Tamil, listing what may legitimately be used in
composition, names **regional speech** and **Sanskrit loanwords** as two of its four categories. Not
as corruption to be tolerated. As **kinds of word**.

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** This bears directly on two rows of the Bias Ledger in
`../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §9 — *Terminology* (செந்தமிழ் "refined" vs கொடுந்தமிழ் "crooked")
and *Purism* (movement-era de-Sanskritisation). The twentieth-century purist project of expelling
வடசொல் from Tamil is not a return to the tradition; **the tradition made room for them in நூற்பா 1
of its chapter on poetic vocabulary.** That is a fact about the text, and stating it is not an
argument for or against any modern position — the repo takes none. But a bias ledger that records
purism as a selection pressure should record that the founding grammar is not its ancestor.

**[திறந்த கேள்வி | OPEN QUESTION]** Tolkāppiyam says more about *how* வடசொல் may enter — there are
நூற்பா on which sounds must be adapted before a northern word is admissible. Those are not read
here. Whether the text is *permissive* or *permissive-under-conditions* is exactly the question, and
it should be settled before this section is cited in an argument.

## 3. ஐயம் | Doubt

- All நூற்பா quoted exactly from `pmuni0100`, numbered as printed. **No commentary consulted** —
  சேனாவரையர் is the great commentator on சொல்லதிகாரம் and has not been read. Sutras without உரை are
  provisional; `../varalaru/arivu-varalaru.md` §5 cuts both ways.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The English glosses in the case table are ours. "The thing a verb
  falls on" for ஐ is a paraphrase of *வினையே வினைக்குறிப்பு / அவ் இரு முதலின் தோன்றும்*, not a
  translation of it.
- **[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION]** The reading that the locative vocabulary is body-derived
  (கண், கால், புறம், அகம்) is our observation. That these words are *also* body-words is plain; that
  the locative sense *derives* from the body sense is an etymological claim we have not tested.
- **[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** Bound suffix → free word, now with a third instance (இன் → -ஐ விட).
  Three examples is not a sample. Pre-register invariants and a failure condition before this is
  called a finding.
- **Not read:** கிளவியாக்கம், வேற்றுமை மயங்கியல், விளிமரபு, பெயரியல், வினையியல், இடையியல்,
  உரியியல், and the rest of எச்சவியல் — seven of the nine இயல். **உரியியல் in particular** is the
  chapter on உரிச்சொல், a word-class with no clean English equivalent, and it is the most likely
  place in this book to hold something for phase 2. Next session.
- **காதுக்காக:** in your own speech, which of the eight endings do you actually still *say* as a
  suffix, and which have become separate words? That inventory is data we cannot get without you,
  and it would turn the drift hypothesis into something testable.
