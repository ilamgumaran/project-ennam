# தொல்காப்பியம் · உரியியல் | The Words That Do Not Come Plain

> **மூலம்** [pmuni0100](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0100.html), local copy
> `../_src/txt/pmuni0100-tholkappiyam-moolam.txt`, சொல்லதிகாரம் 2.8, from line 1861. Read 2026-09-06.
> Companion: [`tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md`](tholkappiyam-sollathikaram.md).

Tamil grammar recognises four kinds of word: **பெயர்** (noun), **வினை** (verb), **இடை** (particle),
and **உரி**. The first three map onto categories any grammar has. The fourth does not, and English
has no name for it. This chapter is what it is.

---

## 1. The definition — a class defined by *not being obvious*

> **வெளிப்படு சொல்லே கிளத்தல் வேண்டா**
> **வெளிப்பட வாரா உரிச்சொல் மேன.** (2)

*A word whose meaning comes out plainly needs no explaining. உரிச்சொல் are those that do **not**
come out plainly.*

Read that again. **Tolkāppiyam has a formal word-class whose defining property is that the word does
not explain itself.** Not a semantic field, not a part of speech in the usual sense — a class
constituted by *opacity to the reader*. And having named the class, the chapter then spends itself
glossing its members, one short நூற்பா at a time. It is a grammar carrying a dictionary of its own
difficulty.

The fuller definition, நூற்பா 1:

> உரிச்சொல் கிளவி விரிக்கும் காலை
> **இசையினும் குறிப்பினும் பண்பினும்** தோன்றி
> பெயரினும் வினையினும் **மெய் தடுமாறி**
> **ஒரு சொல் பல பொருட்கு** உரிமை தோன்றினும்
> **பல சொல் ஒரு பொருட்கு** உரிமை தோன்றினும்
> பயிலாதவற்றைப் பயின்றவை சார்த்தி
> தம்தம் மரபின் சென்று நிலை மருங்கின்
> எச் சொல் ஆயினும் **பொருள் வேறு கிளத்தல்**. (1)

Four properties, each worth its own line:

| in the text | what it says |
|---|---|
| இசையினும் குறிப்பினும் பண்பினும் | they arrive by **sound**, by **implication**, or by **quality** — not by direct denotation |
| பெயரினும் வினையினும் **மெய் தடுமாறி** | their **body wavers** between noun and verb — they will not sit still in a class |
| ஒரு சொல் பல பொருட்கு | **one word may own many meanings** |
| பல சொல் ஒரு பொருட்கு | **many words may own one meaning** |
| பொருள் வேறு கிளத்தல் | whatever the word, its work is to **state a meaning apart** |

**மெய் தடுமாறி** is the phrase to keep. *The body staggers.* The grammar's own metaphor for a word
that will not hold a grammatical shape.

## 2. The glosses — a dictionary in sutra form

Each நூற்பா is a definition. A sample, quoted exactly:

> உறு தவ நனி என வரூஉம் மூன்றும் / **மிகுதி** செய்யும் பொருள என்ப. (3)
> உரு **உட்கு** ஆகும் புரை **உயர்பு** ஆகும். (4)
> குருவும் கெழுவும் **நிறன்** ஆகும்மே. (5)
> சல்லல் இன்னல் **இன்னாமை**யே. (6)
> மல்லல் **வளன்**ே ஏ பெற்று ஆகும். (7)
> உகப்பே **உயர்தல்** உவப்பே **உவகை**. (8)
> பயப்பே **பயன்** ஆம். (9)
> **பசப்பு நிறன்** ஆகும். (10)
> இயைபே **புணர்ச்சி**. (11)
> இசைப்பு **இசை** ஆகும். (12)
> அலமரல் தெருமரல் ஆயிரண்டும் **சுழற்சி**. (13)
> மழவும் குழவும் **இளமை**ப் பொருள. (14)
> சீர்த்தி **மிகு புகழ்** மாலை இயல்பே. (15)
> கூர்ப்பும் கழிவும் உள்ளது **சிறக்கும்**. (16)

| உரிச்சொல் | gloss | what it is doing |
|---|---|---|
| உறு · தவ · நனி | மிகுதி — excess | three intensifiers, distinct in feel, identical in function |
| உரு | உட்கு — dread, awe | *form* meaning *the fear a form causes* |
| புரை | உயர்பு — height, nobility | physical height standing for rank |
| குரு · கெழு · **பசப்பு** | நிறன் — colour | three words for colour, and the third is not a colour |
| அலமரல் · தெருமரல் | சுழற்சி — whirling | the motion of distress, twice |
| மழ · குழ | இளமை — youth | |
| கூர்ப்பு · கழிவு | excess of what is there | |

## 3. பசப்பு — one word that shows what the class is for

**பசப்பு** is glossed simply: *நிறன் ஆகும்*, "it is a colour." It is not a colour. It is the
**pallor that comes over a woman's body when she is separated from the man she loves** — the sallow
green-gold that Sangam poetry returns to again and again. There is no English word. "Pallor" carries
none of the cause; "lovesickness" carries none of the *visible* fact.

That is what a உரிச்சொல் is for, and why the class had to be named. **பசப்பு is a physical sign that
is also an emotional state that is also a poetic convention, in four letters.** The grammar cannot
define it by denotation, so it does what நூற்பா 1 said it would: it points — *it is a colour* — and
leaves the reader to supply the rest.

Note where this puts the chapter. [`tholkappiyam-meippattiyal.md`](tholkappiyam-meippattiyal.md)
catalogues the **bodily signs** of feeling; உரியியல் supplies the **words** for them. The grammar
holds both halves of the same problem — how an interior state becomes visible, and how it becomes
sayable.

## 4. என்ன கிடைத்தது | What this gives the project

**[மூலம் | SOURCE]** Tamil grammar contains a word-class **defined by not being self-evident**
(நூ. 2), whose members arrive by sound, implication or quality rather than denotation, whose
grammatical body "staggers" between noun and verb, and which permit many-to-one and one-to-many
mappings between word and meaning (நூ. 1).

**[விளக்கம் | INTERPRETATION] This is reader-completion as a lexical class.** வி-008 found that the
Kural and the திணை code achieve compression by requiring the reader to close the arc — a property of
*forms*. வி-021 found that மெய்ப்பாட்டியல் closes by requiring **உணர்வு** in the receiver — a
property of *persons*. உரியியல் is the same requirement at the level of the **word**: a வெளிப்படு
சொல் delivers its meaning; a உரிச்சொல் requires the reader to arrive at it. The tradition located
reader-dependence at all three levels and gave the lexical one a formal class.

**[கருதுகோள் | HYPOTHESIS]** For phase 2: a language being extended to carry realised thought needs
somewhere to put terms that are *precise but not decomposable* — the ones that cannot be built from
parts and must be learned by acquaintance. Tamil already has that slot, named, with entry conditions
and a gloss convention. **Coinages that fail as compounds might succeed as உரிச்சொல்.** That is a
design option this project did not know it had, and it is testable: propose a term, gloss it the way
நூ. 4–16 gloss theirs — *X is Y* — and see whether use converges.

**[எல்லை | LIMIT — and it runs the other way for once]** English has no உரிச்சொல் class and no
single-word equivalents for பசப்பு, அலமரல், or உட்கு. This belongs in `../aaivu/vithai.md` alongside
வி-020 (அரிவை) as a second, structurally different case of Tamil out-resolving English: வி-020 was a
**closed ordered series**; this is an **open class defined by opacity**.

## 5. ஐயம் | Doubt

- The chapter continues well past நூற்பா 16 and **only the opening is read here**. The full inventory
  of உரிச்சொல் and the closing நூற்பா are not covered.
- **No commentary.** சேனாவரையர் is the major commentator on சொல்லதிகாரம் and is not in `../_src/`.
  உரியியல் is a chapter of *glosses*, so commentary disagreement is likely to be dense here.
- **[விளக்கம்]** The rendering of **மெய் தடுமாறி** as "the body staggers" is ours and is a literal
  reading; commentators may take மெய் as *form* in a narrower grammatical sense.
- **[விளக்கம்]** The account of **பசப்பு** in §3 draws on Sangam usage, not on this chapter, which
  says only *நிறன் ஆகும்*. The four letters do not contain the convention; the corpus does.
- **[திறந்த கேள்வி]** Is உரிச்சொல் a class of *poetic* vocabulary specifically, or of any
  non-obvious word? நூ. 2's definition is general; every example given is poetic. The two readings
  have different consequences for the phase-2 proposal in §4 and this document does not settle it.
- **காதுக்காக:** do **உறு**, **தவ** and **நனி** feel different to you, or interchangeable? The
  grammar says they share one function (மிகுதி) and troubles to list all three — which usually means
  a distinction the text is not stating.
